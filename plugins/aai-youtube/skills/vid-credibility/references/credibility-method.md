# Credibility Method

Reference for `vid-credibility`. Read when drafting starts. Don't paste into chat.

## The rule: Big plus Specific plus Personal

Every credibility brag has all three:

- **Big.** The number or result is impressive.
- **Specific.** Real numbers, real timeframes, real client outcomes. Not "many years" or "lots of businesses."
- **Personal.** The creator DID the thing. Past tense. "I built" not "I've been studying how to build."

If a brag is missing one, it's not a brag yet. It's a draft.

## What makes a brag "viewer-relevant"

A brag answers: "Why should I trust THIS person on THIS topic?"

It does NOT answer: "Why should I respect this person?"

### Viewer-relevant

> "I made $4M from my YouTube channel in 2025."

If the avatar wants YouTube revenue, this lands. The number is specific. The outcome is what they want.

### Not viewer-relevant

> "I've been a digital marketer for 12 years."

The avatar doesn't care about years. They care about whether they'll get the result they want.

### Viewer-relevant

> "I helped a client go from 400 subscribers to 358,000 in just over a year."

Specific numbers, specific timeframe, outcome the viewer wants.

### Not viewer-relevant

> "I've worked with Fortune 500 companies."

Unless the avatar is a Fortune 500 marketer, this doesn't apply to their situation.

## Good vs bad: paired examples

### Pair 1: the "years" trap

**Good:** "I've built automation systems for 50+ businesses since 2022."

Why: Big number, specific date, declarative past tense, creator did the work.

**Bad:** "I've been doing this for 10+ years."

Why fails: years say nothing about what was produced. Someone could fail for 10 years.

### Pair 2: big result vs vague scale

**Good:** "My last client went from 80 hours a week to 15 after we built their operations system."

Why: dramatic concrete transformation, real before/after, creator built it.

**Bad:** "I've helped lots of businesses become more efficient."

Why fails: "Lots" means no number. "More efficient" means nothing measurable.

### Pair 3: the credential trap

**Good:** "I made $4M from my YouTube channel in 2025."

Why: number stops the scroll, year locked, direct ownership.

**Bad:** "I have an MBA from Stanford and have consulted with major Fortune 500 companies."

Why fails: generic credential, not result. The avatar doesn't recognize it as authority in THEIR world.

### Recognized authority is NOT the credential trap

The credential trap is GENERIC credentials: MBA, certifications, "X years of experience," vague Fortune 500 name-drops the avatar has no relationship with.

A recognized institutional role inside the avatar's own world is the opposite. It is authority and it anchors the set.

- "Executive producer at [a brand the avatar follows]" is authority. The avatar knows that brand. Keep it. It is often a near-permanent brag, not a candidate for cutting.
- "Head of growth at [a company the avatar admires in their niche]" is authority.
- "MBA from Stanford" to a YouTube-creator avatar is a credential trap. They don't care.

Test: would the avatar recognize this name or role and think "okay, this person operates at the level I want"? If yes, it is authority, keep it, anchor with it. If no, it is a credential trap, cut it.

### The recognized-name vs unknown-name rule

Names in brags only carry weight if the avatar recognizes them.

- **Recognized name** (a brand or person the avatar follows or admires): use the name. The name IS the proof.
- **Unknown name** (a client the avatar has never heard of): drop the name. Replace it with the avatar's own label, then lead with the surprisingly small inputs and the big result. The anonymity plus the contrast is the proof.

The anonymized descriptor must be the avatar's exact public label, pulled from the Avatar section of `foundation/avatar.md`. Never a generic "client," "creator," or "someone" unless that word is literally the avatar's label. If the avatar is "busy parents," the brag says "a busy parent." If the avatar is "first-time homebuyers," it says "a first-time buyer." The brag mirrors the buyer back to themselves.

Example (neutral domain, to calibrate the move only). A fitness coach whose avatar is busy parents. The client lost 40 pounds training 20 minutes a day.

- Weak, unknown name: "I coached Dave to lose 40 pounds." Why it fails: the avatar has never heard of Dave, so the name is dead weight.
- Weak, generic label: "I coached a client to lose 40 pounds." Why it fails: "a client" is faceless, the avatar does not see themselves in it.
- Strong: "I coached a busy parent to lose 40 pounds training 20 minutes a day." Why it works: the avatar's own label mirrors them back, and the small input ("20 minutes a day") destroys the belief that it takes hours they do not have.

### One proof point per brag (no cramming)

Each brag carries ONE clean number or claim. Do not stack two numbers into one sentence to make it feel bigger. It reads crammed and the creator rewords it on the read-aloud.

- Crammed: "I lost 40 pounds in under 6 months and without ever stepping in a gym." Why it fails: two proof points (speed, no-gym) fight for attention, neither lands clean.
- Clean: "I lost 40 pounds without ever stepping in a gym." Why it works: one claim, one bite.

If two numbers are both strong (a speed number AND a constraint), that is two separate brags or a rotation extra. Pick the single sharpest one for the locked set. Bank the other.

### Pair 4: anti-proof (the self-sabotage)

**Good:** "Since 2022, I've built the systems behind dozens of businesses. The number one mistake I see business owners make before they even talk to me is..."

Why: the creator is the expert. The mistake is OTHER people's. The creator knows what to do.

**Bad:** "Since 2022, I've built the systems behind dozens of businesses. The number one reason those systems fail is..."

Why fails: the creator IS the source of failure in this framing. Reads as "my systems fail." Even though the sentences look nearly identical, the frame destroys the proof.

**Check every brag for anti-proof framing before locking.** This is the single most common credibility mistake.

### Pair 5: hedged vs declarative

**Good:** "I've treated 3,000+ patients in my clinic."

Why: declarative, past tense, specific scale, no wiggle words.

**Bad:** "I've been working on developing treatment approaches for a variety of patients over the years."

Why fails: "Been working on" is a hedge, feels in-progress. "A variety of" is vague. "Over the years" is no real timeframe.

## How to surface and pick brags

Surface a pile through guided prompts asked one at a time, then rank and lock the strongest three for the avatar. The prompts surface material. They do not become the locked slots.

Collection prompts (ask one at a time, pull more than three):

- **Personal result.** "Biggest result you personally got that this avatar wants too?"
- **Client or customer result.** "Strongest result you helped someone else get? Before, after, how long."
- **Authority or operator proof.** "Biggest role, brand, platform, or person you've worked with that this avatar would recognize and respect?"
- **Volume proof.** "Biggest scale number, only when it produced something measurable."
- **Belief-breaker.** "The result that would shock this avatar most given what they think is impossible."

Then rank by buyer relevance and belief-kill, and lock three. The locked set does not have to take one from each prompt. Volume is not automatically slot three.

### Sample pulls by niche

**Business coach:**
- "I've built automation systems for 50+ businesses."
- "My last client's operations went from 80 hours a week to 15."
- "I automated my own job from 45 hours to 15 before AI was a thing."

**Physiotherapist:**
- "I went from 400 subscribers to 358,000 in a year."
- "I launched my first $15k week with a low-ticket product."
- "I've treated 3,000+ patients in my clinic."

**Founder coach:**
- "I made $4M from my YouTube channel in 2025."
- "I've helped 700+ business owners make millions on YouTube."
- "My best video did $260k in sales."

## Anti-proof in depth

Anti-proof is when the creator's brag accidentally positions THEM as the cause of failure. It happens when:

- The brag chains "I built X" with "X fails because..."
- The brag says "I've worked on" instead of "I built"
- The brag credits a system, method, or tool that didn't work

### Fix patterns

| Anti-proof | Reframed |
|---|---|
| "My systems fail because..." | "Business owners fail before working with me because..." |
| "I've been studying why X is hard." | "I've taught [N] people how to do X. The mistake they all make is..." |
| "I'm not perfect at this. I struggle with..." | "Even at my level, the mistake I had to unlearn was..." |

Vulnerability is fine in backstory. NOT in credibility brags.

## When the creator has no big numbers yet

Three fallback angles, weakest to strongest:

- **Education.** "I spent 2 years studying [specific thing]."
- **Consumption.** "I've read 300+ books on [topic]."
- **Personal transformation.** "I went from [bad state with numbers] to [good state with numbers] myself."

Personal transformation is the strongest because it's still a result. Just the creator's own.

Flag the MVP caveat. As soon as the creator produces a real client win, replace the fallback. Stronger proof always wins.

## Format

Each brag: one sentence. Specific numbers. Past tense (or present tense for ongoing state like patient counts). No hedging.

> Good: "Since 2022, I've built systems for over 50 businesses."
>
> Bad: "Since 2022, I've been working on building systems for various businesses."

## Use in intros

In the hook section. Usually between the Problem statement and the Promise.

Example intro flow:

- **Problem.** "The number one reason business owners can't scale is they've built their business around themselves."
- **Proof (credibility brag).** "Since 2022, I've built automation systems for over 50 businesses."
- **Promise.** "In this video, I'm going to show you the five-part system I use..."

Creators rotate between the three locked brags. Don't use the same one in every video. Pick the brag that best matches the video's topic.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic time brags ("I've been doing this for 10 years")
- Vague scale ("I've worked with many businesses")
- Credential listing ("I have an MBA from X")
- Social proof without specifics ("worked with some of the top brands")
- Brags that are impressive but irrelevant to the avatar
- Locking before the anti-proof check
