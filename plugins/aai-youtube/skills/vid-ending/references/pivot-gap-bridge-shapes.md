# Pivot / Gap / Bridge Shapes

Examples-first reference for the 3-Part End Formula. Every section pairs a worked example with a near-miss and a one-line "why this lands / why this doesn't." Sourced from working endings that drove 20%+ end-screen click-through, expanded into multiple niches.

This file is reference material for Claude to think with at draft time, not to paste at the creator.

## The shape

```
PIVOT  → one sentence recap of the transformation the body just delivered
GAP    → one sentence revealing the new problem the viewer now has (from avatar Top 3)
BRIDGE → one sentence pointing to a specific next video that solves the Gap
```

That's it. The whole close runs 30-60 seconds when read aloud. No recap of every lesson. No "and finally." No "thanks for watching." No begging for likes.

The Pivot exists so the viewer feels the value landed. The Gap exists so the viewer feels the next video is a need, not an option. The Bridge exists so the click feels obvious.

If any of the three is missing, the close is broken.

---

## 1. Pivot shapes (recap the transformation)

The Pivot is ONE sentence. Plain language. Echoes the promise the intro made.

### Worked: "you now have everything you need to..."

> "You now have everything you need to write a hook that stops scrolling."

Why this lands: names the specific outcome (hook that stops scrolling), uses second-person to put the receipt in the viewer's hands, no padding. The intro promised "I'll show you how to write a hook that stops scrolling", the Pivot closes the loop.

### Worked: "that's how you..."

> "That's how you turn a 60-hour workweek into 22 hours without dropping a single client."

Why this lands: declarative recap with the body's hardest receipt baked in (60 to 22 hours), no list of every step, one breath.

### Worked: "so now you know how to..."

> "So now you know how to land your first 3 clients without a website."

Why this lands: low-energy recap that still names the specific transformation. Works when the creator's voice profile skews calm/email-energy and a louder Pivot would feel off.

### Near-miss: list-recap

> "So we covered hooks, then we covered intros, then we covered transitions, then we covered endings."

Why this misses: this is a recap of the body, not a Pivot. Tells the viewer the value is over and they should leave. Pivot is ONE sentence with the transformation, not a tour of the chapter titles.

### Near-miss: vague recap

> "So now you know how to make better videos."

Why this misses: "better videos" is the empty-noun trap. The body taught a specific thing (a hook that stops scrolling, transitions that forward-hook, etc). Pivot has to name that specific thing, not the category.

### Near-miss: self-congratulation

> "I hope you enjoyed today's video on hooks."

Why this misses: tells the viewer the video is over. Burns the close. Doesn't recap a transformation, just announces a topic was covered.

### Pivot length budget

- 1 sentence ideal
- 2 sentences acceptable when the transformation has two parts (e.g., "now you know X AND Y")
- 3+ sentences = recap dump = REJECT

## 2. Gap shapes (reveal the new problem)

The Gap is ONE sentence. Names the problem your next video solves, the thing they need after this lesson (what they just learned isn't enough without it). It lands on one of the channel's recurring problems naturally, because that's what the catalog covers. The lesson opened a new gap, it didn't close all of them.

### Worked: "but here's the thing..."

> "But here's the thing. A hook that stops scrolling won't keep them past minute three if your first body shot doesn't match."

Why this lands: hard pivot ("but here's the thing"), named second-order problem (first body shot mismatch), specific stake (minute three). The viewer who watched the hook video now feels they need the body video.

### Worked: "the next problem is..."

> "The next problem is keeping clients past month two."

Why this lands: direct, specific, names a problem the avatar already knows ("retention" is on their stack). One beat.

### Worked: "here's the gap most people hit next..."

> "But here's the gap most people hit next. Three clients won't survive if you don't have a renewal system."

Why this lands: diagnostic framing (most people hit this gap), names the specific consequence ("won't survive"), points at the next lever (renewal system).

### Worked: "the reason most still don't get the result..."

> "The reason most coaches still grind is the delivery model. That's what I'm going to show you next."

Why this lands: implies the body's lesson alone isn't enough, names the specific blocker (delivery model), creates urgency without hyperbole.

### Near-miss: novel problem

> "The next problem is your tax planning."

Why this misses: tax planning is off-channel for a creator/coach. There's no video to bridge to, and it isn't a problem the catalog covers. The Gap has to point at something the channel actually addresses. An off-channel problem breaks the connection, the viewer's brain registers "different topic" and clicks away.

### Near-miss: vague gap

> "But there's still more to learn."

Why this misses: "more to learn" is the dead-noun trap. Doesn't name a specific problem. Reads as "I have more videos, please watch them", that's the begging the formula explicitly bans.

### Near-miss: contradiction with body

> "But the truth is, hooks don't actually matter."

Why this misses: contradicts the body's lesson (which just spent 12 minutes teaching hooks). Viewer feels gaslit. The Gap reveals the next problem, not a retraction of the current one.

### Gap length budget

- 1 sentence ideal
- 2 sentences acceptable when the Gap needs a stake clause ("won't survive if X")
- The Gap problem MUST be one the channel actually covers (you have, or could point to, a video for it). It's usually a different problem than the video taught, the next logical watch. If nothing in the catalog connects to the body, the Pivot may be off, regenerate it, or fall back to a subscribe pointer.

## 3. Bridge shapes (position the next video as the fix)

The Bridge is ONE sentence. Names the next video (or implies it via the on-screen end-screen card), uses confident "watch this next" or equivalent, never begs.

### Worked: "I made a video on it. Watch this next."

> "I made a video on it. Watch this next."

Why this lands: short, confident, no begging, no "if you have time" hedge. The end-screen card appears as the line is spoken. Done.

### Worked: "watch this next, where I'll show you..."

> "Watch this next, where I'll show you exactly how to write a hook that's gonna make people binge your videos and buy faster."

Why this lands: tells the viewer what they're going to get from the next video (specific outcome + specific result). The "show you" verb is correct, never "tell you" (audio-coded).

### Worked: "that's what I'm going to show you next."

> "That's what I'm going to show you next."

Why this lands: works when the Gap sentence already named the topic the next video covers. The Bridge then just confirms direction. Combined with the on-screen end-screen card, the click feels obvious.

### Worked: "the next thing on your stack is X. Watch this next."

> "The next thing on your stack is keeping retention through the middle. Watch this next."

Why this lands: stack-language reads as a checklist the viewer is on. Implies the videos are designed to be watched in sequence. Confidence is in the framing, not the volume.

### Near-miss: hedge bridge

> "If you have time, you might want to check out this next video."

Why this misses: "if you have time" telegraphs low confidence. Viewers respond to confidence, if the creator isn't confident the next video is worth watching, the viewer agrees.

### Near-miss: bare CTA

> "Subscribe and like."

Why this misses: no Pivot, no Gap, just a request for attention. The whole 3-Part Formula collapsed into a generic ask. This is the failure mode the formula exists to prevent.

### Near-miss: future-promise bridge

> "I'll be making a video on this soon. Stay tuned."

Why this misses: the viewer can't watch a video that doesn't exist yet. "Stay tuned" is broadcast-era filler. By the time the next video drops, this one's audience is gone. Never link to a video you haven't made.

### Bridge length budget

- 1 sentence ideal (when the Gap already named the topic)
- 2 sentences when the Bridge has to introduce a new framing ("Watch this next, where I'll show you exactly how to X")
- The Bridge target MUST be a real, already-published video. No exceptions.

## 4. Worked examples (full Pivot → Gap → Bridge)

### Step-by-Step, goal=sales

> "You now have everything you need to land your first 3 clients without a website. The next problem is keeping them past month two, and that's where most coaches get stuck. I made a video on it. Watch this next."

Pivot (1 sentence, names transformation) → Gap (1 sentence, names problem from Top 3) → Bridge (2 sentences, confident next-video). Reads aloud in ~22 seconds.

### Success Story, goal=sales

> "That's how Steve went from 0 clients to $80k a month in 9 weeks. The reason most coaches still grind even with a methodology like this is the delivery model, when client three signs, the bottleneck moves to delivery. That's what I'm going to show you next."

Pivot recaps the transformation in plain language, includes the receipts already in the body. Gap names the second-order problem (delivery bottleneck). Bridge points to a deep-dive on the methodology.

### Review, goal=sales

> "So now you know the three biggest mistakes that kill thumbnails and the fixes that make them work. Yours is probably making one of these too. I review thumbnails every couple of weeks, drop yours in the form linked below, and watch this next where I show you a fix that doubled one creator's CTR overnight."

Pivot recaps the universal lesson. Gap implicates the viewer's own thumbnail. Bridge does double duty: submission CTA (mandatory for review format per planner) AND next-video pointer.

### Deep Dive, goal=sales

> "That's the complete system. The reason most creators with a system like this still don't break $10k a month is the offer doesn't match the system. If you want to see exactly how to package the offer so it converts, watch this next."

Pivot is short (the body did the lift). Gap names the next-stage problem (offer mismatch). Bridge is direct because deep-dive viewers who finished are buyers, leaving them without a strong CTA leaves money on the table.

### News, goal=views

> "So that's what just changed. The thing to watch for over the next 30 days is how the bigger platforms respond. While we wait, here's a video I made that explains why this category was always going to break this way."

Pivot is one line. Gap is "what to watch for" (action position, not a problem). Bridge points to non-news evergreen (per news format planner, convert the algorithm spike into channel audience, never end-screen to more news).

### List Video, goal=emails

> "Those are the 7 brutal truths I wish I'd known. The next one's the one most people miss until year three: how to systemize what you've learned so it actually compounds. The full checklist is in the link below, and watch this next where I walk through the system step by step."

Pivot recaps the cumulative payoff. Gap names the systemization gap. Bridge does double duty (lead magnet pointer + next video).

### Interview, goal=views

> "And that's how Sarah did it. The reason most people watching this won't get the same result is execution, knowing what works isn't enough if you can't run the play. Watch this next, where I walk through the exact system I use to install plays like Sarah's into a business in 30 days."

Pivot recaps the GUEST's transformation. Gap names the execution problem. Bridge points to a video where the CREATOR is the expert (per interview format planner, host credibility doesn't transfer from guest, end-screen to creator's evergreen is the entire monetization mechanism).

## 5. Voice fit overlay

Every Pivot/Gap/Bridge passes through the creator's voice profile before lock. Apply the rhythm rules from `knowledge/voice-rhythm.md`:

- Match sentence-length distribution (don't run all-medium)
- Match opener pattern (declaration vs, question vs, anecdote)
- Match punctuation signature (no em-dashes, parens for asides)
- Match energy modulation (YouTube energy is dialed up; same close for newsletter would be dialed down)

If the creator's voice profile is heavy on personal-confession openers ("I used to think hooks were optional"), the Pivot can lean into that style: "I used to think the body did all the work. Turns out the close is what makes them stay." Same Pivot job, different voice fit.

Per `knowledge/voice-pressure-test.md`, run the read-aloud test on every locked close. If the creator would reword anything, drop back to draft.
