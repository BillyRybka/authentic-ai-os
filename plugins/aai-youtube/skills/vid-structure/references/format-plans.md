# Format Plans

Read only the section matching the locked format. Start from the repository format planner, then adapt when the creator deliberately wants a different shape.

Every section still carries the shared core: **Job**, **Sources**, and **Takeaway**. The fields below are conditional additions, not blank slots to fill.

## Short Process

Plan a bounded route to one result.

Decide:

- the finish state the viewer can reach in one sitting
- the ordered actions and any true dependencies between them
- one shared story, contrast, or demonstration only when the process needs belief or orientation
- an extra example or evidence item only on a step that is hard to understand or trust

Useful optional fields: **Action**, **Input**, **Output**, **Demonstration**, **Evidence**, **Dependency**.

Do not give every step a fresh story. Do not keep setup that delays the first useful action.

Positive: three clear actions, one opening contrast, and a screenshot attached to the one result claim.

Negative: eight steps padded from five, each carrying a separate metaphor and client story.

## Case Study

Plan one real transformation as a causal story, not five generic lessons.

Confirm the selected source contains enough to establish:

- starting state and stakes
- the mistaken belief, obstacle, or failed attempt when relevant
- the decisions and actions that caused the change
- the outcome and evidence for any material result claim
- the lesson and useful application for the viewer

Useful optional fields: **Story beat**, **Decision**, **Action**, **Outcome**, **Evidence**, **Application**.

The outcome may be disclosed in the title or opening. In that case the reason to continue is how it happened, why it worked, or whether the viewer can repeat it.

An unknown material outcome or causal action is a critical gap. Do not replace it with a vague success line.

## Roast

Plan from the real work or asset being reviewed.

For each subject or review unit, decide:

- the exact source asset and whether it is public or supplied with permission
- the visible problem or decision being reviewed
- the diagnosis the creator can support
- the concrete fix or improved version
- what the viewer can apply to their own work

Useful optional fields: **Asset**, **Diagnosis**, **Fix**, **Demonstration**, **Expected effect**, **Evidence**.

Label a predicted effect as predicted. Do not turn a proposed fix into a result that already happened. Group subjects when they teach the same diagnosis instead of repeating it.

## Deep Dive

Plan the shortest complete path through a connected system or argument.

Decide:

- why the depth is earned and what the viewer can understand or do afterward
- the major modules and their dependencies
- the proof or orientation needed early enough to justify the commitment
- which claims need evidence and where synthesis occurs
- which detail belongs in examples under a module rather than its own section

Useful optional fields: **Module role**, **Dependency**, **Framework**, **Example**, **Evidence**, **Synthesis responsibility**.

Do not force three to five lessons. Use the number the system requires, then remove anything that does not change understanding or action.

## Interview

Plan questions and editorial targets without pretending unrecorded answers exist.

Before recording, a question section may contain:

- **Known context:** verified material already supplied by the guest or creator
- **Target story:** the real episode the question is meant to elicit
- **Target insight:** what the audience needs the answer to clarify
- **Follow-up trigger:** a specific detail that should prompt the host to dig deeper
- **Package responsibility:** which viewer question this answer needs to serve

Use `to elicit in recording` as provenance for an unknown answer. It is a question target, not source material and not a writer-ready factual claim. A question can be fully planned before its answer exists because the host is writing the prompt, not the guest's answer.

At pre-recording handoff, `vid-segment` writes only the host's framing, question, and planned follow-ups for each section. It must leave the guest answer unscripted. This is filming-ready for an interview even though the later edited answer does not exist yet.

When a transcript is available before the body plan is written, replace targets with exact transcript spans or supplied clips and plan the edited body. When host sections are already completed, use re-structure safety and do not overwrite them silently. Cut weak answers rather than scripting words for the guest.

Do not require every question to produce a story. Some should clarify a decision, mechanism, objection, or useful process.

## News

Plan for speed, factual provenance, and a clear point of view.

At minimum establish:

- what happened, with source and date
- what is confirmed, disputed, or still unknown
- why it matters to this viewer now
- the creator's supported interpretation
- what the viewer should do, watch, or reconsider

Useful optional fields: **Claim source**, **Date**, **Confidence**, **Point of view**, **Action**.

An unsourced material fact is a critical gap. Keep the body as short as the story warrants. Do not manufacture a late reveal when the useful fact belongs first.

## Listicle

Plan distinct items that each create value on their own.

For every item, decide:

- why it is different from the other items
- the exact source material
- the useful lesson or action
- a story, example, demonstration, metaphor, framework, or evidence item only when it earns its space

Useful optional fields: **Example**, **Demonstration**, **Story**, **Metaphor**, **Framework**, **Evidence**.

Order by comprehension, momentum, stakes, or package fulfillment. The biggest item may be last, but it does not have to be. Merge items that repeat the same lesson and never pad the count promised by an old working title. If the locked title names a count the material cannot support, return to title or framing rather than fabricating items.
