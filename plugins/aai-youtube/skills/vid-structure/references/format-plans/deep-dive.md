# Deep Dive

Proof first, then one parable, then the steps with proof at each one. The only format where proof is its own block: nobody sits through an hour without seeing early that the time is worth it.

## The body

```
## Proof
## The parable: {old way, new way}
## Step 1: {step}
...
## Step N: {step}
```

- **The proof block** is thirty seconds of concentrated evidence right after the intro: client wins, before and afters, testimonials. It is a list, not a story.
- **The parable** is usually old way, new way: discredit the status quo, prove the new way, tease the result. A story or demo works when the material is better that way.
- **The steps** pass the planner's highest-leverage filter: of everything the system contains, the fewest steps that produce most of the result. Everything else is a cut with a reason. Twenty out of a hundred is the planner's own ratio.
- Each step can carry its own parable when it helps. Do not assume parables only go at the start, and do not give one to a step that does not need it.

## What each section locks

Every field is a label line with its beats under it. As many beats as the section needs for a writer to draft without asking.

The proof block:

- **Proof:** one beat per item, each a wikilink or exact source and what it shows. Three to five.

The parable section:

- **Parable:** the type on the label. The beats: what gets discredited, what proves the new way, the result teased.

Every step:

- **Principle:** the step, why it matters, the framework name where one exists, the detail that makes it doable.
- **Proof:** the client win or before and after that shows this step worked. The planner's ideal pattern is step, then proof the step worked, so most steps carry one. `to build` where the banks are empty.
- **Parable:** only where the step needs a story, metaphor, or demo to land, with its beats. Otherwise no field.

## Where the payoff lands

The synthesis step, usually the last one, where the pieces connect. Each step opens a question, pays it off, and raises the next one higher; that chain is the thread. Do not save every payoff for the end in a sixty-minute video, or the middle dies.

## Do not

- skip the proof block
- include every step because it is a deep dive
- run a pure information dump with no parable
- plan a step with no proof and no flag

## Example

Title: "The exact system to book 4 calls a week from a small list"

```markdown
## Proof
**Proof:**
- [[proof-bank/steve-4-calls-a-week]]: 4 a month to 4 a week in 8 days
- [[proof-bank/list-under-2k-bookings]]: 11 calls from 1,800 subscribers
- testimonial clip, dump 14:20, "I stopped doing outbound entirely"

## The parable: cold outbound is done
**Parable:** contrast, dump 06:30
- old way: the 200-message week, the spreadsheet, six no-shows on a Wednesday
- the moment: one line changed in one follow-up email
- new way: 4 calls in 8 days from the same list, nothing else touched

## Step 1: Write the trigger
**Principle:**
- a message with no trigger is noise
- the trigger is the moment the reader already has the problem: the launch that flopped, the hire that quit
- one sentence that names that moment before anything else
**Proof:** [[proof-bank/steve-4-calls-a-week]]

## Step 2: Send it to the ones who opened but did not click
**Parable:** metaphor, dump "they walked up to the door and stood there"
- the opened-not-clicked segment is the crowd at the door
- everyone else is still down the street
**Principle:**
- opened and did not click is the warmest cold list you own
- pull that segment first, before any new list
- send the trigger message to them only
**Proof:** to build

## Step 3: Book from the reply, not the link
**Principle:**
- the reply is the sale; a booking link in the first message is a door slammed
- answer the reply with one question, then the link
**Proof:** [[proof-bank/reply-first-booking-rate]]
```
