# Step-by-Step

One parable up front, then the steps. The planner calls this the distinction that defines the format: it is not parable, principle, parable, principle like a list video. One emotional beat, then the process.

## The body

```
## The parable: {what it shows}
## Step 1: {action}
## Step 2: {action}
...
## Step N: {action}
```

- **One parable section**, first. Pick the type from the planner's matrix: Visual Demo when the problem is invisible, Metaphor when it is abstract, Contrast (old way, new way) when it is wrong way versus right way, Story when viewers do not feel the stakes.
- **Eight steps at most.** If ten exist and five deliver most of the result, plan the five and log the rest as cuts. The count is what the material supports, never a target.
- **A second parable in exactly one place:** before a step viewers will not understand or will not believe until they see it work. Most videos need zero. It lives inside that step's section, not as its own section.
- Setup that delays the first useful action is a cut.

## What each section locks

Every field is a label line with its beats under it. As many beats as the section needs for a writer to draft without asking.

The parable section:

- **Parable:** the type and the material on the label. The beats under it: what happens, in order, to the moment it lands. For a contrast, both sides. For a demo, what gets shown and what the viewer sees change.

Every step:

- **Principle:** the action as a verb, what it gets the viewer, and the detail that makes it doable. `Proof:` rides the beat that makes a claim, only where the viewer could doubt it.
- **Parable:** only on the hard or doubted step, with its beats. Every other step has no Parable field at all.

The forward hook between steps is the writer's to compose. Do not plan transitions.

## Where the payoff lands

At the last step, by construction: the viewer now has the whole process. The thread is the viewer's growing capability. Do not withhold a step to manufacture tension; the sequence is the tension.

## Do not

- give every step a fresh story or metaphor
- pad the step count
- plan a step the avatar already knows
- carry a full system; that is a deep dive. Step-by-step is the sponge, not the whole cake
- split one action into two steps to reach a number

## Example

Title: "How to train your dog to walk off leash without losing him"

```markdown
## The parable: the day my dog ran
**Parable:** story, dump 04:10
- trying to teach him to walk off leash for the first time, on our own block
- the leash came off and he bolted down the street
- felt stupid: he had never done it before and now I had no idea where he was
- found him two days later. He had been hit by a car

## Step 1: Pick a place he cannot lose you
**Principle:**
- a fenced field or an empty park, nowhere he can reach a road from
- the location does the safety work so you do not have to
- you never know how they will respond in the moment, so the place has to forgive it

## Step 2: Put him on a twenty-foot line
**Principle:**
- a long line or an e-collar, so "off leash" is still on something
- if he goes, you still have a way to get control
- start small: ten feet of slack before twenty, one session at a time

## Step 3: Recall before freedom, every session
**Parable:** visual demo, to build
- one clip: the dog turning on the word, on the long line, mid-sniff
- the same clip with the line dropped, same turn
**Principle:**
- call him back three times on the line before the line comes off
- the recall is the leash now
- if he misses one, the session ends on the line. Proof: to build
```

Three steps, one story up front, one demo on the step people skip. The story has four beats because it needs four. Step 1 has three because that is what the writer needs.
