# Gap taxonomy

Eight shapes that a person leaves behind when they dump a story they already know. Each one is a place where they hold detail they did not type.

## The line you do not cross

You are looking for gaps in **the telling**, not gaps in **the video**.

A gap in the telling is something already in their head that did not make it to the page. Asking about it retrieves.

A gap in the video is something that would make the video better. Asking about it manufactures. "What's the counterargument here", "do you have a stat to back that", "what would make this more relatable", "is there a second example". Every one of those asks the creator to invent material to satisfy you, and invented material is what a script sounds like when nobody believes it.

Test before you ask: is the answer already in their memory, or would they have to make it up right now? If it is the second one, do not ask.

## The eight

**1. Named, never opened.** A person, company, tool, client, or event named once and left standing. They know the whole story. They said the name because to them the name contains it.
> You mentioned Sarah once. What happened with Sarah?

**2. Claim with no incident.** "This always breaks." "Nobody does this right." A general statement is a compressed pile of specific times. Ask for one.
> When's the last time it broke on you? What happened that time?

**3. Number with no origin.** "3x", "six months", "half my clients", "two hours a week". They know where it came from and how it was measured.
> Where's the 3x from? What was it before?

**4. Assumed shared knowledge.** "Obviously", "you know how it is", "the usual way", "everyone does this". The word obviously marks the exact spot where they stopped explaining.
> You said "obviously it fails." Say the obvious part out loud. Why does it fail?

**5. Ratio inversion.** Four paragraphs of setup and nine words on the thing that actually happened. The word count is inverted against the importance. They skimmed the part they lived through because reliving it is work.
> "And then she cried on the call." That's the whole line. What happened on that call?

**6. Abandoned thread.** They opened something and never came back. "There was also the time when", "the other thing is", "which reminds me". Then a tangent, and the thread never closed. This is invisible in your memory of the conversation and obvious in the file. It is the reason pass two reads from disk.
> You started "there was also the time when" and went somewhere else. Where were you going?

**7. Unearned transition.** "So then it worked." Two states, no middle. The middle is the part with the actual mechanism in it.
> Between "nothing was working" and "then it worked" there's a gap. What changed?

**8. First person absent.** They narrated an event start to finish and never said what they did, what they decided, or what it felt like. Usually the most valuable missing material in the whole dump, and the easiest one to miss, because the story reads complete.
> The whole thing happened and you're not in it. What were you doing while that went down?

## How to ask

**Quote their words back.** Every question opens with the fragment it is about, so they know exactly which moment you mean and do not have to hunt for it. It also proves you are asking about something they said, not something you decided the video needs.

**Ask a question that has an answer.** "Can you expand on that" is a request for effort with no target. "What did she say next" has an answer waiting.

**Select by weight, not by count.** A dump with twenty named entities does not get twenty questions. Pick the three to five gaps that have the most sitting behind them. Type 5, 6, and 8 usually carry the most.

**Never stack two questions in one number.** They answer the second and drop the first.
