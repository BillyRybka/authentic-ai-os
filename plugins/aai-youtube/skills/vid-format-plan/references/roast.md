# Roast format card

Fifteen numbered blanks, in the order the file lays them out and the order they get asked. Blanks 9 through 12 repeat once per contestant, so the real count is 11 plus 4 per review.

`knowledge/format-planners/roast.md` is the content source of truth. This card holds the wording of each ask and the bar a passing answer clears, and points back at the planner for everything else. Nothing from the planner gets restated here. Two copies of one rule drift the first time either gets edited.

**Load-bearing: blanks 11 and 13.** The fix, per review, because the planner says the fix is where the gold is and that showing the problem without it proves nothing. And the submission CTA, because the planner states in as many words that it is non-optional and that it is how the format compounds. Those two get up to two follow-ups when the answer comes back thin, then a gap callout. Every other blank gets one ask and takes whatever comes back.

**Never say the word roast to the creator's audience.** The planner gives this its own section: "free review" gets a thousand submissions, "free roast" gets twenty. Anything this skill writes that the audience will see (blank 1's post, blank 3's title, blank 13's CTA) says review. Internally, in the file and in the conversation, roast is fine.

**Section names in the plan file are the blank names below**, numbered as they are numbered here.

**One thing per ask.** Some blanks hold two things and carry an `Ask 2`. That is two short turns, not one long question. The count in the progress line does not move between them.

**Blank 7 sets the contestant count.** Until it lands, the total in the progress line carries a `+`. See the fan-out rule in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. The submission post

- **Section:** `## 1. The submission post`
- **Planner:** step 1, and the Submission funnel section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Do you already have submissions, or does the funnel need to run?
- **Ask 2:** What are you offering to review, and what result does it get them?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** either a count of submissions already in hand, or the thing being reviewed plus the result it promises, which is what the post needs.
- **Notes:** the planner has the post template. Fill its two variables from their answer and write the filled post into the file. If the audience is too small to produce submissions, the planner's workaround is public material: roast famous people or brands, no submission needed. Offer that once, only if they say the list is thin.

## 2. The form

- **Section:** `## 2. The form`
- **Planner:** step 2, and the Submission funnel section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What's the link they submit?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** what the submission actually is (a thumbnail, a landing page, a script), or a note that submissions are already in.
- **Notes:** the planner's four form fields are name, submission link, "what's your biggest struggle right now", and a permission checkbox. Write them into the file as the form spec. The struggle field is market research and the planner says to keep the form short, so do not add fields to it.

## 3. Goal

- **Section:** `## 3. Goal`
- **Planner:** step 3
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Sales or emails?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one of the two named.
- **Notes:** usually answered at step 0. The planner recommends sales and notes views can spike, but the format's strength is conversion, not reach.

## 4. Title and thumbnail

- **Section:** `## 4. Title and thumbnail`
- **Planner:** step 4
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Working title?
- **Ask 2:** What's on the thumbnail?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a title in words plus what the thumbnail shows.
- **Notes:** the planner builds the package off the most universal problem in the submissions, and wants the thumbnail to hint at the fix rather than only the problem. The title says review, never roast.

## 5. Viewer's top 3 questions

- **Section:** `## 5. Viewer's top 3 questions`
- **Planner:** step 5
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What three questions does that package put in their head?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** three questions, phrased as questions.
- **Notes:** the planner's usual three for this format run toward "could you fix mine?", which is what makes the submission CTA land later.

## 6. The problems you found

- **Section:** `## 6. The problems you found`
- **Planner:** step 6
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What's wrong across the submissions? All of it.
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a messy list of problems seen across the pile, not yet tied to contestants.
- **Notes:** the planner wants the problems looked at first, before contestants get picked, so the universal ones surface. Do not sort or group as it comes in. Blank 7 does that.

## 7. The cut: contestants and problems

- **Section:** `## 7. The cut: contestants and problems`
- **Planner:** step 7
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Which three are you reviewing?
- **Ask 2:** Themed, all the same problem, or variety?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** up to three contestants named, each with up to three problems. The planner caps both at three.
- **Notes:** hold the cap. Four contestants and five problems each is a named mistake in the planner, and the fix is a second video, not a longer one. This blank sets the contestant count for blanks 9 through 12: write the list to the file, then write one block per review before the next question goes out. The planner also says pick universal problems over obscure ones, since the watching audience has to see themselves in the seat.

## 8. Intro: what's on screen

- **Section:** `## 8. Intro: what's on screen`
- **Planner:** the Intro adaptation table, row 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Which submission opens the video?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one submission named, the one whose problem is most visible on screen.
- **Notes:** the planner says the format hands you raw visual material and to use it as the parable, not as the hook itself. The submission carries the energy while the spoken line frames the stakes. Do not write the line.

## 9. Review {n}: what they have

- **Section:** `### 9.{n} Review {n}: what they have`
- **Planner:** the Per-review structure, beat 1
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Repeats:** once per contestant, count set by blank 7
- **Ask:** Review {n}. What are we looking at?
- **Ask 2:** What did they say their struggle was?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the submission described well enough to put on screen, whose it is, and the problem they wrote in about.
- **Notes:** the second ask runs only when the submission came through the form. The self-diagnosis is usually wrong, and the distance between the problem they named and the problem blank 10 finds is the review's story. Write both down and let the video walk that gap.

## 10. Review {n}: what's wrong

- **Section:** `### 10.{n} Review {n}: what's wrong`
- **Planner:** the Per-review structure, beat 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Repeats:** once per contestant, count set by blank 7
- **Ask:** What's wrong with it?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** up to three specific problems, each pointing at something visible in the submission.
- **Notes:** the planner's tone benchmark is a stern but kind expert at 7 out of 10, not 10. Write what they say. If it lands at 10, say once that cruelty costs the conversion because viewers stop seeing themselves in the seat, then write what they decide.

## 11. Review {n}: the fix

- **Section:** `### 11.{n} Review {n}: the fix`
- **Planner:** the Per-review structure, beat 3
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Repeats:** once per contestant, count set by blank 7
- **Ask:** What would you do instead?
- **Push 1:** Specifically. What do they change first?
- **Push 2:** If you had their file open right now, what's the first thing you'd move?
- **Passing:** a do-this-not-that the viewer could copy onto their own work. "Make it clearer" is thin. "Cut the bottom two lines and move the number to the left" passes.
- **Notes:** the planner: the fix is where the gold is, pointing out the problem is one thing, showing the solution is where viewers convert. It repeats per review, so the pushes run per review. The first one teaches the shape and the rest usually land.

## 12. Review {n}: the result

- **Section:** `### 12.{n} Review {n}: the result`
- **Planner:** the Per-review structure, beat 4
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Repeats:** once per contestant, count set by blank 7
- **Ask:** What does the fixed version look like?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a mockup you can make, a comparable winner you can show, or a number the fix should move.
- **Notes:** the planner wants the result shown, not described. A named comparable ("here's a channel doing it right") counts.

## 13. The submission CTA

- **Section:** `## 13. The submission CTA`
- **Planner:** step 8, and the Intro adaptation note that it is non-optional
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What's the ask for the next round of submissions?
- **Ask 2:** Where do the three mentions land?
- **Push 1:** What do they actually do? Where do they click?
- **Push 2:** Say the first one. It has to be inside 90 seconds.
- **Passing:** an ask with a destination, plus three placements, the first inside the first 90 seconds.
- **Notes:** the planner calls this non-optional and the reason the format compounds: no CTA, the funnel breaks, future videos run dry. It says review, never roast. On the sales goal this sits alongside the offer CTA rather than replacing it, since the planner runs both.

## 14. Delivery

- **Section:** `## 14. Delivery`
- **Planner:** step 9, and the Output format section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Screen record while you film, or review offline and present after?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one of the two named.
- **Notes:** the planner says most creators try the live screen record once, find it too slow, and end up doing the offline version. Say that once if they are undecided, and that the scripted ones come out tighter, then write down their call.

## 15. Upload and track

- **Section:** `## 15. Upload and track`
- **Planner:** step 10, and the End screen routing section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Which video does the end screen point at?
- **Ask 2:** What number tells you this one worked?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a real published title and one metric tied to the goal in blank 3.
- **Notes:** the planner routes by goal but says always consider another review, since this format has a loyal audience that binges it. A video they intend to make is a gap, not an answer.
