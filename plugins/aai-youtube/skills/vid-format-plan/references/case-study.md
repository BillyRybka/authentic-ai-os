# Case study format card

Fifteen blanks, in the order the file lays them out and the order they get asked. Nothing repeats in this format.

`knowledge/format-planners/case-study.md` is the content source of truth. This card holds the wording of each ask and the bar a passing answer clears, and points back at the planner for everything else. Nothing from the planner gets restated here. Two copies of one rule drift the first time either gets edited.

**Load-bearing: the five story questions, and nothing else.** The planner is explicit about them: a missing one leaves the case study with a hole. Those five get up to two follow-ups when the answer comes back thin, then a gap callout. Every other blank gets one ask and takes whatever comes back.

**Section names in the plan file are the blank names below**, numbered as they are numbered here.

**One thing per ask.** Some blanks hold two things and carry an `Ask 2`. That is two short turns, not one long question. The count in the progress line does not move between them. No ask on this card bundles two clauses, because a bundled question gets answered as a form.

## 1. Goal

- **Section:** `## 1. Goal`
- **Planner:** step 1 of the 8-step planner
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Sales, emails, or views?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one goal named. Not two, not "all of them."
- **Notes:** usually answered at step 0. It sets the ask in blank 13 and the end screen in blank 15, so it cannot be left open. The planner recommends sales for this format. Say that once if they are stuck between two, then take their call.

## 2. Title and thumbnail

- **Section:** `## 2. Title and thumbnail`
- **Planner:** step 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Working title?
- **Ask 2:** What's on the thumbnail?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a title in words plus what the thumbnail shows. Both can be rough. Both feed blank 3.
- **Notes:** usually answered at step 0, since most creators arrive with both already done. This skill does not generate titles or thumbnails and does not critique the one they bring. If they ask for options, the planner has title formulas and the thumbnail strategies that suit this format.

## 3. Viewer's top 3 questions

- **Section:** `## 3. Viewer's top 3 questions`
- **Planner:** step 3
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What three questions does that package put in their head?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** three questions, phrased as questions, pulled off the package rather than out of the story.
- **Notes:** the planner names the three that come up most for case studies. Offer them as candidates only after the creator has taken their own swing, never before. This blank is a plan check, not intro copy: the planner says the three get answered through the story, so if one is still dangling when the story blanks close, say so once. The intro that raises them gets written later, off this same field.

## 4. The one transformation

- **Section:** `## 4. The one transformation`
- **Planner:** step 4, plus the "Critical" line under The structure
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Who went from what to what?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one person, one before state, one after state.
- **Notes:** the failure here is too much, not too little. If they name two or three transformations, ask which single one the video is, and write only that. That is a scope cut, not a push, and it does not count against the two follow-ups. The planner: pick the single biggest thing.

## 5. Intro: the lead

- **Section:** `## 5. Intro: the lead`
- **Planner:** the Intro adaptation table, row 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Open on the receipt, or on the stakes?
- **Ask 2:** Which one specifically?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** receipt or stakes, named, plus the fact it leads with (the figure, or the situation they were in).
- **Notes:** this is a lead choice, not a written line. Do not draft the opening. If the outcome in blank 9 lands sharper than what they had here, offer once at the close to update this blank, and only once.

## Body 1: the parable

Blanks 6 through 10 are the five questions the planner says every case study answers. All five are load-bearing. They are the reason this format converts and the reason it fails.

## 6. Problem

- **Section:** `### 6. Problem`
- **Planner:** the 5 questions, Q1
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What was their problem when they came to you?
- **Push 1:** Put a number on it. What was the count, the revenue, the number of weeks?
- **Push 2:** What was the last thing that went wrong before they called you?
- **Passing:** a number or a hard state, plus a timeframe. The planner's bar: not "they were stuck" but the count and the deadline.

## 7. Stakes

- **Section:** `### 7. Stakes`
- **Planner:** the 5 questions, Q2
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** Why did they have to fix it?
- **Push 1:** What happened to them if it didn't work? Where are they in six months?
- **Push 2:** What did they say on that first call? Their words.
- **Passing:** a consequence in their life, not another business metric. Job, runway, family, a date on the calendar. Without it the viewer has no reason to stay.

## 8. Actions

- **Section:** `### 8. Actions`
- **Planner:** the 5 questions, Q3
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What did you actually do?
- **Push 1:** Which of those moved the number, and which one just filled time?
- **Push 2:** What did you try first that bombed?
- **Passing:** a short ordered list where every item ties to the outcome, failures included.
- **Notes:** a long list is the common answer and the cut happens here, not downstream. The planner: if they tried seven things and three worked, the video covers the three. Failures that came before the working action stay in. Side missions come out.

## 9. Outcome

- **Section:** `### 9. Outcome`
- **Planner:** the 5 questions, Q4
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What was the number?
- **Ask 2:** How long did it take?
- **Push 1:** What was it before, and what was it after?
- **Push 2:** Over what window? Weeks or months?
- **Passing:** a specific figure and a specific timeframe. One without the other is thin.

## 10. Proof

- **Section:** `### 10. Proof`
- **Planner:** the 5 questions, Q5
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What can you put on screen?
- **Push 1:** Do you have it now, or do you have to ask them for it?
- **Push 2:** What's the single strongest one? The one you'd freeze the video on.
- **Passing:** a named artifact that exists, or a named one that has to be requested plus who holds it. "I'll find something" is thin.
- **Notes:** check `banks/proof-bank/` for an entry that already covers it. If the file is there, link it as `[[proof-bank/{slug}]]`. If it is not, plain text.

## Body 2: the principle

## 11. The one big lesson

- **Section:** `### 11. The one big lesson`
- **Planner:** step 5, plus the Principle section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What does this prove?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one claim, stated plainly, that the story just demonstrated.
- **Notes:** the one blank where offering candidates earns its keep. Build them out of the actions they named in blank 8 and nothing else. Two candidates, not five, and their edit wins.

## 12. The viewer's 1-3 steps

- **Section:** `### 12. The viewer's 1-3 steps`
- **Planner:** the Principle section, item 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What can the viewer go do themselves?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** between one and three, each one an action rather than an idea.
- **Notes:** if they give more than three, ask which three. The planner caps it because a longer list gets remembered as none.

## 13. Conversion

- **Section:** `## 13. Conversion`
- **Planner:** step 6, and the matching subsection of Conversion strategy by goal
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** set by the goal in blank 1. Read that subsection of the planner first, then ask the one that matches.
  - **Sales:** Where does the ask go, the end or woven in?
  - **Sales 2:** What's the top link in the description?
  - **Emails:** What's the lead magnet?
  - **Emails 2:** What's it called?
  - **Views:** Anything at the end, or just let it run?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the form of the ask plus the description decision. For views, "no links" is a complete answer.

## 14. Delivery

- **Section:** `## 14. Delivery`
- **Planner:** step 7, the Output format section, and the Client interview footage rule
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Talking head the whole way?
- **Ask 2:** Got footage of them? Which moment do you cut to?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the shooting shape, plus either one named client moment or a clear no footage.
- **Notes:** the planner caps client footage at one confirming line at the moment of impact. Hold the cap if they want four. Do not write the line, name the moment.

## 15. Upload and track

- **Section:** `## 15. Upload and track`
- **Planner:** step 8, and the End screen routing section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Which video does the end screen point at?
- **Ask 2:** What number tells you this one worked?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a real published title and one metric tied to the goal in blank 1.
- **Notes:** the planner routes the end screen by goal. A video they intend to make is a gap, not an answer, because the end screen has to point somewhere on upload day.

## Where this card sits

Six sibling cards run the other formats, all built to this one's shape. The field set is the contract and `SKILL.md` holds it, so this file never has to explain itself to the skill that loads it.

Case study is the only card with five load-bearing blanks. That is the planner's doing, not a template: it names five questions and says a missing one leaves a hole. Other formats have one, two, or three, and each card's header names the line that earned the mark.
