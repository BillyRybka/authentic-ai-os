# Metaphor Integration

How to drop a metaphor into a segment without it landing as "let me give you an analogy" theater. Examples-first. Each integration pattern pairs a worked example with a near-miss.

This file is reference material for Claude to think with at Phase 3 (prose pass) when a metaphor block is in play. The metaphor builder (vid-bank's `references/metaphor-builder.md`) lives upstream and defines what metaphors ARE and how they're built. This file is about the splice.

## Why integration is harder than capture

Captured metaphors land in the bank well-shaped. Integrated metaphors land in scripts well-paced. Most metaphor failures aren't about the metaphor itself; they're about how it gets dropped in. A great metaphor announced as a metaphor flops. The same metaphor dropped clean lands.

Three integration rules. Then the bridge to the principle rule. Then the visual layer for visual metaphors.

---

## Rule 1: Don't announce the metaphor

Drop it in clean. Announcement language ("let me give you an analogy", "imagine if I told you", "think of it like") flags the metaphor as a teaching device, and the second viewers tag it as a teaching device, the metaphor stops working.

**Worked (channel-strategy niche, dropped clean):**

> "Most creators are running a restaurant with no recipes. Every Monday someone walks in and says 'what's good today?' and the chef has to invent the menu from scratch. Half the time the food's great. Half the time the chef burns out."

Why it lands: no announcement. The metaphor IS the sentence. Viewer's brain picks up the comparison without being told it's a comparison. The "burnout" punchline works because it sets up the exact problem the segment's about to solve.

**Near-miss (same niche, announced):**

> "I want to share an analogy with you. Think of your content like a restaurant. So imagine if you had a restaurant but no recipes. Every Monday, the chef would have to make up the menu, right? That's basically what most creators are doing."

Why it misses: 6 lines of announcement before the metaphor lands. By the time "restaurant with no recipes" arrives, the viewer's brain has tagged this as "lecture mode" and the metaphor's emotional charge is gone. Cut every announcement word. Drop the metaphor as the first sentence of the block.

**Replacement template:**

- Bad: "Imagine your business is like a [thing]."
- Better: "[Your business] is a [thing]."
- Best: "[Most people] / [most creators] / [most coaches] [verb] like a [thing]."

The general framing ("most creators are running a restaurant with no recipes") puts the viewer adjacent to the metaphor's protagonist without having to instruct them. Their brain does the placement.

---

## Rule 2: Don't over-explain the metaphor

A metaphor lands fast or doesn't land. Three sentences max. If it takes a paragraph to explain the metaphor, the metaphor isn't working. Drop it and use a story or visual demo instead.

**Worked (positioning niche, lean):**

> "Niche channels are pouring jelly beans into a small glass while broad channels pour into a big one. The big glass holds more, sure. But the small glass is full of revenue beans. The big glass is full of subscriber beans. Subscribers pay nothing."

Three sentences. The metaphor lands, the punch lands, move on.

**Near-miss (over-explained):**

> "Niche channels are like pouring jelly beans into a small glass. The small glass represents a smaller audience. Each jelly bean represents a viewer. The size of each jelly bean represents how engaged the viewer is. The color of each jelly bean represents how monetizable they are. So when you put a small number of highly-engaged, highly-monetizable jelly beans into a small glass, you get more value per bean than a big glass full of low-engagement beans..."

Why it misses: the metaphor stops being a metaphor and becomes a labeled diagram. Viewer brain stops doing the comparison work, starts trying to memorize what each variable maps to. The emotional punch dies in the explanation.

**The principle:** if you find yourself writing "the [thing] represents the [other thing]" prose, the metaphor failed. Drop it. Pick a different block.

---

## Rule 3: Bridge to the principle

Every metaphor needs a single-sentence bridge back to the actual teaching. Three proven bridges:

- "So what does that actually mean for you?"
- "Here's how to apply this in real life."
- "Here's how to fix that, step by step."

Some bank entries have a custom pivot phrase in the `> [!tip] The pivot phrase` callout. Use that one if it exists.

**Worked:**

> [Metaphor lands: "Niche channels pour into a small glass full of revenue beans"]
>
> "So what does that actually mean for you? It means you can grow slower and earn more. Here's the math."
>
> [Principle: actual revenue math per niche size]

Why it lands: pivot phrase resets the energy from emotional to logical. Viewer's brain shifts gears cleanly.

**Near-miss:**

> [Metaphor lands]
> [Immediately into a 2-minute principle with no bridge]

Why it misses: viewer is mid-emotional-process when the principle arrives, doesn't fully shift gears, half the principle lands. The bridge isn't decoration. It's the gear-shift signal.

## Visual metaphors: the two-layer rule

Visual metaphors have a SPOKEN layer and a SHOWN layer running in parallel. The bank entry should have both layers in its body. The segment integrates them as parallel tracks.

**Worked (jelly bean channels visual metaphor):**

Spoken layer (in script):

> "Two glasses on a table. One full of jelly beans, one with just a few."

Shown layer (in `> [!note] visual:` callout):

> [!note] visual: physical setup. Two short glasses on a kitchen counter, one filled with red jelly beans (label "1"), the other with ~6 jelly beans (label "2"). Hold for 2 seconds. Then on-screen text appears: "$15K" above glass 1, "$150K" above glass 2.

Why it lands: spoken and shown reinforce each other. The viewer hears the comparison and sees it simultaneously. The on-screen captions ($15K / $150K) carry the punchline without the spoken track having to deliver it.

**Near-miss (visual metaphor with only the spoken layer):**

Spoken layer in script:

> "Two glasses on a table. One full of jelly beans, one with just a few. The big one is broad channels, the small one is niche channels."

No `> [!note] visual:` callout. Editor films just talking-head with no props or graphics.

Why it misses: visual metaphors need the visual. Without the props on screen, the metaphor is suddenly trying to do the work of a non-visual metaphor and failing. Either include the visual layer instructions or pick a non-visual metaphor instead.

**Near-miss (visual metaphor with the spoken layer over-describing the visual):**

Spoken layer:

> "I'm holding up two glasses. The one on my left has lots of jelly beans. The one on my right has fewer. See how the left one has more beans than the right one? That's the difference between broad and niche channels."

Why it misses: the spoken layer is doing the visual's job. If the visual is on screen, the spoken track shouldn't narrate what's being shown. The spoken layer should add INFORMATION the visual doesn't carry (the names of the things being compared, the punchline, the lesson). Trust the visual to do its job.

## Mixed metaphor risk

Don't stack two metaphors in one segment. The brain can't hold both.

**Near-miss:**

> "Your content is a garden. You plant seeds, water them, watch them grow. But it's also a rocket ship: once it takes off, you can't stop the trajectory."

Why it misses: garden-and-rocket compete for the viewer's mental real estate. Pick one. The garden metaphor works for slow growth. The rocket works for momentum. Either is fine. Both is noise.

**The check:** if the segment's structure draft pulls TWO metaphor candidates from the bank, surface the conflict to the creator. Pick one for this segment, save the other for a future segment.

---

## When to skip the metaphor

Pulling a metaphor from the bank doesn't mean the segment must use it. Drop the metaphor if:

- The idea is already clear without it. Don't dress up simple ideas.
- A story or visual demo would land harder. Metaphors compete with blocks; pick the strongest.
- The metaphor's everyday category clashes with the segment's tone. A wine-tasting metaphor lands in a positioning segment but feels off in a tactical "fix your CTA" segment.
- The creator has already used a metaphor in a prior segment of this video. Two metaphors in one video dilutes both.

When skipping, mark the bank entry as "considered but skipped for {reason}" in the structure draft notes. Don't update `used_in:` since the entry wasn't used.

## Surfacing metaphor candidates

At Phase 2, surface metaphor candidates with the visual flag visible:

```
Metaphor candidates for this segment (concept: subscriber-vanity):

1. [[jelly-bean-channels]]: visual metaphor (props required), category: food, themes: vanity-metrics
2. [[restaurant-with-no-recipes]]: non-visual metaphor, category: food, themes: systems
3. [[wine-tasting-early-dating]]: non-visual metaphor, category: food, themes: commitment

(Note: candidate 3 is a stretch. It's about commitment, not subscriber-vanity. Surfacing because of partial match.)

Pick one, or want me to skip the metaphor for this segment?
```

If the segment's format can't support a visual metaphor (e.g., news segment with no production time), filter visual metaphors out and only surface non-visual candidates.

## How vid-segment uses this file

At Phase 2 (structure pass), when the block is metaphor:

1. Query `banks/metaphor-bank/*.md` by `concept:` field, `category:`, and theme tags
2. Filter visual metaphors based on format support (news / step-by-step compress visual metaphors out)
3. Surface 0-2 candidates with the visual flag visible

At Phase 3 (prose pass):

1. Drop the metaphor clean (no announcement)
2. Limit to 3 sentences
3. Add the `> [!note] visual:` callout if visual: true
4. Add the bridge to the principle line
5. Verify the metaphor isn't competing with another metaphor elsewhere in the segment

At Phase 4 (save):

1. Update the metaphor's `used_in:` and flip `status:` to `used`
2. Update piece's `metaphors_used:` array
