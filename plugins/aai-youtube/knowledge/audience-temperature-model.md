# Audience Temperature Model

Audience temperature is the warmth of a viewer's relationship with the creator. Cold viewers don't know you. Warm viewers have watched a few videos. Hot viewers feel you're speaking directly to them and are ready to buy. The temperature a video creates determines how well it converts, and a 50k-view hot-audience video will out-earn a 250k-view cold-audience video.

This file is shared knowledge. Every skill that makes a decision about who the video is for or how it converts loads this.

## The three temperatures

### Cold

The viewer is discovering the creator for the first time. They have no memory of prior videos, no opinion about the creator's authority, no buying intent.

**Cold viewer behavior:** clicks based on the thumbnail/title only. Skims the first 30 seconds. Bounces fast if the hook doesn't land. Won't buy. Might subscribe IF the video over-delivers on the title's promise.

**What attracts cold viewers:** broad topics, novelty, viral patterns, curiosity-gap hooks, low specificity. They don't know the creator's niche, so generic-but-attention-grabbing wins.

### Warm

The viewer has seen 1-3 videos from this creator. They're evaluating: is this creator worth their time? Do their videos consistently solve a problem the viewer has?

**Warm viewer behavior:** clicks because the creator has earned a small amount of trust. Watches longer than cold viewers. May click through to an opt-in or a sales link if the offer is clearly aligned with the problem the videos have been solving. Won't buy a high-ticket offer yet.

**What attracts warm viewers:** specific topics that go deeper than the cold-attracting topics. The viewer wants to see "does this creator actually know their stuff?" They reward depth over novelty.

### Hot

The viewer has seen 4+ videos. They feel like the creator is talking directly to them. The viewer trusts the creator's judgment on the problem the creator solves.

**Hot viewer behavior:** clicks because the creator has earned high trust. Watches the full video. Clicks the sales link without hesitation when the offer fits. Buys.

**What attracts hot viewers:** narrow specificity. Named methods, specific results, advanced moves the cold/warm audience can't follow. A 30-minute deep dive on a niche-within-the-niche.

## What determines temperature

The temperature a video creates is determined by FOUR choices:

1. **Topic**, broad topics attract cold; narrow specific topics attract warm/hot
2. **Problem framing**, a generic problem ("how to grow on YouTube") attracts cold; a specific problem ("how to fix retention on a 7-minute deep dive") attracts hot
3. **Stories used**, generic illustrative stories attract cold; the creator's own client-result stories attract warm/hot
4. **Frameworks shared**, borrowed frameworks attract cold; the creator's named systems attract hot

A video can attract any temperature regardless of view count. View count tracks reach, not temperature. The two are independent signals.

## The conversion math

Cold viewers convert at low rates. Hot viewers convert at high rates. The numbers:

- **Broad video example:** 250,000 cold viewers → 5,000 click through to sales page → 10 sales (0.004% video-to-sale)
- **Specific video example:** 50,000 hot viewers → 1,000 click through → 40 sales (0.08% video-to-sale)

Same product, same sales page. The specific video sold 4x more from 1/5 the views. The difference is temperature.

The implication: when the goal is sales or email signups, optimizing for view count is the wrong optimization. Optimize for the temperature the video creates.

## Dollar Per View (the temperature signal)

Dollar Per View (DPV) = total revenue / total views.

A high DPV signals an extremely hot audience. The lesson is to repeat the topic, problem framing, story, and framework that drove that video, because that combination creates hot viewers reliably.

DPV is a post-publish measurement. vid-framing uses it predictively, when an angle anchors to a past high-DPV video from the creator's own channel, that anchor is HIGH confidence.

## Temperature × Goal, the match matrix

Different goals require different temperatures. Picking a goal without matching the temperature is the most common framing mistake.

### Sales goal

**Requires hot audience.** Cold viewers don't buy. Warm viewers might if the offer is low-ticket and the problem is obvious. Hot viewers buy.

**Implication for framing:** sales videos need narrow specificity, a named problem the creator has solved before, and a story-anchored proof element. If the angle is broad and the brain-dump material is thin on specifics, the angle is wrong for a sales goal.

**Hard rule:** if `goal: sales` and the script reads cold (written for a viewer who doesn't know the creator), surface the mismatch. The creator may have a valid reason (e.g., they're building hot audience over time and this video is part of the warming flywheel). Don't block; surface.

### Email signup goal

**Requires warm audience minimum.** Warm viewers will trade an email address for a clearly aligned lead magnet. Cold viewers will trade an email only if the magnet stands alone as obviously valuable (rare).

**Implication for framing:** email-goal videos need clear payoff alignment between the video's content and the lead magnet. The video opens a curiosity loop the magnet closes. Specificity helps but isn't required at hot-level.

### Views goal

**Either cold or warm works.** Views-goal videos are the channel's hot-audience-warming engine. They sacrifice immediate conversion for reach + binge-able-ness.

**Implication for framing:** views-goal videos can be broader. They use the surface of the iceberg, not the depth. Cold-attracting hooks. Format choices that travel well (Short Process, Listicle, News).

**Reminder:** views without temperature build a list of 3,000 that's functionally 750. A views-goal video can run broad, but keep it inside the iceberg, the lane the channel serves. Broad is not the same as off-lane.

## Temperature × Specificity, the calibration

Specificity is the controllable dial that moves a video's temperature.

| Specificity | Example | Temperature attracted |
|---|---|---|
| Generic mindset/concept | "How to be consistent" | Cold |
| Topic-level | "How to make better thumbnails" | Cold-warm |
| Tactic-level | "The 3 thumbnail strategies I rotate" | Warm |
| Result-anchored | "How I made $250k from one specific video" | Warm-hot |
| Named-system | "Inside my Three-Circle Research method" | Hot |
| Edge-case | "When the Three-Circle method fails (and what to do)" | Hot+ |

vid-framing uses this calibration when surfacing angle candidates. Each candidate's specificity level should match the target temperature.

## Worked example, same topic, three temperatures

**Brain-dump topic:** the creator's framework for setting weekly priorities under ADHD.

**Cold-framing angle:** "How to be more productive with ADHD." Generic. Broad. Cold-attracting.

**Warm-framing angle:** "The weekly planning method I switched to after burning out." Specific to the creator's experience. Warm-attracting.

**Hot-framing angle:** "Why the weekly planning method works for ADHD-brain CEOs and fails for everyone else." Niche-within-niche. Hot-attracting.

Same source material. Three different temperatures based on framing choice. Pick the temperature that matches the goal.

## Near-miss to avoid

A creator picks `goal: sales` because they want to sell their offer this month. The angle surfaced is "How to be more productive with ADHD." Cold-attracting framing on a sales-goal video.

The temperature math: 100k cold viewers → 2,000 click → 4 sales. The creator publishes, sees the views, feels good. Sales tracker shows 4 sales. The creator concludes "my offer doesn't convert." Wrong conclusion. The temperature was wrong.

The fix: same brain-dump, re-framed at hot. "Inside the weekly planning method I use to scale my coaching business as an ADHD CEO." 20k hot viewers → 800 click → 32 sales. 8x the sales from 1/5 the views.

## Temperature signals during framing (predictive)

vid-framing can't measure temperature pre-publish. It predicts based on the framing choices:

- Anchor outlier from the creator's own channel with high DPV signal → angle is HIGH confidence for hot
- Anchor outlier from niche channel with high view count + creator's stories used → angle is WARM-HOT
- Adjacent-niche structural pattern + creator's specific topic → can go either way; depends on how specific the topic is
- No anchor (experimental) → temperature is creator's gut estimate; flag uncertainty
