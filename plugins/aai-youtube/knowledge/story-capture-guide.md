# Story Capture Guide

Stories beat facts. Facts activate one or two memory pathways. Stories activate many more, because emotion is the trigger that stores information into long-term memory. A well-told 20 to 30 second story can do more persuasive work than three minutes of explanation.

This guide teaches how stories are structured. Vid-capture uses it to extract and log stories into the bank. Downstream writing skills (vid-segment, vid-intro) use it to understand what they're pulling and where it fits in a script.

## Real examples (read these first)

### Example 1: Airbnb photo swap (Own story)

**Problem:** My Airbnb listing was making a loss. I was losing money every month and couldn't figure out why, staring at empty booking calendars.

**Action:** I swapped the main photo in the listing for one that actually showed off the space.

**Outcome:** The listing was booked three times that week and hasn't been empty since.

Why it lands: one specific action, a vivid before (losing money, empty calendar), a specific after (booked three times that week). No filler.

### Example 2: Pricing a digital product (Own story)

**Problem:** When I launched my first course, I priced it at $49 because I was scared to charge more.

**Action:** I worked for three months at that price.

**Outcome:** I made $900, and realized I'd built myself a minimum wage job.

Why it lands: the specific price ($49), the specific timeline (three months), the specific end result ($900) plus the twist reframing. The twist is the kicker.

### Example 3: Parasites and the WhatsApp sales call (Own story)

**Problem:** I was very ill with parasites, trying to grow a business while working only a couple hours a day. I felt awful and wanted to retire.

**Action:** I told the story on a video.

**Outcome:** The next day I got a WhatsApp from a major channel saying "your story is exactly what I'm going through, can you help me?" That one story filled an entire sales cohort.

Why it lands: the vulnerability (wanting to retire) is the hook. The outcome is a direct line from told story to closed business. Proves the "be honest about what you're struggling with" thesis.

## The three story types

Pick the type first. This drives everything else.

### Client story (highest sales power)

A client's transformation. "I helped Sarah do X and she got Y." Removes the objection "of course you could do it, could someone else?" Viewers trust this type most because it's not the creator talking about themselves.

Use when: proving a method works for someone besides yourself. Usually strongest for sales-oriented videos.

### Own story (strong credibility)

The creator's personal experience solving the problem. Admitting failure makes it more powerful, not less. "I was broke for three years trying this the wrong way. Here's what finally worked."

Use when: establishing credibility, breaking a viewer belief, or the creator's experience is uniquely relevant.

### Viewer story (fallback)

Someone else's journey, told as "you sign up, you do this, you see this result." Use when the creator has no personal story and no client story that fits. Avoid well-known public figures because the audience already knows the arc. No surprise left.

Use when: nothing better exists. This is the least trusted type, better than no story.

## The Problem / Action / Outcome structure

Every story in the bank follows this shape. 20 to 30 seconds total when spoken aloud.

### Problem (1 to 2 sentences)

Drop the viewer straight into the relatable pain point at its worst moment. Not the beginning of the journey, the moment of struggle. Specific, vivid language.

"I had not slept for 48 hours" works. "I wasn't feeling that great" doesn't. Go for emotion: frustration, fear, confusion, embarrassment.

Ask: what's the relatable problem my viewers have right now that I'm about to mirror?

### Action (1 to 3 sentences max)

Show the one big thing that started solving the problem. One choice, one decision. Not everything you did, the key move. Three maximum.

Don't list. The story's job is to inspire, not to inform. Avoid vague language like "I decided to try something different." Be concrete: "I stopped posting broad content and only made videos on one niche topic."

Ask: what's the most surprising action I took that started solving the problem?

### Outcome (1 to 2 sentences)

Reveal what happened. Must connect back to the problem you started with or viewers feel cheated. Can be a win, a loss, or a surprising lesson.

The best outcomes have a twist. If the end is wildly different from what anyone expected, that's the story to tell. Otherwise the outcome needs to be specific: exact numbers, exact timeline, exact before vs. after.

## The 6 prompts for unlocking stories

If the creator says "I don't have a story for this," walk through these. Each is designed to surface a memory they forgot they had. Each has dynamic follow-ups: reframe if the opener doesn't land, pivot to a different prompt if the angle isn't right.

**One prompt per round. Don't rapid-fire all six.** Pick the prompt that matches the video's emotional tone and the avatar's pain. Start there. If it doesn't land within 1-2 rounds, pivot to the next-best prompt. Don't grind on a prompt that isn't unlocking a memory.

### How to use each prompt

For every prompt below:

1. **Open with the conversational version** (not the dimensions). Make it feel like a real interview question.
2. **Listen for a thin or vague response** ("I guess so," "I think one time," "well, I had something like that..."). Thin = no specific moment, no time/place, no concrete consequence.
3. **Reframe** if the first phrasing doesn't unlock. Use the alternate phrasings under each prompt. Sometimes a small wording change opens the door.
4. **Drill the dimensions** if a memory IS surfacing but is vague. Use the 3 sub-questions plus the generic probes in "Dig deeper probes" below.
5. **Pivot** if 1-2 rounds of reframe don't unlock a memory. Route to the next-best prompt for this video's tone (see Pivot routes per prompt).
6. **Bail to "skip" or "use a placeholder"** if 3 prompts in a row don't land. Don't force a story that isn't there.

### Prompt 1: Embarrassing First Attempt

**Opener:** "What's a time you tried [the thing this video teaches] for the first time and it went badly? Even better if it's embarrassing now looking back."

**Reframes** (if the opener doesn't unlock):
- "Doesn't have to be the first time you ever did it. First serious attempt. First time you really tried."
- "What's a memory you'd cringe at if it came up at dinner?"
- "First public attempt. First time someone watched you try."

**Drill (after a memory surfaces):**
- What made it embarrassing, what specifically was bad?
- How did you react in the moment? Did you keep going or quit?
- What did you learn that shaped how you do it now?

**Thin vs strong:**
- Thin: "Yeah I had a bad first video, it didn't get many views." (no moment, no specifics)
- Strong: "I filmed my first video on my laptop webcam, the audio was distorted, and my mom commented 'are you okay?' before anyone else watched it. I didn't post for 6 months after."

**Pivot routes:** if the creator's "first attempt" memory is too distant or rosy, try Prompt 2 (Costly Rookie Mistake, sharper because of the cost) or Prompt 6 (Did What Taught, sharper because of the betrayal angle).

### Prompt 2: Costly Rookie Mistake

**Opener:** "Tell me about a time you made a beginner mistake that actually cost you something: money, time, a relationship, an opportunity."

**Reframes:**
- "What's a mistake you'd undo if you could? Not a small one. One that hurt."
- "What's something you'd warn a beginner about because YOU did it?"
- "Has anything cost you a client / a sale / a launch / a deadline because you didn't know better?"

**Drill:**
- What did you overlook or underestimate?
- How could you have avoided it (with the wisdom you have now)?
- What changes did you make after?

**Thin vs strong:**
- Thin: "I should've niched down sooner."
- Strong: "I spent $4,200 on Facebook ads to a generic landing page and got two leads. Both ghosted. I burned through three months of savings."

**Pivot routes:** if the creator gives a "shoulda" without a specific cost, pivot to Prompt 6 (Did What Taught, same betrayal energy with a clearer pivot point) or Prompt 4 (Advice Ignored, easier if the cost is emotional rather than financial).

### Prompt 3: Sudden Breakthrough Moment

**Opener:** "When did the thing you teach actually click for you? Not gradually, the specific moment."

**Reframes:**
- "What's the moment you stopped guessing and started knowing?"
- "When did the lights come on?"
- "What's the moment you realized you'd been doing it wrong this whole time?"

**Drill:**
- What had you been struggling with right before the breakthrough?
- What exactly shifted, what changed in your perspective or method?
- How did your results change immediately after?

**Thin vs strong:**
- Thin: "I just kept practicing and eventually got better."
- Strong: "I was on call #47 of the year, getting hung up on for the third time that morning, and I tried a different opener, 'Hey, I'm cold-calling you, want me to keep going or hang up?' The guy laughed and gave me 8 minutes. Closed him. Used that opener every cold call after."

**Pivot routes:** breakthroughs are often quiet, not dramatic. If the creator can't name a moment, try Prompt 5 (Tiny Tweak, same shape but framed as a small change) or Prompt 4 (Advice Ignored, sometimes breakthroughs come from finally listening).

### Prompt 4: Advice Ignored, Then Learned

**Opener:** "Has anyone ever given you advice you blew off, and then later realized they were right?"

**Reframes:**
- "Whose advice do you wish you'd taken sooner?"
- "What's something you used to fight on, and now you teach?"
- "What's the thing your mentor told you that you didn't believe at first?"

**Drill:**
- What made you dismiss it at the time?
- What experience proved them right?
- How do you teach that thing now?

**Thin vs strong:**
- Thin: "My mom said school was important and I get it now."
- Strong: "My business coach told me 'stop selling on the call, get them to ask for the offer.' I argued for 20 minutes. Three months later I tried it on a discovery call, the prospect said 'how do I work with you?' before I'd pitched. Closed her. Now it's the whole training I sell."

**Pivot routes:** if no specific advisor comes to mind, try Prompt 6 (Did What Taught, flip side, where wrong advice was followed) or Prompt 3 (Sudden Breakthrough, sometimes the "advice" was self-realized).

### Prompt 5: Tiny Tweak, Huge Payoff

**Opener:** "What's the smallest change you ever made that got the biggest result?"

**Reframes:**
- "What's a one-line change that doubled your results?"
- "What did you switch ON or OFF that made everything work?"
- "What's a single sentence / setting / decision you'd undo if it stopped working?"

**Drill:**
- What was the exact tweak (one specific change)?
- Why did it work, what was broken about the old way?
- What's the lesson someone else can take from this?

**Thin vs strong:**
- Thin: "I improved my workflow."
- Strong: "I moved my morning email check from 9am to 4pm. That's it. My deep work doubled. I went from finishing two videos a week to four."

**Pivot routes:** if the creator can't isolate a single tweak, try Prompt 3 (Sudden Breakthrough, same payoff energy, allows for a moment instead of a tweak) or Prompt 1 (Embarrassing First Attempt, sometimes the tweak shows up against the failed first attempt).

### Prompt 6: Did What You / They Were Taught, But It Didn't Work

**Opener:** "Tell me about a time you followed proven advice or common wisdom in your field and it just didn't work for you."

**Reframes:**
- "What's the conventional wisdom in your space that's actually wrong?"
- "What did all the gurus tell you to do that flopped?"
- "What did you do by the book that the book got wrong?"

**Drill:**
- Why didn't it work in YOUR case (audience, market, niche)?
- What did you do instead?
- What's the takeaway someone else in your shoes should know?

**Thin vs strong:**
- Thin: "All the advice didn't really fit my niche."
- Strong: "Every YouTube guru said 'post 3x a week consistently for 6 months.' I did. 18 videos, 200 subs total, no sales. Switched to one video a month, scripted in 10 hours, and went from $0 to $40k in three months. Frequency wasn't the lever. Depth was."

**Pivot routes:** if the creator can't think of failed advice they followed, try Prompt 2 (Costly Rookie Mistake, same energy, framed as their own error rather than bad advice) or Prompt 4 (Advice Ignored, flip side).

### When to bail

If the creator goes 3 prompts deep without a memory surfacing, stop. Either:

- The video doesn't actually need a story (a metaphor or demo might land it faster, see [[metaphor-builder]] or [[parable-decision-matrix]])
- The creator's lived material on this topic is genuinely thin and the right move is to capture stories from clients, viewers, or studied creators instead
- The creator needs to come back later. Sometimes the right memory surfaces hours after the prompt was asked

Don't fabricate a story to fill the slot. An empty slot beats a thin one.

## Dig deeper probes (use when P-A-O is thin)

Creators often give a polished, vague version of the story first. The specifics are what make it land. Push.

When the Problem is vague:
- "What did that feel like at the worst moment?"
- "Paint the picture. Where were you? What were you looking at?"
- "What's the detail that made it sting?"

When the Action is vague:
- "What's the one thing you did? Not everything, the key move."
- "What made that action different from what you'd tried before?"

When the Outcome is vague:
- "What's the exact number, timeline, or result?"
- "What changed that you can measure?"
- "Is there one surprising detail you skipped over?"

Typically 2 to 3 rounds. Loop until specificity emerges, then save. If the story still doesn't have specifics, flag in Notes. Don't force detail that isn't there, and don't invent.

## When to use a story in a video

Not every point needs a story. Use one when:

- The tip feels bland or overused without context
- The viewer needs to feel "that's exactly my struggle"
- The topic is emotionally charged
- You're asking the viewer to change a habit, invest effort, or spend money

Skip the story when:

- The point is simple and self-explanatory
- You've already used a story at the previous emotion point (don't stack)
- A metaphor or demo would make it land faster

One story per emotional moment, not two.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting the story at the beginning of the journey.** Start at the worst moment of the problem.
- **Listing every action.** Pick one. Three max.
- **Generic outcomes.** "It changed everything" means nothing. Specifics or skip.
- **Polishing into marketing copy.** The creator's actual phrasing wins. Preserve it.
- **Stacking stories.** Two stories in a row is noise. One story plus a logic point, then onto the next point.
