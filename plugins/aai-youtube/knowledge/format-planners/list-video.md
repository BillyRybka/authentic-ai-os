# List Video Format Planner

A 10-20 minute video that delivers N standalone points (each with its own parable plus principle mini-cycle). Most YouTube videos are technically list videos, even without numbers in the title. Format-loyal viewers want quick, scannable takeaways. Audience-dependent. Some channels' viewers love it, others reject it.

## When to use this format

- **Viewer type:** Casual viewers and beginners. People who want value in pieces, not a unified system. Those who'll consume the video as background while doing something else.
- **Goals:** Views (recommended) OR Emails. Sales as alternative if a strong point connects to an offer.
- **Creator level:** All levels.
- **Best used for:** broad-appeal videos, beginner content, transferable lessons that don't require sequential application, riding algorithm spikes.
- **Format scoring:** Views 3/5, Sales 3/5, Trust 3/5. Threes across the board is what makes this the flexible middle ground, and the source notes some channels run it at fives; if that is true on this channel, the channel's own data overrules the table.

### The audience-fit caveat

List Videos work for some channels and tank for others. A creator's stated experience: their own audience didn't respond to list videos ("they want a process, not 10 random steps"), while another channel in a different niche made list videos a cornerstone format. **Test the format with your specific audience before committing to it as a regular cadence.**

## The structure

```
INTRO → [PARABLE + PRINCIPLE] x N → END
```

Each point follows its own mini-cycle: parable (visual demo, story, contrast, breakdown, or metaphor), then one logical lesson the viewer can action. Then transition to the next point. Repeat.

Unlike Step-by-Step (one parable, then sequential principle steps), a List Video has parable-and-principle at every point. The structural challenge: keeping viewers engaged BETWEEN points. Strong transitions are the lifeline.

## Intro adaptation

List Video intros tease the count and the cumulative payoff. The format makes the promise easy: N specific things. The intro needs to establish the count's value.

| Universal intro step | List Video default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Top 3 viewer questions | Same. Usually variants of "what are these?", "are they actually useful?", "should I take notes?" | List Video viewers want to know what they're committing to. |
| 2. Hook | Often the COUNT itself ("Here's 40 brutal truths I wish I'd known earlier") OR a personal-confession framing of a Statement Hook ("Did you think this would be easy? I did.") | The count creates the contract. Statement Hook works (and a confession framing, admitting a mistake or naive belief, is just a delivery style on top of it). Question Hook works when the count answers a question viewers have. Contrarian Hook works if at least one point in the list inverts widely-held belief. |
| 3. Problem/Result | Often Combine Both, told briefly before the count tease | Establish why this list exists, then promise the count. |
| 4. Setup | The count plus tease of cumulative payoff: "By the end you'll know X, Y, Z" or just "All [N] things every [audience] should know" | The list count IS the setup contract. |
| 5. Transition | "Starting with #1..." OR "The first one is..." | Direct, leads into point 1. |
| 6. Credibility line | Often woven into the count itself ("I tested 50 of these so you don't have to") OR into the intro Result tease | The count and the credibility can fuse. |

**Typical length: 15-25 seconds.**

**Strong intro example:**
> "Did you think growing a channel would be easy? I did. I made over 300 YouTube videos and eventually generated millions of views, but my gosh, it was such a grind. I really wish I could have done things smarter to speed up results. To make your business's journey 10 times easier, here's 40 brutal truths about growing a business's YouTube channel I wish I'd known a lot sooner."

That's: hook (cognitive dissonance, "thought it would be easy"), then problem and result (300 videos, grind, then millions of views), then setup with count plus payoff, ready for transition into #1.

## The 10-step planner

1. **Set goal.** Views, Emails, or Sales.
2. **Thumbnails and title.** Lead with the count plus the cumulative promise.
3. **Viewer's top 3 questions.** Based on title and thumbnail.
4. **Brain dump.** List every idea, lesson, point you might include. Don't worry about order. Aim for 1.5-2x the final count so refinement has room.
5. **Refine.** Cut to the strongest N. Apply: "would the average viewer in our avatar action this?" If no, cut.
6. **Plan parable per point.** Use the Parable Decision Matrix. Each point picks Visual Demo / Contrast / Breakdown / Story / Metaphor based on the problem the point addresses.
7. **Plan principle per point.** One framework or simple lesson per point. Don't bury a 20-step process inside one list video point. Keep it fast.
8. **Conversion strategy.** Embed CTA mid-video after a strong point if relevant.
9. **Write.** Word-for-word.
10. **Upload and track.**

## The parable decision matrix (per point)

For each point in the list:

- **Is the problem invisible or hard to see?** → Visual Demo. Show it on screen.
- **Is the problem abstract or hard to explain?** → Metaphor. Use a familiar frame.
- **Is the problem "wrong way vs right way"?** → Contrast (Old Way / New Way).
- **Is the problem visible but viewers don't feel the stakes?** → Story. Make them feel it.
- **Is the problem complex with multiple components?** → Breakdown. Walk through the parts.

Don't use the same parable type across all points. That flattens the video. Vary them.

## Transitions are the lifeline

The biggest structural challenge of list videos is keeping viewers engaged between unrelated points. Weak transitions kill retention.

**Weak transition:** "Lesson number one."
**Strong transition:** "Lesson number one is gonna get you more consistent sales and leads."
**Strong transition:** "Lesson number one is gonna stop you from worrying about people who comment on your videos ever again."

The pattern: every transition between points should **promise a result** the viewer cares about.

If the transition is just an announcement ("here's number 4"), viewers drop. If it's a forward hook ("number 4 is the one that finally got me consistent leads"), they stay.

## Per-point worked example

For a Photoshop shortcuts list video, point one might run:

> **Parable (Visual Demo):** "OK, this next tip is going to save you 10 minutes per photo edit. Right now, you're probably spending about 15 to 20 minutes cutting out an image, and it's a right pain. But look, you can actually do it in this." [shows the shortcut working, instant cutout]
>
> **Principle:** "Now let me show you the shortcut to save 15 minutes per photo." [demonstrates the shortcut step-by-step]
>
> **Transition:** "Now, point two is gonna change how you handle layer organization forever. Even if you've been working in Photoshop for years."

That's parable, then principle, then forward-hook transition.

## Common mistakes

- Weak transitions ("Lesson number 4") instead of result-promising ones
- Using the same parable type for every point
- Burying a 20-step process inside one list video point (too dense)
- Padding the count to hit a number (10 mediocre points kill more than 5 great ones)
- Aggressive shock-value title without content that delivers (creators copy the loudest titles they see, then the script doesn't deliver, viewers churn faster than the average video would)
- Keeping points the average viewer wouldn't action (the refine test from step 5)

## Output format

Word-for-word script. Use text chapter cards on screen between points (e.g., "Mistake #3" / "Lesson #4") so viewers know where they are in the list.

## Conversion strategy by goal

### Goal = Views (recommended)

- NO external links in description
- End screen → another list video (binge) or a related deep dive (variety)
- Focus entirely on retention through transitions

### Goal = Emails

- Lead magnet related to the list itself ("the printable cheat sheet of all 40 lessons")
- Mention 3x: early, middle (after a strong point), end
- Top link: lead magnet
- End screen → past high-converting email video

### Goal = Sales

- Embed CTA mid-video right after a strong point. Example: "By the way, if you want to save 3 hours per edit, check out the link below. I've got my top 20 Photoshop shortcuts."
- Place it where the emotional high of a point is fresh
- End screen → past high-converting sales video

## Title formulas

- "[Number] [Things] [Audience] [Should/Need To] [Verb]"
- "[Number] Brutal Truths About [Topic]"
- "[Number] [Mistakes/Lessons/Things] I Wish I Knew About [Topic]"
- "The [Number] [Adjective] [Things] That [Result]"
- "Top [Number] [Things] [Cohort] Are Doing [Comparator]"

## Thumbnail strategies for this format

Lean into: **Result** (specific number from the list, e.g. "$10K/MONTH" if a point's payoff is income), **Cognitive Dissonance** (when one of the points contradicts conventional wisdom), **Curiosity** (when the count itself creates curiosity, "40 truths" pulls more than "10 tips").

Show the COUNT in the thumbnail. The number is the format's contract.

## End screen routing

- **Views goal** → another list video (drives binge) or related deep dive
- **Emails goal** → past high-converting email video
- **Sales goal** → past high-converting sales video

## Strategy for compounding results

- List Videos batch well. Record a 40-point list and split into multiple videos if needed
- Use them to ride trending searches (broad-appeal, easy keyword fit)
- Reference points in future videos ("I covered this in [list-link]")
- The list itself can become a lead magnet ("the cheat sheet of all 40 truths")

## What to skip

- Don't pad the count
- Don't use weak transitions
- Don't reuse the same parable type at every point
- Don't bury complex processes inside list video points
- Don't copy aggressive titles without content that delivers
