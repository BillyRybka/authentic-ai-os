# News Format Planner

A 4-10 minute video that breaks something happening RIGHT NOW (or new-to-the-viewer information that feels like news). Speed is the format's defining constraint. Published late, news becomes "olds."

## When to use this format

- **Viewer type:** Audience that follows the niche actively. Want to be first to know. Trust speed and POV over polish.
- **Goals:** Views (recommended), Emails. **NOT for sales. Viewers came for the story, not your offer.**
- **Creator level:** All levels. Beginners can use news to ride algorithm spikes from trending topics.
- **Best used for:** breaking events relevant to the niche, surfacing under-noticed updates, announcing something new in a tool, policy, or industry that affects the avatar.
- **Format scoring:** Views 5/5, Sales 1/5, Trust 2/5

### "News" doesn't only mean breaking world events

News also means **new-to-the-viewer information.** A new feature in a tool, an under-the-radar policy change, research most people haven't seen. These can all be packaged as news for the right avatar. The energy is "you need to know this NOW," not strictly "this just happened in the world today."

## The structure

```
INTRO → WHAT HAPPENED → WHY IT MATTERS → WHAT VIEWERS SHOULD DO → END
```

- **What happened** equals the storytelling beat (what is this news, who, what, when)
- **Why it matters** equals the parable (the stakes for the viewer)
- **What viewers should do** equals the principle (action steps or watching position)

This is a tighter, faster structure than other formats. The whole video can run 4-7 minutes if the story is small.

## Intro adaptation

News has the **most compressed intro of any format.** Speed is the click-driver. Viewers want the news, not a lecture. These are defaults, not laws. Break them if the creator's channel and audience reward it.

| Universal intro step | News default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Top 3 viewer questions | Same. Derive from title and thumbnail | Same as every format. |
| 2. Hook | Statement Hook or Fact Hook works most often | They land fast and match the urgency. Question hooks waste seconds. Contrarian works when the news genuinely inverts the conventional take. Credibility Hook is unusual for news because the story carries the urgency. But if the channel's brand is built on insider authority and the audience expects the creator to lead with credibility, do it. |
| 3. Problem/Result | Often skipped or 1 line | The news IS the problem. The viewer's stakes are obvious from the headline. A creator with a strong POV can still poke or tease. Just keep it tight or the speed advantage is gone. |
| 4. Setup | Often skipped | "In this video I'm going to show you..." eats 5 seconds the format can't afford. If the news is complex enough to need a setup, give one short line. |
| 5. Transition | Tight: "Here's what happened," straight into the story | Save longer transitions for slower formats. |
| 6. Credibility line | Often skipped | Cold viewers came for the news, not the creator. Save credibility for evergreen content where it builds trust over time. If the creator's POV IS the value (insider analysis, established expert in the niche), credibility can be woven into the "why it matters" beat. |

**Typical length: 5-10 seconds.** Other formats run 15-30. News compresses because speed is the format's identity. A creator who wants a longer news intro is fine. Just understand they're trading speed for depth.

**The principle:** these defaults exist because they pattern-match what works for news. They are not gates. The skill should propose them as starting points, not enforce them as constraints.

## The 8-step planner

1. **Pick one goal.** Views or emails. Sales is wrong for this format.
2. **Thumbnails and title.** Lead with urgency. Show the news element (logo, person, product, screenshot) directly in the thumbnail. Specific titles only. No "Big Changes in Tech This Week."
3. **Viewer's top 3 questions.** Usually variants of: "What happened?", "Why does it matter to me?", "What do I do about it?"
4. **Brain dump.** What happened (facts, dates, players). Why it matters (stakes for the avatar). What to do (action steps or watching positions).
5. **Refine.** Cut everything that isn't directly answering the 3 viewer questions. Speed beats completeness.
6. **Conversion strategy.** Match the goal.
7. **Write.** Or, if you're racing the clock, **present from refined bullets** (this is the ONE format where presenting from bullets is explicitly OK).
8. **Upload and track.** Same day if breaking. The 24-hour window is the entire opportunity for breaking news.

## Speed rules

- **24-hour turnaround for breaking news.** If you can't post in a day, the story has aged out. Do something else.
- **Bullets are permitted if writing eats the window.** Present from refined notes rather than perfect script. "Get the content out fast, not perfect."
- **No b-roll fishing trips.** Use existing footage, screenshots, the news source itself. Don't delay posting for visuals.
- **The first headline you read is rarely the right one.** Verify the news isn't already 3 days old in your niche before committing. You might be late before you even start.

## What makes a news story worth covering

The gut-check: **"Holy crap, this is a big deal."**

- If your reaction is "is there a news story?", there isn't one.
- If your reaction is "wait, this changes things," that's the one.

Don't force it. Bad news videos (where the news isn't actually news to the avatar) tank harder than other formats because they break the implicit "I'll only tell you stuff that matters" contract.

## POV is required

Viewers don't watch news for a summary they could get on Twitter. They watch for the creator's TAKE. Every news video needs:

- **A unique angle or insight.** Not just "here's what happened," but "here's what nobody else is saying about it"
- **A connection back to the viewer's world.** "here's why this matters to you specifically"

A news video without POV is a press release. Press releases don't get watched.

## Common mistakes

- Forcing a story (the "hunt for news" video that breaks no real news)
- Late publication (24-hour window blown)
- Vague titles ("Big Tech This Week," REJECT, must be specific)
- Becoming a news channel (audience comes for news, not for you, kills evergreen channel growth)
- No POV, just summarizing what happened
- Pitching products (sales conversion is wrong for this format)
- Slow visuals, waiting for b-roll that doesn't post the video in time

## Output format

Word-for-word script if time permits. **Bullets-from-notes is acceptable** when speed matters more than polish. News is the one format where the speed-vs-polish tradeoff explicitly favors speed.

## Conversion strategy by goal

### Goal = Views (recommended)

- NO external links in description (kills algorithm recommendations)
- End screen → **non-news evergreen video** that's a great second video for someone new to the channel
- The whole point: convert the algorithm spike from trending content into channel audience

### Goal = Emails

- Lead magnet tied to the news ("the checklist for navigating [thing that just happened]")
- Mention 2 times: opening and closing
- Top link: lead magnet
- End screen → past high-converting email video

### NOT for Sales

If the avatar wants to buy something, news is the wrong vehicle. They came for the story, not the offer. Save sales pitches for success stories and deep dives.

## Title formulas

- "[Specific Event] Just Happened. Here's What It Means For [Avatar]"
- "[Company/Person] Just Did [Specific Thing]"
- "The [Specific Change] That [Affected Group] Need To Know About"
- "Breaking: [Specific Event]"
- "[New Thing] Just Launched. Should You Care?"

Avoid: "This Week In Tech," "Latest News," "Industry Update." Too vague, no urgency.

## Thumbnail strategies for this format

Lean into: **Curiosity** (tease the unexpected angle), **Cognitive Dissonance** (when the news inverts what people assume).

The thumbnail must show the news element directly: the logo, the person, the product, the screenshot. Abstract thumbnails fail because viewers can't tell what the news IS at a glance.

## End screen strategy: the single most important post-publish move

The whole point of news for views is to **convert the algorithm spike into channel audience.** Every viewer who watches a news video should be routed at the end to a non-news evergreen video that demonstrates the channel's actual value.

If the channel's news routes always go to MORE news, you become a news channel. Audience comes for news, not for the creator. Evergreen library never grows. **Always end-screen to evergreen.**

## Strategy for compounding results

- Don't make news a regular cadence. Make it opportunistic. When something genuinely happens, post.
- Use news spikes to grow subscribers, then route them to evergreen content via end screens
- Don't tie news to product launches or sales. Separate the goals

## What to skip

- Don't force stories that aren't news
- Don't wait for perfect polish. Post fast
- Don't end-screen to other news (kills the evergreen funnel)
- Don't pitch products. Wrong format for sales
- Don't skip POV. Without a take, the video has no reason to exist
