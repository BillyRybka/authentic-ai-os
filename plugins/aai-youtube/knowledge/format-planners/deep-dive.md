# Deep Dive Format Planner

A 30-90+ minute video that delivers a complete system or methodology, with proof front-loaded and woven throughout. The longest format. Highest sales conversion when the audience makes it to the end.

## When to use this format

- **Viewer type:** Active learners ready to commit time. Buyers in evaluation mode. People who've watched the creator before and want depth, not snippets.
- **Goals:** Sales (recommended for established creators) OR Views (recommended for small or new channels). Emails as alternative.
- **Creator level:** **Intermediate to advanced only.** Beginners should post 10-20 videos in other formats first. Deep dives are unforgiving. If the substance isn't there, the length kills.
- **Best used for:** evergreen conversion assets, channel trailers, definitive treatments of a topic the creator wants to own. The "this is the only video you need to watch" type.
- **Format scoring:** Views 4/5, Sales 5/5, Trust 5/5

### Critical caveat

A creator's $250k-without-a-pitch deep dive is a known failure mode. The format is so high-trust that viewers who finish ARE buyers, and not pitching at the end leaves money on the table. **Aggressive close at the end is recommended.**

## The structure

```
INTRO → PROOF (~30 seconds, concentrated) → PARABLE → PRINCIPLE (steps with proof per step) → END (with strong CTA)
```

Deep Dive is the only format where **PROOF is a distinct block** that comes right after the intro. The reason: nobody sits through 60 minutes without first seeing evidence the time is worth spending.

### Proof woven throughout

In addition to the upfront proof block, the ideal pattern is: step 1, then proof step 1 worked, then step 2, then proof step 2 worked. Each step transition can carry a client win or before-and-after. Example: "Step five enabled Jerry to go from 25 pounds overweight to winning a bikini modeling competition."

### Parable: Old Way / New Way

Deep Dives commonly use Old Way / New Way as the parable:

1. Discredit the status quo with conviction ("Search doesn't work. It's done.")
2. Prove the new way works (testimonials, data, transformation)
3. Tease the result ("Look what happens when you do it my way")
4. Then the steps

## Intro adaptation

Deep Dive runs the **fullest version of the universal 6-part intro.** The format earns the longer setup because viewers are committing to a long video. They need to know what they're in for. These are defaults. The creator can flex.

| Universal intro step | Deep Dive default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Top 3 viewer questions | Same. For 45+ minute videos, set up **5 things** instead of 3 to raise tension high enough to justify the length | Short videos can promise 3. Long videos need more anticipation to keep the viewer through. |
| 2. Hook | Statement, Contrarian, or Question all common. Fact works if the data is striking | Strong, conviction-led hooks work because the format demands authority. Credibility Hook works if the creator's results are unmistakable. |
| 3. Problem/Result | Combine Both. Almost always | The format is long. The viewer needs both pain (why this matters) AND result (why staying is worth it). Pure poke or pure tease usually leaves one half thin. |
| 4. Setup | 3 things for normal length, 5 things for 45+ min | The setup IS the contract. What the video promises to deliver. Long videos need a fuller contract to earn the time investment. |
| 5. Transition | Direct, often bridging to the proof block: "But before I show you any of this, let me prove it works." | The transition often goes to the proof section first, not directly to step 1. |
| 6. Credibility line | Woven heavily. Often appears in Hook AND Setup | Long-form requires authority. The creator can lean on credibility more than other formats without it reading as bolted-on. |

**Typical length: 30-60 seconds.** Longer than other formats. The format earns the time.

## The 11-step planner

1. **Set goal.** Sales (established creators) or Views (small or new channels) or Emails.
2. **Thumbnails and title.** Lock the package first. Promise must match what the deep dive delivers. Overpromising tanks finish-rate.
3. **Viewer's top 3 questions** (or 5 for 45+ min). Based on title and thumbnail.
4. **Brain dump.** List EVERY step in the system. For every step or claim, find proof that it works.
5. **Refine.** Delete steps that aren't critical to the viewer's outcome. Apply the **highest-leverage filter**: of 100 possible steps, identify the 20 easiest-to-action that produce the biggest result. More overwhelms.
6. **Plan the proof section.** 30 seconds of concentrated proof right after the intro. Clients, before and afters, testimonials.
7. **Plan the parable.** Often Old Way / New Way for deep dives.
8. **Plan principle steps.** For each step: write the step, identify the proof, decide if a story, metaphor, or visual demo would help. Don't assume parables only go at the start.
9. **Conversion strategy.** Strong CTA at the end. Consider stealth CTAs woven through.
10. **Write.** Full word-for-word script. Presenting from bullets is **not recommended**. Film time is exhausting and the result is uneven.
11. **Upload and track.**

## Length thinking

- **30 minutes:** minimum for the format
- **45 minutes:** common sweet spot
- **60-90 minutes:** common for definitive treatments
- **2+ hours:** possible (a creator's 2-hour video at 13M views proves the ceiling exists), but only with truly comprehensive material

## Common mistakes

- Filming from bullets. Produces uneven pacing and bloats film time
- General-claim titles ("Do this, it'll change your life"). Needs specificity
- No proof block. Viewers leave before reaching the value
- No CTA at end. Viewers who finish are buyers. Leaving them without a path is a missed conversion
- Overwhelm. 100 steps when 20 produce 80% of results
- No parable. Pure information dump kills retention
- Skipping the highest-leverage filter. Including everything because "it's a deep dive" guarantees the audience can't action any of it

## Output format

Word-for-word script. Talking-head can work. Light cinematic enhancements are fine if production matches thumbnail aesthetic. Avoid heavy documentary production. Viewers came for substance, not visuals.

## Conversion strategy by goal

### Goal = Sales (recommended for established creators)

- Aggressive CTA at the end. The viewer who finished is the buyer.
- Stealth CTAs woven through (mention the offer naturally as it ties to a step)
- Sales page link in description (top)
- End screen → another deep dive or high-converting sales video

### Goal = Views (recommended for small or new channels)

- NO external links in description (kills algorithm recommendations)
- End screen → another deep dive (binge-drive) or short process for variety
- Focus on retention through the whole length

### Goal = Emails

- Lead magnet directly tied to the system taught in the deep dive
- Mention 3x: early (just after proof), middle (after a strong step), end (final CTA)
- Lead magnet should be the "shortcut version" of the deep dive

## Title formulas

- "The EXACT [System/Method] To [Specific Outcome]"
- "How To [Specific Outcome] (Complete Guide)"
- "Everything You Need To Know About [Topic]"
- "The [Number]-Step System That [Result]"
- "[Specific Result] In [Timeframe]. Full Walkthrough"

Avoid: vague claim titles, single-word category labels, "I'll show you" without specifics.

## Thumbnail strategies for this format

Lean into: **Result** (front-load the receipt: the dashboard, the income figure), **Cognitive Dissonance** (when the deep dive proves a counterintuitive systematic claim), **Before/After Comparison** (when the system produces a measurable transformation).

Avoid: **Curiosity** alone (deep dives need to show something specific or viewers won't commit to length), **Minimal** (the format reads as low-effort if the thumbnail is too sparse).

## End screen routing

- **Sales goal** → past high-converting sales video (or another deep dive if it converts)
- **Views goal** → another deep dive or a short process (drives binge or variety)
- **Emails goal** → past high-converting email video

## Strategy for compounding results

- Pin the deep dive to the channel. It's the single best evergreen asset
- Reference it from future videos ("I cover this fully in [link]")
- Use it as the channel trailer for new viewers if no formal trailer exists
- Update annually if the methodology evolves

## What to skip

- Don't film from bullets
- Don't include every step. Apply the highest-leverage filter ruthlessly
- Don't end without a CTA (buyers are at the end)
- Don't overpromise in the package
