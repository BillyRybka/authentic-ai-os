# Short Process Format Planner

A 10-20 minute video that lays out a specific quick-fire process to get a specific result.

## When to use this format

- **Viewer type:** Time-strapped buyers who already know their problem and are actively looking for solutions. People who want fast action, not ideas.
- **Goals:** Views, Sales, OR Emails (pick ONE per video, do not mix).
- **Creator level:** All levels (beginner, intermediate, advanced).
- **Best used for:** presenting a clear system or workflow (editing workflow, morning routine, funnel setup), teaching strategy or execution that doesn't require deep theory, when the transformation is simple but valuable.
- **Format scoring:** Views 4/5, Sales 4/5, Trust 4/5

### Why scores aren't universal

Format scores reflect patterns observed across creator channels. Your results depend on your audience. An Interview video can get 350,000 views and produce 1 sale. The view count alone is not the point. Format choice is avatar-specific.

## The structure

```
INTRO → PARABLE (one) → PRINCIPLE (sequential steps, max 8) → END
```

This is the critical distinction. It's **NOT** parable-principle-parable-principle like a Listicle. You have ONE big emotional bit up front, then the rest is the step-by-step process.

### Optional: a second parable before a hard step

If one step in the principle is complex or viewers won't believe it, insert a second parable before that step ONLY. Not every video needs this. You might only need to do it once, or not at all. Video by video.

## Intro adaptation

Short Process uses a compressed intro. The whole format is "fast action steps." A long intro contradicts the format's identity. These are defaults. The creator can break them if their style or audience reward something different.

| Universal intro step | Short Process default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Top 3 viewer questions | Same. Derive from title and thumbnail | Same as every format. |
| 2. Hook | Question, Contrarian, or Statement most common. Fact Hook works if there's a real surprising data point | These land fast and match the action energy. Credibility Hook works if the creator's credibility is loud enough to stop cold viewers. That's a creator-specific call, not a hard rule. On small channels it usually under-delivers because cold viewers don't trust an unknown "I" yet, but if the creator wants to lead with credibility anyway, do it. |
| 3. Problem/Result | Tease the Result most often, Combine also strong | The format is action-oriented. Viewers want to see the receipt up front. Pure Poke can work for emotional topics but usually leaves the energy too negative for action. |
| 4. Setup | One line: "In this video I'm going to show you exactly how to [outcome] in [timeframe]" | The format itself IS the list (the steps), so an upfront 3-item setup duplicates structure. A longer setup is fine if the methodology genuinely needs framing. |
| 5. Transition | Direct: "Step one is..." or "Here's how it works." | Hook forward into step 1. |
| 6. Credibility line | Often woven into the Tease the Result section | The result IS the credibility most of the time. A separate credibility line works if the creator's broader credibility is the differentiator. |

**Typical length: 10-15 seconds.** Faster than most formats (only News is faster). The format's identity is "fast action." A 30-second intro contradicts that. A creator who wants longer can do it, just understand they're trading format-fit for depth.

## The 10-step planner

Use this to plan a short process video before writing a single line of script.

1. **Pick one goal.** Sales, emails, or views. Just pick one.
2. **Thumbnails and title.** Decide the package before the script, since it sets the promise.
3. **Viewer's top 3 questions.** Based on the title and thumbnail, what will the viewer expect answers to? List 3.
4. **Brain dump.** List 10-20 rough ideas, insights, or steps. Don't worry about order yet. Make sure they'll match or exceed the expectations set in the thumbnail and title. Use research on other videos on this topic to level up the plan.
5. **Refine.** Keep only what directly pays off the 3 viewer questions.
6. **Plan the parable.** Pick ONE: Visual Demo, Contrast (Old Way vs New Way), Story, or Metaphor.
7. **Plan principle steps.** Max 8 steps. If 10 steps exist but 5 deliver 80% of the result, give the 5.
8. **Conversion strategy.** Matches the goal picked in Step 1. Details below.
9. **Write.** Word-for-word script.
10. **Upload and track.** Into the measurement system.

## Parable Decision Matrix

Pick the parable type based on the problem:

- **Is the problem invisible or hard to see?** → Visual Demo. Show it on screen.
- **Is the problem abstract or hard to explain?** → Metaphor. Use a familiar frame.
- **Is the problem "wrong way vs right way"?** → Contrast (Old Way / New Way).
- **Is the problem visible but viewers don't feel the stakes?** → Story. Make them feel it.

## The secret sauce

- Give fast action steps
- Give just enough to action
- Show proof it works
- Give real examples

## Common mistakes

- Overwhelming viewers with too much information
- Making it feel hard, complex, or scary
- No call to action
- Generic obvious information
- Making the information feel old or already known

## Examples

### Example: "Copy This Video Style," Old Way vs New Way parable

Parable script:
> "The old way was stock footage, advanced animation, tons of broll, lots of cuts, music, sound effects, templates, zooms... But me and my editor have been testing this for over 12 months now and when we cut it all out and came up with a new style, there was an instant shift in results. More comments, more leads, sales and conversions."

Why it works: Old way (concrete list of wrong pattern), then test period (credibility), then new way (result). Sets up the whole video in one paragraph.

### Example: The Cake Analogy, scope definition

> "A short process would really be: I'm gonna show you how to get just the sponge of every cake amazingly fluffy. Instead of: I'm gonna show you how to go from the beginning, buying all the stuff, the recipe, to putting the icing on the end."

Why it works: Short process is NARROW. One specific result, not a full system. Deep Dives are the whole cake. Short process is the sponge.

## Rules

- **One parable up front.** Not one per step. The emotion sets the stakes, then the principle delivers.
- **Max 8 steps.** Format is meant to be short. If it requires ten steps but five of them get eighty percent of the results, just give those five.
- **Don't be cute with the promise.** Make it clear they're gonna get a result they care about.
- **Transition between steps hooks forward.** Tell the viewer why the next step matters before giving it.
- **One goal per video.** Emails, Sales, or Views. Never two.
- **Lead magnet must relate to the content.** If step 3 is about a checklist, give them the checklist.

## Output format

Word-for-word script. Short Process is scripted end to end, teleprompter-read. Not bullets.

## Conversion strategy by goal

### Goal = Emails
- Mention the lead magnet 3 times in the video.
- Lead magnet MUST relate directly to the video content.
- Top link in description plus pinned comment.
- End screen: push to a past high-converting email video.

### Goal = Sales
- Obvious selling (direct pitch) OR stealth selling (demonstrate product in content).
- CTA around 90 seconds, middle, end.
- Sales page link in description.
- End screen: push to a past high-converting sales video.

### Goal = Views
- NO external links in description (kills recommendations).
- End screen: push to the previous version of this same template (the video this one is iterating on).
- Focus entirely on retention and completion.

## Title formulas

- "How to X"
- "How to X in Y"
- "How I X step by step"
- "X was hard, until I did this"
- "Copy this X, It'll Y"

## Thumbnail strategies for this format

Stick to: **Result**, **Cognitive Dissonance**, **Comparison**, or **Curiosity**. The thumbnail should make viewers think "what, how, or why."

## Strategy for compounding results

- Produce a simple 5-6 step system to get a specific result
- Pitch a lead magnet tailored to the video, or pitch an offer
- Once the video has proven conversion, point future videos back to it in end screens

## End screen routing

- **Views goal** → Previous version of same template (the one this iterates on)
- **Sales goal** → Past high-converting sales video
- **Emails goal** → Past high-converting email video

## What to skip

- Don't pad steps. Quality over quantity.
- Don't alternate parable and principle like a listicle. This is one parable then principle steps.
- Don't chase multiple goals. One per video.
- Don't tie the lead magnet to something outside the video content.
