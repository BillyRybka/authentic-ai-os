# Format Rotation Guide

The Rule of 3+1: rotate 3 core formats, then use every 4th video as an experiment. If the experiment wins across repeated tests, promote it into the core 3 and retire the weakest format.

This guide helps pick starting defaults. It does not prove what will work for every creator. Own channel data beats competitor data. Competitor and adjacent-channel data beat guesses. Source-backed defaults are useful when a creator is new, but they should be marked as lower confidence until real performance data exists.

## What Stage 4 Should Save

For each core format and the experimental slot, save:

- **Format:** one of the seven formats below
- **Why this is a good first test:** plain reason tied to the avatar, proof, and creator capability
- **Evidence basis:** creator judgment / source-backed default / own channel data / pattern-bank research
- **Confidence:** low / medium / high / experimental
- **Watch for:** what would tell us to keep, adjust, or drop it

Do not save a format just because the creator likes making it. Save it because the viewer is likely to open it, the creator can deliver it, and the format fits the business goal.

## Evidence Hierarchy

1. **Own data:** the creator's past winners, outliers, comments, buyer feedback, email replies, and sales signals.
2. **Pattern-bank research:** niche and adjacent-channel formats that worked, generalized into structure rather than copied.
3. **Creator judgment:** what the creator knows their audience asks for, buys, complains about, or shares.
4. **Source-backed defaults:** useful when there is no data yet, but marked low or medium confidence.

Competitor research should generalize the pattern:

- format type
- viewer promise
- proof style
- packaging pattern
- audience sophistication
- why it likely worked

It should not copy another creator's wording, claims, personality, or private framework.

## The Seven Formats

Each format has a different structure, creator-level requirement, and scoring profile. The scores are starting signals from source material, not universal truth.

### 1. Short Process (10-20 min)
A quick process to get a specific result.

- Structure: intro, one emotional setup, sequential steps, end
- Level: all creators
- Scores: Views 4/5, Sales 4/5, Trust 4/5
- Strong fit: the avatar wants a clear step-by-step path and does not want theory
- Weak fit: the topic needs deep proof, a transformation story, or complex context
- Best for: workflows, setup processes, routines, execution videos

### 2. Case Study (10-30 min)
One relatable transformation taught through Problem, Action, Outcome.

- Structure: intro, PAO story, one big lesson, 1-3 actionable steps, end
- Level: creators with proven transformation, client wins, or personal results
- Scores: Views 5/5, Sales 5/5, Trust 5/5
- Strong fit: the creator has proof and the avatar needs to believe the method works
- Weak fit: the creator has no real result, no clear story, or the result is not surprising
- Best for: selling systems, proving methodology, showing transformation

### 3. Roast / Review (10-20 min)
Review a public example or audience submission, name the problem, show the fix.

- Structure: intro, review 1, review 2, review 3, end
- Level: creators with visible work to critique and enough examples or submissions
- Scores: Views 2/5, Sales 5/5, Trust 5/5
- Strong fit: the niche has visible outputs like copy, design, thumbnails, funnels, websites, offers, profiles, or performances
- Weak fit: there is nothing visual or concrete to review
- Best for: critique-based education and showing expertise in action

### 4. Deep Dive (45+ min)
A complete system or framework taught end to end with proof at every step.

- Structure: intro, short proof, emotional setup, detailed logic steps with proof, end
- Level: advanced creators, usually after several videos are already working
- Scores: Views 4/5, Sales 5/5, Trust 5/5
- Strong fit: high-ticket offer, deep trust needed, creator has proof and teaching depth
- Weak fit: new channel with cold viewers who do not yet trust long videos from the creator
- Best for: authority, flagship pieces, high-intent buyers

### 5. Interview (30-60 min)
An expert conversation with a guest who has credibility the audience cares about.

- Structure: intro with credibility, planned questions, creator commentary, end
- Level: any level, but guest fit matters
- Scores: Views 4/5, Sales 1/5, Trust 2/5
- Strong fit: the goal is reach, list growth, or borrowed authority
- Weak fit: the goal is direct sales or building the creator's own authority
- Best for: expanding audience, not for the core sales engine

### 6. News (5-15 min)
Timely coverage of a new event, update, trend, or novel angle.

- Structure: what happened, why it matters, what viewers should do
- Level: any level, but requires speed and a repeatable monitoring habit
- Scores: Views 5/5, Sales 1/5, Trust 2/5
- Strong fit: the niche has frequent changes and the avatar needs timely interpretation
- Weak fit: the creator cannot move fast, or the channel would become dependent on daily news
- Best for: fast attention, topical relevance, list growth

### 7. Listicle (10-20 min)
A numbered list of points with emotion plus logic per point.

- Structure: intro, repeated lesson blocks, end
- Level: all creators
- Scores: Views 3/5, Sales 3/5, Trust 3/5
- Strong fit: new creators, new topics, or audiences that want quick wins
- Weak fit: high-intent buyers who need proof before buying
- Best for: accessible starting videos, batchable lessons, broad entry points

## Format Fit Checklist

Before saving the starting rotation, check four things:

1. **Avatar appetite:** would this person naturally click this type of video?
2. **Creator capability:** can the creator make this consistently without breaking the workflow?
3. **Proof and raw material:** does the creator have the stories, examples, results, or submissions the format requires?
4. **Business goal:** does the format match the goal, such as sales, email list growth, views, trust, or authority?

If a format fails two or more checks, do not make it core. Put it in the experiment slot only if there is a clear reason to test it.

## New Channel Mode

When the creator has no useful channel data, pick first defaults from avatar fit, proof, production ability, and source-backed fit rules.

Good starting choices often include:

- Short Process, because it is simple, repeatable, and useful to many audiences
- Listicle, because it is accessible and good for early testing
- Case Study, only if the creator has real proof and can tell the story clearly

Be careful with:

- Deep Dive as a first core format, because cold viewers may not trust a long video yet
- Roast without a submissions or public-example pipeline
- Interview as a sales format, because views may come from the guest rather than the creator
- News as the whole channel, because it can train viewers to come for updates instead of the creator's core expertise

## Existing Channel Mode

When the creator has published videos:

1. Use the creator's own-channel format data first (which of their own videos hit, and in what format).
2. Cross-check against `packaging-bank/` past winners if any exist.
3. Otherwise pick from this menu using the avatar + the 4-check filter below. Format is a menu pick, not a competitor-mined pattern (a competitor's format can't be classified from title + thumbnail without transcripts).
4. Mark temporary defaults lower confidence until research or post-publish measurement confirms them.

Do not throw away what already works because a default says another format is stronger. Repeat what works until the data says it is tired.

## Strong / Plain / Bad Rotation Examples

These are examples from other industries so the skill does not overfit to one creator's business.

### Example 1: Real Estate Advisor For First-Time Investors

**Strong starting rotation**

- Core 1: Short Process. First-time investors need step-by-step decisions like how to estimate rent, evaluate a neighborhood, or spot a bad deal.
- Core 2: Case Study. A real purchase, failed deal, or client win proves the advice is not theory.
- Core 3: Listicle. Lists like "5 red flags before you buy" are accessible for cold viewers.
- Experimental: News. Test timely market updates only if the advisor can publish fast and tie the news back to buying decisions.

Why it works: the mix gives beginner-friendly help, proof, and one timely attention test.

**Plain but usable**

- Short Process, Listicle, Case Study, experimental News.

Why it is weaker: the choices are reasonable, but the saved file does not explain what evidence or audience behavior supports them.

**Bad**

- Deep Dive, Interview, News, experimental Roast.

Why it fails: it asks a cold audience to trust long videos, borrows authority instead of building the creator's, leans too much on timely updates, and uses Roast without a clear visible-work pipeline.

### Example 2: Leadership Consultant For New Managers

**Strong starting rotation**

- Core 1: Short Process. New managers need scripts, meeting systems, and simple decision rules.
- Core 2: Case Study. A manager conflict or turnaround story builds trust and shows judgment.
- Core 3: Listicle. Mistakes, phrases, or habits are easy entry points for cold viewers.
- Experimental: Interview. Test one guest only if the guest is known to this audience and the creator adds expert commentary.

Why it works: the rotation teaches usable behavior while still proving the consultant's judgment.

**Plain but usable**

- Short Process, Case Study, Listicle, experimental Interview.

Why it is weaker: it gives the formats but not the reason each one fits new managers.

**Bad**

- Interview, News, Deep Dive, experimental News.

Why it fails: too much attention goes to guests and updates, while the creator's own authority and repeatable teaching stay underbuilt.

### Example 3: Nutrition Coach For Busy Parents

**Strong starting rotation**

- Core 1: Short Process. Busy parents need simple shopping, prep, and meal routines.
- Core 2: Listicle. Lists of swaps, mistakes, and quick meals are easy to open.
- Core 3: Case Study. A real parent transformation makes the promise believable.
- Experimental: Roast / Review. Test lunchbox, grocery cart, or meal-plan reviews if examples are available.

Why it works: the formats match low-time viewers and keep proof close to the offer.

**Plain but usable**

- Short Process, Listicle, Case Study, experimental Roast.

Why it is weaker: it does not say whether the coach has real case studies or review examples.

**Bad**

- Deep Dive, Interview, News, experimental Case Study.

Why it fails: deep lessons and expert interviews may be too much for busy parents. News is usually not the core reason this viewer follows a nutrition coach.

### Example 4: Website Designer For Service Businesses

**Strong starting rotation**

- Core 1: Roast / Review. Website and offer pages are visible, so critique shows expertise fast.
- Core 2: Short Process. Simple fixes like homepage above-the-fold, contact page, or service page structure are easy to use.
- Core 3: Case Study. Before-and-after client work proves the method.
- Experimental: Deep Dive. Test a longer teardown only after the shorter reviews prove demand.

Why it works: the creator can show the work, name the problem, and show the fix.

**Plain but usable**

- Roast, Short Process, Case Study, experimental Deep Dive.

Why it is weaker: it does not explain the evidence needed, like examples, permission, or strong before-and-after proof.

**Bad**

- News, Interview, Listicle, experimental News.

Why it fails: those formats underuse the visible proof that makes this niche easy to trust.

## Format Rejection Rules

Reject or downgrade these unless the creator has a clear reason:

- **Deep Dive as core for a new channel:** use only if the creator already has trust, strong proof, and a topic worth long attention.
- **Case Study without proof:** if there is no real outcome, it becomes a story-shaped opinion piece.
- **Roast without examples:** no submissions, public examples, or visible work means there is nothing useful to review.
- **Interview as the main sales format:** it may get views, but it often builds the guest more than the creator.
- **News as the whole channel:** it can grow attention while weakening evergreen authority.
- **Listicle for every video:** easy to make, but can become shallow if it never leads to proof or deeper teaching.
- **Short Process without a specific result:** "how to improve" is too vague. The process needs a defined outcome.

## Promotion And Retirement Rules

Promote an experimental into the core 3 when:

- it beats the channel baseline for 2-3 videos in that format
- the goal metric also improves, such as sales, email signups, or qualified comments
- the creator can produce it repeatedly without the workflow breaking

Retire the weakest core format when:

- it repeatedly underperforms the channel baseline
- it attracts the wrong viewer
- it takes too much effort for the result
- it no longer fits the creator's offer or positioning

Retired does not mean banned. It means the format is no longer scheduled as a core default.

## Common Mistakes

- Picking 3 formats the creator likes instead of 3 the avatar responds to
- Treating source-backed scores as universal laws
- Using competitor data as permission to copy instead of generalizing the structure
- Starting a new channel with too many advanced or production-heavy formats
- Promoting an experiment because of one lucky video
- Never running the experimental slot
- Saving defaults without evidence, confidence, and what to watch for
