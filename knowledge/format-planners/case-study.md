# Case Study Format Planner

A 10-15 minute video that tells one client's (or the creator's own) transformation story: Problem, Action, Outcome. With a single big lesson and 1-3 actionable steps for the viewer at the end.

## When to use this format

- **Viewer type:** Buyers actively considering whether the creator's solution works. Want proof, not theory. Want to see someone like them succeed.
- **Goals:** Sales (recommended), Emails, OR Views. Pick ONE. **Sales is the priority for this format.**
- **Creator level:** Intermediate to advanced. Requires a real proven transformation. Not for beginners with no track record.
- **Best used for:** building belief in a methodology, converting cold viewers into leads or customers, and showing real results that abstract teaching can't.
- **Format scoring:** Views 5/5, Sales 5/5, Trust 5/5

### The two gates

The score is straight fives, and the cost of that is the entry requirement. This is the one format the source explicitly says beginners should not attempt, and it fails on two specific things rather than on execution quality.

**It needs storytelling.** Getting good at it takes years, so this is not a first-format pick. **It needs a result that surprises.** A predictable arc where the ending lands at "meh" does not work; the transformation has to carry a twist. Miss either gate and the fives do not show up, which is why a case study that flops usually flopped at selection, not at the edit.

Viewers who watch a case study and stay are warm. They have seen proof and want to know if they can replicate it, so the end-screen routing should capitalize on that.

## The structure

```
INTRO → PARABLE (story: Problem-Action-Outcome) → PRINCIPLE (one big lesson + 1-3 steps) → END
```

The parable IS the story. Problem-Action-Outcome spans the intro setup AND the parable. The principle is the takeaway: the ONE thing the viewer should remember and the 1-3 things they can do.

**Critical:** the case study must focus on ONE clear transformation. If the story tries to cover everything, it gets too complex and viewers drop. Pick the single biggest thing.

## Intro adaptation

Case Study INVERTS the universal intro template. Lead with the receipt (the outcome), then the problem, then the setup. These are defaults that pattern-match what works. The creator can break them if their story arc reads better in a different shape.

| Universal intro step | Case Study default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Top 3 viewer questions | Same. Derive from title and thumbnail | Same as every format. |
| 2. Hook | Lead with the OUTCOME or the stakes. "Six months ago Steve hadn't landed a single client. Now he's doing $80k a month." OR "Steve's wife was pregnant, he'd lost his job, and his business was making zero." | The implied question is "how?" That's what pulls viewers through. Question and Statement Hooks both work. Credibility Hook works if the result IS the credibility (a large client transformation IS a credibility statement). Other hook types are fine if the creator finds an angle that fits. |
| 3. Problem/Result | Combine Both works most often | The case study LIVES on poke-then-tease. Problem equals where they started, result equals where they ended. The arc IS the video. Pure Tease can work if the result is shocking enough on its own. Pure Poke usually feels heavy without the relief. |
| 4. Setup | Tight: "In this video I'm going to show you exactly what we did and the one big lesson you can apply yourself" | The 3 viewer questions get answered THROUGH the story, not via an upfront list. A longer setup can work if the methodology has multiple distinct components viewers need to track. |
| 5. Transition | "So let me take you back to where this started" or "So here's what happened" | Orientation cue plus hook forward into the narrative. |
| 6. Credibility line | Often built INTO the outcome itself ("Took us 9 weeks." "Hit $1M ARR.") | The numbers ARE the credibility. Usually no separate credibility line is needed. If the creator's broader credibility helps frame the story (they've coached 200+ clients, this is one of them), weaving it in can land. |

**Tonal note:** failure is acceptable, even valuable. If the first three months went backwards before forwards, say so. Honest narrative builds trust. Polished testimonial-style usually doesn't.

## The 8-step planner

1. **Pick one goal.** Sales (recommended), emails, or views.
2. **Thumbnails and title.** Lead the package with the result or transformation. The title hints at the journey, the thumbnail shows the receipt.
3. **Viewer's top 3 questions.** Based on title and thumbnail. For case studies, common questions: "What did they actually do?", "Could I replicate this?", "How long did it take?"
4. **Plan the parable (the story).** Map out Problem, Action, Outcome. Answer the 5 questions in section below.
5. **Plan the principle.** Identify ONE big lesson the viewer should walk away with. Then list 1-3 specific steps they can take.
6. **Conversion strategy.** Match the goal picked in step 1.
7. **Write.** Word-for-word script, talking head style. Avoid heavy cinematic production.
8. **Upload and track.**

## The 5 questions every case study answers

Inside the parable, the story must answer all 5:

1. **What was their problem?** Specific. Not "they were stuck," but "they had 0 clients in 3 months and rent was due."
2. **Why did they need to fix it?** The stakes. Wife pregnant, lost job, runway running out. Without stakes, the viewer doesn't care.
3. **What actions did they take?** Including what didn't work first. ONLY actions that led to the outcome. No side missions.
4. **What was the outcome?** Specific number, specific timeframe.
5. **Where is the proof?** Screenshots, metrics, testimonial clip, before and after.

If any of the 5 are missing, the case study has a hole. Story-driven content survives or dies on completeness here.

## Story rules

- **Failures are gold, not weaknesses.** "We tried X first and it bombed" makes the creator more trustworthy, not less. Honesty is the differentiator.
- **Match the avatar's stage.** A case study about taking someone from $250k to $1M/month does NOT resonate with viewers making $10k/month. The relatable story must match where the buyer is RIGHT NOW.
- **Only include actions that drove the outcome.** Side missions break the story. If they tried 7 things and 3 worked, talk about the 3.
- **One transformation per video.** Don't combine "got their first client AND scaled to $1M AND hired their team" into one story. Pick one.
- **Stakes raise emotion. Numbers build trust.** Both required. Stakes alone equals melodrama. Numbers alone equals boring case study.

## Client interview footage rule

Most clients are not media-trained. They ramble. Use client footage SPARINGLY:

- **Tell the story yourself** as narration
- **Cut to the client for ONE confirming line** at the moment of impact
- Example flow: "The next thing we did was [X]. That had the biggest impact." [CUT TO CLIENT: "I couldn't believe how fast it worked."]

Long client monologues kill pacing. The narration carries the story, the client confirms.

## Principle (the takeaway)

After the story ends, deliver:

1. **The ONE big lesson.** State it plainly. "What this proves: [insight]."
2. **1-3 specific steps the viewer can take themselves.** Not 7. Keep it actionable.
3. **Show proof on each step.** Screenshot, before and after, testimonial. Anything visual.

This is the moment the viewer converts from "wow, that's cool" to "wait, I could do this too." Don't waffle here.

## Common mistakes

- Hero worship. Making the case study about how amazing the client is, not how the methodology produced the result
- No stakes. "Steve was a freelancer who did better" doesn't pull anyone through 12 minutes
- Side missions. Including actions that don't tie to the outcome
- Wrong-stage relatability. A $1M/mo case study aimed at $10k/mo viewers
- Too much client footage. The client rambles, viewers leave
- Cinematic production. Case studies work best as honest talking-head, not documentary

## Output format

Word-for-word script. Talking-head style. Avoid heavy cinematic edits. Authenticity over production value.

## Conversion strategy by goal

### Goal = Sales (recommended)

- Direct CTA at end ("If you want this for yourself, here's the link") OR stealth CTA woven through ("That's exactly what we do in [program]")
- Sales page link in description (top)
- End screen → past high-converting sales video
- Mid-video: when the action section names the methodology, optionally tie it to the offer

### Goal = Emails

- Lead magnet tied to the case study's challenge: "the exact checklist [client] used to do this" or "the 7-day system from this case study"
- Mention 3 times: early, middle, end
- Top link: lead magnet
- End screen → past high-converting email video

### Goal = Views

- NO external links in description (kills recommendations)
- End screen → another similar case study or related deep dive (drives binge)
- Focus entirely on retention through the story arc

## Title formulas

- "How [Person] [Did Specific Outcome] In [Timeframe]"
- "We Took [Person] From [State A] to [State B] In [Timeframe]"
- "Why [Person]'s [Approach] Worked When Everything Else Failed"
- "The [System/Method] That Got [Person] [Outcome]"
- "[Specific Number] In [Timeframe]. Here's How We Did It"

## Thumbnail strategies for this format

Lean into: **Result** (show the receipt: the after photo, the dashboard, the income figure), **Before/After Comparison** (split the visual: where they were vs where they ended), **Cognitive Dissonance** if the case proves a counterintuitive lesson.

Avoid: pure Curiosity (case studies need to show something specific or viewers don't trust the proof) and Social Hacking (the case study itself is the credibility).

## Strategy for compounding results

- Build a small library of case studies covering the same transformation at different stages
- Pin the strongest one to the channel
- Reference them in deep dives ("we covered this in detail in [case study link]")
- Email nurture: case studies belong in the welcome sequence. They convert cold subscribers into warm leads

## End screen routing

- **Sales goal** → past high-converting sales video (or another case study if the next-best video is one)
- **Emails goal** → past high-converting email video
- **Views goal** → another case study with similar transformation arc

## What to skip

- Don't story-tell about people the viewer can't relate to (stage match required)
- Don't ramble on client interview clips
- Don't bury the result. Viewers need to see the outcome promised early
- Don't pretend everything went smoothly. Failure mid-arc builds trust
