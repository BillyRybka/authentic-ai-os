# BENS Framework

A copywriting system for titles, thumbnails, hooks, and CTAs. Every strong title triggers at least one of these emotional responses. One or two strong letters is enough. You don't need all four.

## The four letters

**B — Big.** A bold claim or dramatic stakes. Makes the viewer think "wait, that's a lot."

**E — Easy.** Makes the result feel achievable. Signals a shortcut, a simple method, a defined number of steps.

**N — New.** Makes the information feel fresh. Either genuinely new or repackaged to feel novel. Frustrated viewers who have tried everything respond most to New.

**S — Safe.** Builds trust and credibility. Specific numbers, known sources, or a credible person attached.

## Examples

### "How to get SO rich you question the meaning of making money"

- **B:** "SO rich" is massive. "Question the meaning of making money" is the biggest claim possible.
- **N:** Nobody frames wealth this way. Feels genuinely new.
- **S:** The claim is so big it signals expertise.

Primary drivers: B + N

### "I read 300 self-improvement books to learn these 3 lessons"

- **B:** 300 books is a big claim.
- **E:** "3 lessons" is digestible. 350 lessons would scare viewers off.
- **N:** Most haven't read 300 books. Lessons feel hard-earned.
- **S:** "I did the thing" signals the value is real.

Primary drivers: B + E

### "The Silent Hack YouTubers Use To Hook You"

- **B:** YouTubers are doing something specific to hook you.
- **E:** "Hack" signals shortcut.
- **N:** Doesn't name the hack. Keeps it mysterious.
- **S:** Attributed to known creators (borrowed credibility).

Primary drivers: E + N + S

### "The 15-Second Rule That Makes Procrastination Impossible"

- **B:** "Procrastination impossible" is a massive claim.
- **E:** 15 seconds is tiny.
- **N:** Named "rule" makes it feel novel and defined.
- **S:** The rule structure signals rigor.

Primary drivers: B + E + N

## N is the most powerful letter

For audiences who have tried every obvious solution and are frustrated, New is the strongest driver. A frustrated viewer clicks "The New Way To [result]" over "How To [result]" every time.

**The info doesn't need to be genuinely new — it needs to FEEL new.** Repackage familiar ideas through unique stories, metaphors, or naming.

"How to write better emails" becomes:
- "The 3-Line Email Rule That Doubled My Replies" (N via naming + B via result)
- "Why Your Emails Are Dying in the First Sentence" (N via contrarian framing)
- "I Tested 47 Email Subject Lines. Here's What Won." (N via volume + S via specificity)

## Specificity = S (Safe)

Specific numbers, time frames, ages, distances make claims believable. Specificity signals that real work went in.

- "Make $14,332" beats "Make money"
- "400 Sq. Ft." beats "Small Space"
- "23:07 → 19:42" beats "Got faster"
- "29 DAYS" beats "Quickly"

The number doesn't need to be big. It needs to be precise.

## Naming systems, rules, or methods

Named methods signal N (novel, defined) and E (process exists, you just follow it).

- "The 90-Minute Rule"
- "The Trident Method"
- "The 15-Second Rule"
- "The BENS System"

A named method also signals proprietary authority. The creator didn't just observe a pattern. They built a system.

## Credibility matching

Some titles only work for well-known creators:
- "79 Minutes of Advice"
- "My Morning Routine"
- "How I Did X" (where I means a famous I)

These won't work for small or unknown channels. Cold viewers don't trust or care about "I" yet.

For small channels, build Safe through:
- Specific results ("$14,332 in 90 days")
- Named methods (even if the creator invented the name)
- Borrowed credibility ("What Warren Buffett does differently")
- Data sources ("According to MIT's 2024 study...")

## Title rules

- Keep titles under 50 characters. Platforms truncate beyond that.
- Inject at least one BENS letter into every title. Usually two.
- Avoid colons in titles. Avoid pipe characters `|`.
- Don't keyword-stuff. Platforms read transcripts and every frame.
- AI-generated titles used verbatim almost always fail the read-aloud test. Edit them.

## Common mistakes

- **Lukewarm claims.** "How to Get Better at Writing" has no BENS. "The 3-Sentence Rule That Makes Emails Impossible to Ignore" does.
- **All four letters stuffed in.** The title becomes noisy and breaks readability.
- **Using "I" before you have the credibility to carry it.**
- **Copying big-creator titles without equivalent results behind them.**
- **Keyword stuffing.** Hurts humans AND the algorithm.
