# Worked Example

One session start to finish, then the two situations that break the rhythm. Not lines to paste. Italic notes are for whoever maintains the skill.

## A full session

**The dump.** An autocorrect for AI writing. Claude keeps using the same recognisable AI words and punctuation even after being corrected. The fix is a check that runs on the file the AI just wrote, loaded with the creator's own banned words. There is an MCP server version for people not in Claude Code. No numbers.

### Stage 1, internal

Already tried: the rule in the prompt, in the chat, in project instructions, in a memory file. Four attempts, same shape, all failing the same way.

Only this creator has: months of catching the same tells, a real banned-word list built from his own corrections, and the read that this is enforcement rather than prompting.

*Four failed attempts of identical shape is the most useful thing in the dump. It is what makes a why-does-this-keep-failing direction findable, and none of it would surface from naming the topic.*

### Stage 1, the message

> Mechanism: the checker plus your banned-word list. Delivery rather than the draw, because nobody in your audience is searching for a linter, so it stays out of every framing and shows up in the body once they already care.
>
> Two things. Before you built this, what had you already tried to make it stop? And do you have an angle in mind already?

**Creator:** "Everything. Custom instructions, project instructions, told it in the chat about fifty times, memory file. Behaves for two messages and the em dash is back. I was just going to walk through the setup."

### Stage 2, the core desire

The climb, run with his answer in hand: the AI remembers the rules, so he stops re-explaining the same correction, so he has a co-writer instead of a first draft to repair, so more of what he writes actually goes out.

> A co-writer that already sounds like him, instead of a first draft he has to repair.

*One rung higher is "AI that is actually usable," which he would not say out loud and which cannot be framed. One rung lower is "the AI remembers," which is the mechanism wearing a want's clothes.*

*"Behaves for two messages and the em dash is back" is what makes the co-writer rung the right one. Run off the dump alone, the climb lands on the mechanism, because the dump is written about the tool.*

**Creator:** "Yeah, that's it."

### Stages 3 and 4, the options

> Four ways this could go. I cut three: an MCP setup angle, because a search answers that in a line, and two that turned out to be the same want as others here.
>
> 1. **The Em Dash**
>    This video shows people who have already told Claude twice how to never see an em dash in a draft again.
>    Core payoff: they can stop the specific tell they hate, permanently rather than for one session.
>
> 2. **Not Mine**
>    For people publishing under their own name. This video shows them how to stop shipping drafts that do not sound like them on the days they were too fried to read closely.
>    Core payoff: they can tell before publishing whether a draft is theirs.
>
> 3. **Why It Forgets**
>    For people who have already written the rules into their instructions. This video explains why the AI stops following them after a few messages.
>    Core payoff: they can tell whether a correction will hold or whether the output needs a different kind of check.
>
> 4. **Stop Proofreading**
>    This video shows people who read every draft line by line how to stop being the one who catches things.
>    Core payoff: they can hand the checking over and spend the time writing instead.
>
> Your walkthrough angle is not on the list, and I should say why. It describes what happens in the video rather than why anyone would watch, so it is a plan rather than a framing. The setup and the live catch still get filmed.

Then, separately:

> I would take 3. It is the only one that explains why the four things you already tried all failed, and that is what makes somebody believe the fix. The other three assume they already accept the problem, and 3 uses the enforcement read, which is the part nobody else is saying.
>
> Pick one, or: more, a different direction, sharper, start again.

*Four rather than a round number, and the cuts named so the creator can argue with a judgment they would otherwise never see.*

*Read the conditions: already told it twice, name on the writing, already written the rules in, reads every draft line by line. Without those, all four collapse into "business owners."*

*Check the wants, not the wording. Kill the specific tell, keep the work mine, understand why it fails, stop checking. Four wants.*

**Creator:** "Three. That's the thing I actually know that nobody says."

*A choice with a reason attached is the signal it worked. Lock it and move.*

### Stage 5, the read

> **Target.** They write with Claude every day and they want content that comes out sounding like them. But Claude keeps using the same AI sounding words, phrases and punctuation they would never use, even after they correct it. So they have quietly become the AI's proofreader, hunting the same handful of words every time. Which means AI can cost them more time than writing it themselves would have.
>
> **Transformation.** They stop writing rules for the AI and give it its own autocorrect instead. Their own words go in a list, and every draft gets checked against that list before it reaches them. So when they catch something new they add it once and never catch it again. Which means they are not the proofreader any more, and the draft that lands in front of them is already clean.
>
> **Stakes.** They keep proofreading, so the time AI was supposed to save never shows up. And because proofreading is boring they stop reading closely, and the ones they miss go out to the audience. Nobody tells them it sounds like AI. They just open less, watch less and trust less, and the drop looks like a content problem, so they change thumbnails and topics when the problem is the words. Eventually they go back to writing everything by hand.

*Target describes the loop and stops on a cost. Stakes take that cost and compound it, landing back on the thing Target ended with. The wrong diagnosis is in there because the material supports it. Without that, Stakes would run three sentences and stop.*

### Stage 6

> Deep-dive, goal emails. Say the word if either is wrong.

*Deep-dive because the framing spends real time discrediting the old way before building anything. Emails because the banned-word list is the obvious opt-in and this audience is warm.*

Saved, second person, one outcome, no opener:

> core_payoff: "You'll be able to tell whether another correction will hold or whether the output needs a different kind of check."

## When the cost will not come

**AI:** "What did you believe about this before?"

**Creator:** "I thought onboarding was the boring part. I put everything into the sales call and handed them a folder. Took me two years to work out that's exactly where I was losing people."

**AI:** "What did that cost you?"

**Creator:** "Lost four clients that year and blamed delivery the whole time. They'd already checked out in week one, I just didn't find out until month three."

> **Stakes.** If they keep running onboarding as admin, the client checks out in week one and nobody hears about it until month three. So they read it as a delivery problem and rebuild delivery. Delivery was fine. Four clients gone that year, every one diagnosed wrong, because the damage happened twelve weeks earlier, in the week they had decided was just admin.

*Two questions, not four. Stop the moment one opens something. Neither answer arrived shaped like a field, and both got written out of what he said.*

*The bill arriving in month three is why nobody traces it to week one, which is the strongest shape a cost can take.*

## When everything comes back flat

**AI:** "There's no real conflict in this one. Nobody's wrong about anything, it's just quicker. That's a reference video rather than a click video. Three ways to play it: narrow to the one part people will get wrong, let a number carry it if you have one, or film it fast and know the ceiling going in."

**Creator:** "It's news, I just want the speed. Film it."

> **Stakes.** Small, honestly. They burn an afternoon finding out, or they skip the one change that would have saved them one.

*A flat set is evidence rather than failure. Name the ceiling once and the creator films with their eyes open. Modest stakes get written as modest, and inflating them into a threat is the exact failure the flat set just warned about.*

## Where sessions go wrong

- **The whole ladder printed instead of one rung.** "So they get X, so they get Y, so they get Z" is the working, not the answer.
- **Two framings with the same opening.** Same shape means the same framing wearing different words, whatever follows it.
- **Several framings, one want.** Every option reads differently and three answer the same want.
- **A framing that describes the contents.** Nobody clicks to find out what happens in the video.
- **A who with no condition.** "Business owners" is a category.
- **Options and no recommendation.** That hands the creator the strategic work they came here to have done.
- **A recommendation made of taste.** "It's the strongest" cannot be checked. Point at the material.
- **A flat pick read as a yes.** "Three is fine" and "three, that's the thing nobody says" are different signals.
- **Building before the pick.** If they choose differently, every word is aimed at the wrong person.
- **A Target made of demographics.** An age is not a situation.
- **A Transformation with nothing in it.** "They go from stressed to confident." Nothing changed but a mood.
- **A payoff with a second thing bolted on.** Pick one.
- **Saving a handle.** vid-title inherits a title written before it looked at anything.
