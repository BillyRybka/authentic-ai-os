# Stake finder

Open this at step 2, before you write the read. It is for your thinking, never pasted into chat.

**This file serves two of the four fields:** Target's blind spot, and Stakes. They are the hardest two to write and the two most often written soft, so open this first rather than drafting them blind. It also carries the dig, for when the dump has no conflict in it at all.

**The one principle: a boring idea is boring because nothing is at stake for the viewer.** The job is not "make this sound interesting." It is: name the thing the viewer stands to lose, or has already lost without noticing. Everything downstream (the argument, the title, the hook) runs on that; if it is not here, there is nothing for them to work with.

## The four shapes a blind spot takes

When the material has a blind spot, it is almost always one of these. Find which one, and it goes in Target as the "they think X, it is actually Y" clause.

**A video does not need one.** A straight tutorial or a news reaction can be worth making with no blind spot at all. Do not manufacture one to fill the slot; leave Target as goal, challenge, and pain point, and let the Stakes carry the weight.

1. **A belief that is costing them.** They are doing the thing they were told to do, and it is the problem. "You don't need a better planner, you need fewer decisions."
2. **A cost they cannot see.** They are fine with it because the bill arrives later. "Your onboarding is why clients ghost at month three."
3. **A thing they feel but cannot name.** They have lived it and have no word for it. Naming it is the whole value.
4. **A scoreboard they are wrong about.** They are optimizing the wrong number. "The video that made me the most money had 4,000 views."

One is enough. Two is usually two videos.

## Digging when Target or Stakes came up thin

Most brain-dumps arrive as accurate information with no conflict in them. That is normal and it is not a dead end. The conflict is usually in the creator's head and did not make it into the dump, because they have held the belief so long they forgot it was ever contested.

Ask one at a time, in flow, in your own words. Stop the moment one opens.

- **What do people get this wrong about?** The most direct route to a belief that costs.
- **What did you believe before?** The creator's old position is the viewer's current one. This one lands most often.
- **What does it cost them to keep doing it the way they do it now?** Route to the invisible bill.
- **Who pushed back on this, and what did they say?** If someone argued, there is a conflict. Find who.

Do not run all four as a battery. That is an interrogation and it makes the creator defensive, which closes exactly the door you are opening.

## The ceiling call

If two rounds of digging surface nothing, say it plainly instead of manufacturing tension. "There's no real conflict in this one. It's a reference video, not a click video." Then give them the three honest options:

- Narrow to the one part that does have a stake, and cut the rest.
- Let a real number carry it, if the material has one.
- Film it anyway as a low-ceiling utility video, knowing that going in.

This is not a kill gate. Framing does not kill videos, measurement does. It is a ceiling named out loud so the creator spends a filming day with their eyes open.

## Near-misses

- **Accurate restatement.** "Document your onboarding" describes the video. Nothing is at stake, nothing is contested. Test it: if the viewer's brain fills in nothing, it is a label.
- **Manufactured contrarianism.** A flip the creator does not actually believe. It reads as a stunt, the body cannot deliver it, and it burns trust faster than a boring title ever would.
- **A stake for the creator, not the viewer.** "Most people build this wrong" is only interesting if the viewer is one of those people and knows it. Check who pays the cost.
