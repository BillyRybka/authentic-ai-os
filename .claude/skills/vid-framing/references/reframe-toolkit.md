# The reframe toolkit

Load this at step 3, when you go looking for the angle. It is for your thinking, do not paste it into chat.

**The one principle: interesting is the frame, not the information.** The viewer has heard the topic before. What earns the click and holds the watch is the angle on the idea, not the facts inside it. A reframe makes a known idea feel new.

## The five moves

The angle usually wants one of these. Pick the one the material already leans toward, do not force all five.

1. **Fresh comparison or metaphor.** Explain the idea through an unexpected lens the viewer already understands. "Your onboarding is a pre-flight checklist" lands harder than "onboarding matters."
2. **Contrarian flip.** Name the advice the audience already believes, then cut against it. Only when it is true. Never contrarian for sport.
3. **Named system or rule.** Package the idea as a defined thing: "The 90-minute rule," "the boring-system play." A name does double duty, it feels proprietary (new) and it feels doable (a defined process, not a vague idea).
4. **The creator's own story.** The idea lived through something real that happened to the creator or a client. The story is the frame, the lesson rides inside it.
5. **Visual framework.** A picture or model the video is built around, a two-axis grid, a before and after, a simple diagram that makes the idea graspable in one look.

## The test: does it feel new?

Judge every candidate against BENS, and weight N.

- **N (New) is the one that matters most here.** Feel-new beats be-new. The information does not have to be original. The framing has to make the viewer feel they have not seen this take.
- **B (Big):** is the promise large enough to be worth the click.
- **E (Easy):** does the payoff feel achievable, not overwhelming.
- **S (Safe):** does it feel believable and honest, not hype.

If a candidate feels new, big, and believable, it is a real frame. If it only restates the topic, keep hunting.

## Near-misses (these are not reframes)

- **Accurate restatement.** "Document your onboarding" restates the topic. It describes the video, it does not frame it. Test it: if the viewer's brain fills in nothing, it is a label, not an angle.
- **Clever with nothing under it.** A metaphor or flip that sounds sharp but the body cannot deliver. Anti-fabrication holds. The frame is a promise the video has to keep.

Keep the frame to one line, in the creator's voice. If the creator would reword it saying it out loud, it is wrong.
