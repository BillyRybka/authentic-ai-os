# Core Desire

The material tells you what the video does. The core desire is what that gets somebody. You find it by climbing, and then you land on one rung.

The climb is working. One rung is the output.

## Five worked ladders

**Autocorrect for AI.** A checker runs on the draft against the creator's own banned-word list.

> The AI remembers the rules, so they stop re-explaining the same correction, so they have a co-writer instead of a first draft to repair, so more of what they write actually goes out.

Lands on: **a co-writer that already sounds like them, instead of a first draft they have to repair.**

Too high: "AI that is actually usable." Nobody says that out loud, and there is no video in it.
Too low: "the AI remembers the rules." That is the mechanism wearing a want's clothes.

**Meal prep.** Batch cooking, aimed at students.

> They cook once for the week, so they stop buying lunch every day, so they eat properly on the money they actually have, so they stop choosing between eating well and making rent.

Lands on: **eating properly on the money they actually have.**

Too high: "they take control of their life." True of almost any video, so it aims at nobody.
Too low: "they batch cook on Sunday." The mechanism again.

**Swiss meringue.** A technique video.

> The meringue holds, so the dessert does not collapse in front of people, so they stop only baking the two things they already know will work.

Lands on: **they stop only baking the things they know will work.**

Too high: "they become a confident baker." A mood, not a behaviour, so nothing observable changes.
Too low: "they know the right temperature." A fact, not a want.

**Five reasons people don't buy.** Objection handling.

> They hear the objection coming, so they answer it before it hardens, so they stop losing deals they were told were a yes.

Lands on: **they stop losing deals they were told were a yes.**

Too high: "they get better at sales."
Too low: "they can name five objections."

**Onboarding.** The creator lost four clients and misdiagnosed it as delivery.

> Onboarding does real work, so the client is still there in week one, so they stop losing clients they already closed.

Lands on: **they stop losing clients they already closed.**

Too high: "they build a business that lasts."
Too low: "they have an onboarding checklist."

## Sideways is not up

In that last one, "so they stop rebuilding delivery to fix a problem that is not in delivery" is tempting as another rung. It is not one. It restates the same loss from a different seat.

Only count a rung when the answer got genuinely bigger. When it stops getting bigger, you have hit the top, and the answer is the rung below it.

## What a bad landing looks like

The most common failure is printing the ladder instead of picking off it.

Weak:

> The AI stops handing its own words back as yours, so you stop scrubbing every draft, so what it writes actually goes out sounding like you.

That is three wants joined by "so." Every framing downstream now has three things to serve, so it serves none of them properly and the set comes back blurry.

Stronger:

> A co-writer that already sounds like them, instead of a first draft they have to repair.

Weak, in the other direction:

> They want their brand voice reflected consistently across all their content.

Nobody says this. It is the want translated into the language of a marketing deck, which means it came from a category rather than from this person.

Stronger:

> They want to stop being the one who catches the same word every single time.

## Two tests

**Would they say it out loud, in their own words?** Not "improve consistency." More like "I'm sick of fixing the same thing."

**Ask "so what" once more.** If the answer is another benefit, keep climbing. If it is the end of the line, stop, and take the rung below the one that stopped.

## Where it comes from

The dump is written about the tool, because the creator built the tool. What they tried and what kept going wrong is what turns a tool description into a want, and that usually arrives in their answer at stage 1 rather than in the material.

Run the climb off the dump alone and it lands on the mechanism nearly every time.
