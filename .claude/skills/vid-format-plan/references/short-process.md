# Short process format card

Thirteen numbered blanks, in the order the file lays them out and the order they get asked. Blank 10 repeats once per step, so the real count is 12 plus 1 per step.

`knowledge/format-planners/short-process.md` is the content source of truth. This card holds the wording of each ask and the bar a passing answer clears, and points back at the planner for everything else. Nothing from the planner gets restated here. Two copies of one rule drift the first time either gets edited.

**Load-bearing: blanks 7 and 8.** The planner's Rules section states both as hard: one parable up front, which the structure section calls the critical distinction that separates this format from a listicle, and a maximum of eight steps, where five that get eighty percent beat ten that dilute. Those two get up to two follow-ups when the answer comes back thin, then a gap callout. Every other blank gets one ask and takes whatever comes back.

**Section names in the plan file are the blank names below**, numbered as they are numbered here.

**One thing per ask.** Some blanks hold two things and carry an `Ask 2`. That is two short turns, not one long question. The count in the progress line does not move between them.

**Blank 8 sets the step count.** Until it lands, the total in the progress line carries a `+`. See the fan-out rule in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Goal

- **Section:** `## 1. Goal`
- **Planner:** step 1 of the 10-step planner
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Sales, emails, or views?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one goal named.
- **Notes:** usually answered at step 0. The planner's Rules section says one goal per video, never two. If they name two, ask which one this video is for and write only that.

## 2. Title and thumbnail

- **Section:** `## 2. Title and thumbnail`
- **Planner:** step 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Working title?
- **Ask 2:** What's on the thumbnail?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a title in words plus what the thumbnail shows.
- **Notes:** the planner locks the package first because it sets the promise, and its Rules section says do not be cute with that promise. The title formulas for this format are plain on purpose: how to X, how to X in Y.

## 3. Viewer's top 3 questions

- **Section:** `## 3. Viewer's top 3 questions`
- **Planner:** step 3
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What three questions does that package put in their head?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** three questions, phrased as questions.
- **Notes:** these three are the filter blank 5 runs on, so they earn their keep here rather than being a formality.

## 4. Brain dump: everything you might cover

- **Section:** `## 4. Brain dump: everything you might cover`
- **Planner:** step 4
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Every step or idea you might include. No order.
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a rough list. The planner wants 10 to 20 so the cut has room.
- **Notes:** do not sort or tidy as it comes in.

## 5. The cut

- **Section:** `## 5. The cut`
- **Planner:** step 5
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Which of those pay off the three questions?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the survivors, everything else struck.
- **Notes:** the planner's filter here is narrow on purpose: keep only what directly answers the three viewer questions. Its cake analogy is the scope test if the creator's list is sprawling into a full system. Short process is the sponge, deep dive is the whole cake. Say it once if the scope has clearly slipped, then take their call.

## 6. Intro: the result tease

- **Section:** `## 6. Intro: the result tease`
- **Planner:** the Intro adaptation table, rows 3 and 4
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What result do they see up front?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the outcome and, where there is one, the timeframe.
- **Notes:** the planner's intro for this format runs 10 to 15 seconds, faster than everything except news, because a long intro contradicts an action format. It leads with the result rather than the pain most of the time. Do not write the opening.

## 7. The one parable

- **Section:** `## 7. The one parable`
- **Planner:** step 6, the Parable Decision Matrix, and the Rules section
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What's the one bit up front that makes them feel it?
- **Push 1:** Demo, contrast, story, or metaphor. Which one?
- **Push 2:** What's the old way you're arguing against?
- **Passing:** one type named plus the material behind it: the thing being demoed, the two sides of the contrast, the story, or the frame.
- **Notes:** one, up front, not one per step. That is the planner's critical distinction and the line between this format and a listicle. Run the matrix silently against the problem the video solves and offer the type it lands on as a candidate. The doc's Old Way / New Way example is there if they want to see one, but do not read it at them.

## 8. The steps

- **Section:** `## 8. The steps`
- **Planner:** step 7, and the Rules section
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What are the steps, in order?
- **Push 1:** If they could only do five, which five?
- **Push 2:** Which one could you drop and they'd still get the result?
- **Passing:** an ordered list, eight or fewer, each one an action.
- **Notes:** the planner caps it at eight and says that if ten steps exist but five deliver eighty percent of the result, give the five. Hold the cap. This blank sets the step count for blank 10: write the list to the file, then write one section per step before the next question goes out.

## 9. The hard step

- **Section:** `## 9. The hard step`
- **Planner:** the "Optional: a second parable before a hard step" section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Is one of those a step they won't understand or won't believe?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one step named plus the demo, story, or metaphor that carries it, or a clear no. Those are the two triggers: a step they get stuck on, or a step they will not believe is worth doing until it is shown working.
- **Notes:** the planner is explicit that this is optional and that many videos need it zero times. No is a complete answer and the common one. Ask once, take it, move.

## 10. Step {n}: the forward hook

- **Section:** `### 10.{n} Step {n}: the forward hook`
- **Planner:** the Rules section, transitions hook forward
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Repeats:** once per step, count set by blank 8
- **Ask:** Why does step {n+1} matter? Say it before you give it.
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a reason to stay, stated as what the next step gets them. The shape: "step two will stop your viewers getting bored and get you more consistent leads."
- **Notes:** the last step hooks into the ending rather than into another step, so ask that one as what they get for staying to the end.

## 11. Conversion

- **Section:** `## 11. Conversion`
- **Planner:** step 8, and the matching subsection of Conversion strategy by goal
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** set by the goal in blank 1.
  - **Emails:** What's the lead magnet?
  - **Emails 2:** Which step does it come out of?
  - **Sales:** Direct pitch, or do you demo the product inside the content?
  - **Sales 2:** What's the top link in the description?
  - **Views:** No links on this one. Anything at the end instead?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the form of the ask plus the description decision. For views, "no links" is a complete answer.
- **Notes:** the planner's Rules section says the lead magnet must relate to the content: if step 3 is about a checklist, the magnet is that checklist. If they name a magnet from a different video, say so once. The one exception worth taking without a fight: a magnet with a record of converting no matter which video carries it. On sales it wants the CTA at roughly 90 seconds, middle, and end.

## 12. Delivery

- **Section:** `## 12. Delivery`
- **Planner:** step 9, and the Output format section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Word for word, teleprompter?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the shooting shape.
- **Notes:** the planner scripts this format end to end and reads it off a teleprompter, not from bullets. Say that once if they want bullets, then write down their call.

## 13. Upload and track

- **Section:** `## 13. Upload and track`
- **Planner:** step 10, and the End screen routing section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Which video does the end screen point at?
- **Ask 2:** What number tells you this one worked?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a real published title and one metric tied to the goal in blank 1.
- **Notes:** the planner's views route is unusual and worth getting right: point at the previous version of this same template, the video this one iterates on. No previous version that performed means the next video a new viewer should see. A video they intend to make is a gap, not an answer.
