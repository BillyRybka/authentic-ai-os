# Interview format card

Ten numbered blanks, in the order the file lays them out and the order they get asked. Blank 7 repeats once per question, so the real count is 9 plus 1 per question.

`knowledge/format-planners/interview.md` is the content source of truth. This card holds the wording of each ask and the bar a passing answer clears, and points back at the planner for everything else. Nothing from the planner gets restated here. Two copies of one rule drift the first time either gets edited.

**Load-bearing: blanks 3, 7, and 10.** The package, because the planner gives packaging-first its own section and says an interview recorded without a locked title and thumbnail sprawls. Each question, because the planner gives question planning its own section with three things every question has to do. And the end screen, because the planner heads that section "THE single most important post-publish move" and calls it the entire monetization mechanism. Those three get up to two follow-ups when the answer comes back thin, then a gap callout. Every other blank gets one ask and takes whatever comes back.

**This card runs before the recording, not after.** Blanks 3 through 7 are the reason the recording works. If the creator has already recorded, the plan is still worth filling for the edit and the end screen, but say once that the package was supposed to come first.

**Section names in the plan file are the blank names below**, numbered as they are numbered here.

**One thing per ask.** Some blanks hold two things and carry an `Ask 2`. That is two short turns, not one long question. The count in the progress line does not move between them.

**Blank 6 sets the question count.** Until it lands, the total in the progress line carries a `+`. See the fan-out rule in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. The guest

- **Section:** `## 1. The guest`
- **Planner:** step 1 of the 7-step planner
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Who's the guest, and what did they do?
- **Ask 2:** Are they known to your audience?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a name plus the specific achievement, and a yes or no on recognition.
- **Notes:** the planner's bar is that the guest achieved what the viewers want, is not shy, and has real insights, verified before the invite. The recognition answer decides blank 3: if they are not famous, the title leads with the achievement and never with the name.

## 2. Goal

- **Section:** `## 2. Goal`
- **Planner:** step 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Views or emails?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one of the two named.
- **Notes:** usually answered at step 0. Sales is wrong for this format and the planner is hard about it: a documented 300k-view interview produced zero sales because the credibility flowed to the guest. If they say sales, say that once and ask which of the two instead. If they hold, write it down as theirs.

## 3. Title and thumbnail

- **Section:** `## 3. Title and thumbnail`
- **Planner:** step 3, and the Packaging-first approach section
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** Working title?
- **Ask 2:** What's on the thumbnail?
- **Push 1:** What's the one remarkable thing they did? Lead with that.
- **Push 2:** Would someone who has never heard their name click it?
- **Passing:** a title built on the achievement, plus what the thumbnail shows.
- **Notes:** the planner's example is the whole rule: "The Man Making YouTubers Rich For Free" beats "[Guest Name] On YouTube Marketing", because the achievement carries the curiosity and an unknown name does nothing. If blank 1 said the guest is known to the audience, the name can lead and the pushes do not run. No title yet usually means the pre-call has not happened: the planner locks the package on a call before the recording, by getting the guest talking and listening for the line that stops you. If the package is still open, write the pre-call in as the gap, skip the pushes, and keep going. Once it is locked, the guest gets told that is what the interview is built around.

## 4. Viewer's top 3 questions

- **Section:** `## 4. Viewer's top 3 questions`
- **Planner:** step 4
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What three questions does that package put in their head?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** three questions, phrased as questions.
- **Notes:** the planner's usual three for this format are who is this person, why should I trust them, and what specifically did they do. Every question in blank 7 has to tie back to one of these, so they are worth getting right before moving on.

## 5. Intro: the credibility load

- **Section:** `## 5. Intro: the credibility load`
- **Planner:** step 6, and the Intro adaptation table
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What are the guest's numbers?
- **Ask 2:** What's the most unusual proof they've got?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** specific results, notable names, odd proofs. The planner's example of the kind of thing that lands: made a dog trainer $40k a month.
- **Notes:** almost the whole intro is credibility, and all of it goes on the guest. The planner says not to talk about yourself here, because cold viewers came for them. It also says the intro gets recorded separately and added in the edit, so it can be written after the conversation happens. Do not write the intro.

## 6. The question list

- **Section:** `## 6. The question list`
- **Planner:** step 5
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** How many questions, and what do they cover?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a count and a rough area per question.
- **Notes:** this blank sets the question count for blank 7: write the list to the file, then write one section per question before the next question goes out. The planner does not name a number. Whatever they say is the count, and the aggressive edit later is what protects the runtime.

## 7. Question {n}

- **Section:** `### 7.{n} Question {n}`
- **Planner:** step 5, and the Question planning section
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Repeats:** once per question, count set by blank 6
- **Ask:** Question {n}. What do you actually say?
- **Push 1:** Reword it so it makes them tell a story. "What was the moment you realized..."
- **Push 2:** Which of the three viewer questions does it pay off?
- **Passing:** a question, worded as it will be asked, that forces a story before logic, leads somewhere actionable, and ties to one of the three in blank 4.
- **Notes:** the planner's failures are named and worth catching: "tell me about your business" is too broad and the guest rambles, "what's your advice for X" gets generic advice with no story, and yes or no questions kill momentum. Its strong shapes all start from a moment: what was the moment, what was the one thing stopping you, walk me through the day. The pushes run per question, and the first one or two teach the shape.

## 8. The edit plan

- **Section:** `## 8. The edit plan`
- **Planner:** step 7, and the Edit aggressively section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** How long is the recording, and how long is the video?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** two numbers, where the second is much smaller than the first.
- **Notes:** the planner's normal is 45 minutes cut to 10, and it says top interview channels routinely cut more than half. If they plan to publish the whole recording, say that once and write down what they decide. Non-media-trained guests need the edit as protection.

## 9. Conversion

- **Section:** `## 9. Conversion`
- **Planner:** the Conversion strategy by goal section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** set by the goal in blank 2.
  - **Views:** No links on this one. Anything at the end instead?
  - **Emails:** What's the lead magnet?
  - **Emails 2:** Opening and closing for the two mentions?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the form of the ask plus the description decision. For views, "no links" is a complete answer.
- **Notes:** the planner gives interviews two mentions rather than three, and no product pitch at all. The magnet can hang off the guest's area or off the channel's broader value.

## 10. The end screen

- **Section:** `## 10. The end screen`
- **Planner:** the "End screen routing" section, and The hard truth about interviews
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** Which of your videos does the end screen point at?
- **Ask 2:** What number tells you this one worked?
- **Push 1:** Not another interview. Which one shows you as the expert?
- **Push 2:** A new viewer just met you through someone else. What do you show them next?
- **Passing:** a real published video where the creator is the authority, plus one metric tied to the goal in blank 2.
- **Notes:** the planner calls this the entire monetization mechanism for the format, because the credibility went to the guest and this is the only place it comes back. Interviews that end-screen to interviews make a podcast: viewers come for guests, and the channel never grows on the host's name. The pushes exist to move them off another interview.
