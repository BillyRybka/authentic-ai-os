# Post-type: Checklist

## When to pick it

The idea gives the reader a repeatable action they can run right now: a pre-flight before they start, an audit of what they already do, a review they can repeat each week. The job is to hand them a short list they can check themselves against today, then tell them which one or two items actually move the needle.

## Structure

1. State the use case and when to run it. Be specific about the moment.
2. List 5 to 8 checks. Each one short, concrete, and checkable.
3. Call out the one or two checks that matter most. Most readers ignore those.
4. Close with a save-this line or a reflective question.

## Good example

> Run this before your next 30-minute session, when you are tired and tempted to wing it.
>
> 1. Does it open with one compound lift, not a warm-up that eats ten minutes?
> 2. Are you supersetting, so there is no standing around?
> 3. Is every exercise loaded heavy enough to fail by rep eight?
> 4. Did you cap conditioning at five minutes, not twenty on a bike?
> 5. Is the whole thing written down before you walk in?
>
> The one that decides everything is number three. A 30-minute session at half effort is a 30-minute warm-up, and most founders live there.
>
> Screenshot this and check it Sunday night before the week starts.

Why it works: it names the exact moment to run the list (tired, before a session), gives five short checkable items, flags the one that actually matters (load, not duration) with the specific cost of skipping it, and closes with a save-this line tied to a real weekly trigger. Specific, committed, no filler.

## Bad example (slop)

> Here are some tips to make sure your workouts are as effective as possible.
>
> 1. Make sure you are staying consistent with your routine.
> 2. Try to challenge yourself and push past your limits.
> 3. Focus on proper form to avoid injury.
> 4. Stay hydrated and get enough rest.
> 5. Listen to your body and adjust as needed.
>
> At the end of the day, the most important thing is to find what works for you and keep showing up.
>
> Save this for later and let me know which tip resonated most with you.

Why it fails: the items are generic platitudes, not checkable actions ("push past your limits," "listen to your body"). Nothing is specific to a 30-minute session, nothing is measurable, and no single check is flagged as the one that matters. It hedges ("try to," "as needed"), reaches for filler ("at the end of the day"), and could be pasted under any fitness post on earth. It uses no em-dashes and is still pure slop. Slop is a substance failure, not a punctuation one.
