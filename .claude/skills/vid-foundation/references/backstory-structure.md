# Backstory Structure

A 1-2 paragraph story that establishes the creator's personal journey from Problem to Outcome. Used in intros, on the channel banner, in emails, and as the foundation for case study videos.

## The structure: Problem, Action, Outcome

**Problem:** what the creator (or their ideal client) struggled with. Same problem the viewer has now.
**Action:** what they did about it. Concrete, specific actions. Verbs.
**Outcome:** the transformation. Specific result, numbers where possible.

Length: 1-2 short paragraphs. Conversational tone. Not polished marketing copy.

## Examples

### Example 1: physiotherapy creator (borrowed client backstory)

> "I'd been a physio for 15 years and kept seeing the same thing: patients coming back month after month with the same knee, hip, or back pain. They'd tried everything. Nothing stuck.
>
> So I stopped giving generic exercises and started mapping where each patient's pain actually originated. Usually 2-3 joints away from where it hurt. I ran the new method with 500 patients over 3 years, tracking what worked. 87% saw results in under 8 weeks. That method is what this channel is built on."

What makes it work:
- Problem is specific (patients returning, tried everything, nothing stuck)
- Action is concrete verbs (stopped giving generic exercises, started mapping, ran with 500 patients, tracked what worked)
- Outcome has real numbers (87%, under 8 weeks, 500 patients)

### Example 2: YouTube growth creator

> "Six months into my channel I had 400 subscribers, zero revenue, and was about to move back in with my parents. I'd read every guide, tried every 'hack,' and nothing was working.
>
> So I stopped chasing views and started studying what actually makes videos convert. Rebuilt my packaging using proven title structures. Rewrote every script around a single payoff instead of rambling. Added CTAs that matched the lead magnet exactly. Eighteen months later the channel was at 360,000 subscribers and doing $4M a year. I help creators skip the guide-and-hack phase."

What makes it work:
- Problem is concrete (6 months, 400 subs, $0, parents)
- Action is specific verbs (stopped chasing, studied conversion, rebuilt packaging, rewrote scripts, added CTAs)
- Outcome has numbers and timeframe (360k subs, $4M, 18 months)

### Example 3: narcissism recovery creator

> "I spent eight years in a relationship that was slowly erasing me. I went to six different therapists. Not one used the word 'narcissist.' I walked out of every session convinced something was wrong with me.
>
> So I started researching personality disorders on my own. Joined support communities. Read every clinical paper I could find. Eventually left the relationship and spent three years rebuilding. I filmed my first video on the exact moment I realized he was a narcissist and what I wish someone had told me sooner. First five videos flopped. Video six hit 2.3 million views. The channel now has 40,000 subscribers of women who recognized themselves in those first videos."

What makes it work:
- Problem is specific (8 years, 6 therapists, none named it)
- Action is concrete (researching, joining communities, reading papers, filming a specific moment)
- Outcome has numbers (2.3M views, 40k subs)

## Good vs bad: paired examples

Show the creator these pairs. The Action section is where most backstories fail. Creators summarize instead of listing concrete moves.

### Pair 1: the Action section test

**Good:**
> "Six months into my channel I had 400 subscribers, zero revenue, and was about to move back in with my parents. I'd read every guide, tried every 'hack,' and nothing was working.
>
> So I stopped chasing views and started studying what actually makes videos convert. Rebuilt my packaging using proven title structures. Rewrote every script around a single payoff instead of rambling. Added CTAs that matched the lead magnet exactly. Eighteen months later the channel was at 360,000 subscribers and doing $4M a year."

Why it works:
- Problem is concrete (6 months, 400 subs, $0, parents. No one invents that level of detail.)
- Action section is a list of MOVES: stopped chasing, started studying, rebuilt packaging, rewrote scripts, added CTAs. Someone else could follow these steps.
- Outcome has numbers and timeframe (360k subs, $4M, 18 months)

**Bad:**
> "My first business struggled, so I read a bunch of books and eventually figured out the system that works. Now I help other entrepreneurs do the same."

Why it fails:
- Problem is vague ("struggled". How? for how long? what did the numbers look like?)
- Action is summary, not action. "Read books and figured it out" could describe 10,000 different journeys.
- Outcome is missing completely. "Figured out the system that works" says nothing about what was achieved.
- Can't pressure-test it. Can't believe it.

### Pair 2: specificity in the Problem

**Good:**
> "I'd been a physio for 15 years and kept seeing the same thing: patients coming back month after month with the same knee, hip, or back pain. They'd tried everything. Nothing stuck.
>
> So I stopped giving generic exercises and started mapping where each patient's pain actually originated. Usually 2-3 joints away from where it hurt. I ran the new method with 500 patients over 3 years, tracking what worked. 87% saw results in under 8 weeks. That method is what this channel is built on."

Why it works:
- Problem names the exact pattern (patients returning with same pains, tried everything, nothing stuck)
- Action verbs: stopped giving, started mapping, ran the new method, tracked what worked
- Outcome: real percentage (87%), real timeframe (under 8 weeks), real scale (500 patients over 3 years)

**Bad:**
> "I had been practicing physiotherapy for many years when I noticed that traditional approaches weren't producing optimal outcomes for patients. I decided to develop a more effective methodology that I now use to help people."

Why it fails:
- Corporate tone. "Practicing physiotherapy," "traditional approaches," "optimal outcomes" is how a press release talks.
- No specific complaint pattern. "Weren't producing optimal outcomes" could describe any profession.
- "Develop a methodology" is summary. What did they actually DO day-to-day?
- "Help people" has no number, no timeframe, no proof

### Pair 3: outcome specificity

**Good:**
> "I spent eight years in a relationship that was slowly erasing me. I went to six different therapists. Not one used the word 'narcissist.' I walked out of every session convinced something was wrong with me.
>
> So I started researching personality disorders on my own. Joined support communities. Read every clinical paper I could find. Eventually left the relationship and spent three years rebuilding. I filmed my first video on the exact moment I realized he was a narcissist and what I wish someone had told me sooner. First five videos flopped. Video six hit 2.3 million views. The channel now has 40,000 subscribers."

Why it works:
- Problem is emotionally concrete (8 years, 6 therapists, not one named it)
- Action is specific (researching, joining communities, reading papers, filming a specific moment)
- Outcome layers numbers (2.3M views, 40k subs) AND includes the honest path (first five flopped)

**Bad:**
> "After a difficult relationship, I went on a healing journey and now I share what I learned with others who are going through similar experiences."

Why it fails:
- "Difficult relationship". Which kind? Narcissistic? Abusive? Codependent? Each needs a different backstory.
- "Healing journey", the cliché phrase, tells nothing
- "Share what I learned". Share how? Where? With what scale?
- No numbers anywhere. Can't tell if this happened last week or ten years ago.

### The Action-section test

Before approving any backstory, read just the Action section out loud. Ask: "Could someone else do this from what's written?" If the answer is "they'd know exactly what to do", the Action is good. If the answer is "I have no idea what they actually did", the Action is summary, not action. Rewrite.

## Rules

- **Same problem as the viewer.** The backstory has to match the positioning. If the creator helps physio patients with knee pain, the backstory can't be about marketing struggles.
- **Past tense for Problem and Action.** It already happened. "I was" not "I am."
- **Action section needs real verbs.** Not "I built a system" (that's summary). Use specific moves: "I started documenting X," "I stopped doing Y and replaced it with Z," "I ran the new method with 500 patients."
- **Specific numbers in Problem and Outcome.** Not "many hours", say "70-80 hours." Not "a lot of patients", say "500 patients over 3 years."
- **Conversational tone.** It's the creator talking, not a press release. Contractions, natural rhythm, no corporate language.
- **Short paragraphs.** 3-5 sentences each. Two paragraphs maximum.

## If the creator never had the viewer's problem

Doctors, physiotherapists, consultants who teach things they didn't personally experience. Use a real client's backstory instead.

Format:
- **Problem:** what the client was struggling with
- **Action:** what the creator did WITH the client (not just what the client did alone)
- **Outcome:** the client's transformation

Attribute clearly: "A client came to me with..." or "I worked with a patient who..."

## Where the backstory gets used

- Intro of videos (shortened to 2-3 sentences, called the credibility line)
- Channel banner / about page
- First email in welcome sequence
- Case study videos (expanded into full story)
- LinkedIn / Twitter bios

The full backstory lives in creator-foundation.md. Shorter versions get pulled per-context by downstream skills.

## Common mistakes

- **Jumping from Problem to Outcome with no real Action.** "My first business failed. Then I built my second business around systems." The middle is missing. What specifically did they DO? Document what? Build what? Hire whom? Try what first that didn't work? The Action section is usually the weakest because creators summarize instead of listing moves.
- Generic journey ("I struggled with work-life balance for years")
- No specific outcome ("I figured it out and now I help others do the same")
- Outcome without action ("I was broke. Now I make $4M.")
- Corporate tone ("I leveraged my 12 years of expertise to develop a proprietary methodology")
- Fabrication (never invent details)
- Mixing creator backstory with client backstory in one story (pick one per story)

## The 3-sentence version

For quick use in video intros, compress the full backstory to 3 sentences:

> "I've been a physio for 15 years. I ran my new treatment method with 500 patients over 3 years. 87% got results in under 8 weeks. That method is what this channel is built on."

One sentence of context, one sentence of the proof, one sentence of the promise.

Before finalizing a backstory, read just the Action section out loud. Ask: "Could someone else do this?" If the answer is "they'd know what to do", the Action is good. If the answer is "I have no idea what they actually did", the Action is summary, not action. Rewrite.
