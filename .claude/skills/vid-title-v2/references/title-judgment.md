# Sharpen title judgment

Read this only when relevant bank evidence is thin or the first pass lacks distinct shapes. Use it for private drafting, not as creator-facing terminology.

## Choose a useful shape

Match the shape to the locked promise. Do not force every shape into the set.

- **Cost of the current behavior:** `[Mistake] That Keeps [Wound] Happening`
- **Contrarian correction:** `Stop [Current Behavior]. [Better Direction]`
- **Desired result:** `How [Subject] Reached [Supported Result]`
- **Constraint proof:** `[Desired Result] Without [Expected Requirement]`
- **Consequence:** `Why [Seemingly Harmless Choice] Costs [Supported Stake]`
- **Specific curiosity:** `The [Supported Specific] That Changed [Supported Result]`

These are drafting shapes, not phrases to copy. Bank evidence outranks them when a relevant proven structure exists.

## Keep the promise, change the pressure

Locked video:

- Frame: Show freelance designers how to stop approved design decisions from being reopened during a project.
- Core payoff: Create a one-page decision record the client confirms and use it when a settled choice comes back into question.
- Stakes: Reopened decisions create unpaid revision work and make the designer look disorganized.

Useful title shapes:

- `Why Clients Keep Reopening Approved Designs`
- `The Approval Mistake That Creates Free Revisions`
- `Stop Explaining Yourself to Clients. Do This Instead.`

Weak title shapes:

- `How to Make a One-Page Decision Record`
- `The Approval Mistake That Creates Free Revisions`
- `The 7-Step Client Communication System`

The useful set titles the tension or consequence. The weak set recites a process, uses a generic label, or invents a step count.

## Open one question

Ask what the viewer's mind must resolve after reading the title. Keep one strong question:

- `Why Clients Keep Reopening Approved Designs` opens: what causes this?
- `The Approval Mistake That Creates Free Revisions` opens: which mistake?

Cut a title when it opens unrelated questions, gives away the whole answer, or creates curiosity the video does not resolve.

## Use one dominant mechanism

Let one mechanism lead:

- curiosity about a cause
- fear of a true consequence
- desire for a supported result
- contrast with a common belief
- proof from a supported specific

Do not pile several mechanisms into one line. A clean consequence usually beats a title that adds social proof, urgency, novelty, and a warning at once.
