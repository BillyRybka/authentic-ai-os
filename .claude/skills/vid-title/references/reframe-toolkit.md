# The reframe toolkit

For your thinking at Step 3. Never pasted into chat.

**What this is for.** vid-framing already found the stake, the thing the viewer stands to lose. These five moves are the shapes you can pour it into. They are delivery vehicles, not generators: applied to a locked argument with a real stake in it they sharpen the pull, and applied to an idea with no stake they return a decorated restatement. If a candidate feels flat after all five, the problem is upstream in the angle, not here. Say so and route back to vid-framing.

Bank structures come first. Reach for these when the banks are thin for this material, when the wildcard slot needs a shape, or when the plainest true statement of the argument is not landing.

## The five moves

Pick the one the material already leans toward. Do not force all five.

1. **Fresh comparison or metaphor.** Carry the stake through a lens the viewer already understands. "Your onboarding is a pre-flight checklist" beats "onboarding matters," but only when missing the checklist actually costs them something.
2. **Contrarian flip.** Name the advice the audience already follows, then cut against it. Only when the creator genuinely believes it. Never contrarian for sport; a flip the body cannot defend costs more trust than a boring title.
3. **Named system or rule.** Package it as a defined thing: "The 90-minute rule," "the boring-system play." A name does double duty, proprietary (New) and doable (Easy).
4. **The creator's own story.** The stake lived through something real. The story is the shape, the lesson rides inside it.
5. **Visual framework.** A two-axis grid, a before and after, a model that makes the idea graspable in one look. Coordinate with vid-thumbnail when this one wins.

## Near-misses

- **Decoration.** A metaphor or flip laid over an argument with nothing at stake. It sounds sharp and it converts nothing, because there was no tension for it to carry.
- **Clever with nothing under it.** A shape the body cannot deliver. Anti-fabrication holds; the frame is a promise the video has to keep.
- **All five at once.** Stacked shapes read as trying too hard, the same way stacked click mechanisms do. One shape, executed all the way.
