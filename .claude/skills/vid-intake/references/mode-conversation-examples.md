# Capture Examples

Worked dialogues showing the one vid-intake capture flow running on different kinds of material. Optional depth: the flow and the watch-list in SKILL.md are enough for a normal run. Open this when you want to see a full run end to end, or when a conversation stalls and you need calibration on the feel. Each example pairs the shape that lands with a near-miss and a one-line "why this fails."

There is one flow. What changes is the material the creator brings and the one or two things to get right with it. The creator never hears a category; you open to match. Each example shows one way a conversation can go. These are illustrations, never scripts to follow.

The creator in these examples is a fitness coach. Iceberg statement: "I help busy founders build sustainable strength training habits in 30 minutes a day." The lane covers time scarcity that collapses routines, cardio-bias that misallocates effort, and inconsistency under stress.

## An idea, talked through

### Worked

**Creator:** "I want to make a video about why founders shouldn't run for cardio. Most of my clients waste 4 hours a week on zone 2 they could be using for strength."

**Skill (open the door):** "Dump everything you've got. The points you want to make, anything kicking around, raw. I'll listen."

**Creator:** "OK so the main thing is that founders pick running because it feels productive. They've been told cardio is healthy. But the math is wrong. 4 hours of jogging burns maybe 1800 calories. 4 hours of strength gives you 5 lbs of muscle over 8 weeks which raises BMR and burns more long-term. Plus injury risk. Plus the time. I had a client, James, drop running, gain muscle, lose more fat than when he was running. And the time saved goes back into deep work."

**Skill (reflect):** "Heard 4 points: cardio feels productive but isn't, the BMR math favors strength, injury risk on running, time recovered for deep work. A client story about James. A claim about 1800 cal vs 5 lbs muscle. Anything I missed?"

**Creator:** "That's it."

**Skill (drill once on the thin spot):** "James story is the strongest piece. Quick: how long did he run for, how long until you saw the body comp shift?"

**Creator:** "He ran 4x a week for 18 months, lost 6 lbs of fat. Switched to my program. 12 weeks later he was down 14 lbs and his squat went from 135 to 225."

**Skill (fit check):** "That all sits inside your iceberg, the cardio-bias lane of helping founders build sustainable strength. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill (save):** "Locked. Slug: `cardio-vs-strength-for-founders`. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill:** "Saved to `content/pieces/cardio-vs-strength-for-founders/brain-dump.md`. Run vid-framing next to lock the angle and format."

**Why this works:** the creator dumped freely without interruption. Skill mirrored back the structure they already had, drilled the one weakest spot (James story specifics) to surface the proof, then ran a single-exchange fit check on the way out. Save was clean. The whole thing stayed in flow, no interrogation.

### Near-miss

**Creator:** "I want to make a video about why founders shouldn't run for cardio."

**Skill:** "Got it. Let's structure this. First, who is your ideal viewer? Demographics, psychographics, where they spend time online?"

**Creator:** "...uh, I have all that in my foundation doc."

**Skill:** "Right. What's the format you want to use? Short Process, Case Study, Listicle, News?"

**Creator:** "I don't know yet."

**Skill:** "Let's discuss your goal. Sales, emails, views?"

**Why this fails:** skill ran an interrogation instead of letting the creator dump. Asked questions that belong in `vid-framing`, not intake. Creator's enthusiasm for the topic dies in the questionnaire.

## Notes or bullets, pasted

### Worked

**Creator:** "Here are the bullets I have so far for a video on morning routines for founders."
- Most morning routines are aesthetic, not functional
- The 3 things that actually matter: sleep window, sunlight, one hard physical task
- I tried the 5am Hard 75 thing for 3 months and it broke me
- My current routine takes 22 minutes
- Clients report better focus when they cut the journaling step

**Skill (scan the bullets):** "5 bullets. Quick scan: 3 are full thoughts, 2 are placeholders waiting for your specifics. Want to expand the placeholders, or save what you have and let the next step chase them later?"

**Creator:** "Expand the 5am Hard 75 one. That's the story I want to anchor on."

**Skill:** "Tell me the moment, not the lesson. What broke you?"

**Creator:** "Day 47. I'd been waking at 4:45am for cold plunges and a 20-min run. Came down with a flu, kept pushing through, ended up bedridden for a week. Lost 8 lbs of muscle. Took me 2 months to rebuild."

**Skill (fit check):** "Inside your iceberg. This is over-doing-it under stress, the consistency lane flipped. Same thread. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Exactly."

**Skill:** "Slug: `morning-routine-functional-not-aesthetic`. Save?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Why this works:** light touch on the bullets that are already full thoughts. One drill on the placeholder the creator flagged as the anchor story. Alignment caught the angle (over-doing-it as a flavor of inconsistency under stress).

### Near-miss

**Creator:** [pastes 5 bullets]

**Skill:** "Let's go through each bullet and expand. Bullet 1: Most morning routines are aesthetic, not functional. Why? Who told you that? What's the proof? Worked example? Counter-example?"

**Why this fails:** the creator already wrote bullet 1 as a full thought. Drilling it is wasted effort. Only drill the placeholders or the bullets the creator flags as anchors.

## Their own transcript

### Worked

**Creator:** "Here's the transcript from a Loom I recorded ranting about why founders quit gyms in February."

[creator pastes ~800 words]

**Skill (ownership check):** "Quick check: this is your own recording, not someone else's? I treat the contents as your voice fuel, so I want to be sure."

**Creator:** "Yep, mine."

[skill reads the transcript once, silently]

**Skill (reflect):** "Got 3 main points: gym attrition is psychological not physical, the New Year fantasy collapses when stress hits, the fix is matching gym time to existing habits. One story about your own gym quit in 2019. One claim about 80% of new gym members ghosting by March without a number source. Did I catch it right?"

**Creator:** "Yeah. The 80% number I'm not sure about. I think it was a Bodybuilding.com survey. Flag that one."

**Skill (fit check):** "Inside your iceberg, the consistency-under-stress lane. The 80% number flagged as TODO for fact-check. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill:** "Slug: `february-gym-attrition`. Save?"

**Why this works:** ownership check first ("yours or theirs?") so the words get treated as the creator's own voice. Skill read the transcript once silently rather than echoing it. Reflection surfaced what was there AND what was missing (the 80% claim flagged as TODO).

### Near-miss

**Creator:** [pastes transcript]

**Skill:** "Got it, processing now. So based on this, here are the 3 hooks I think we should use, the format would be Short Process based on the structure, and the goal looks like emails since you mention your email list..."

**Why this fails:** skill jumped past intake into framing. The job here is capture, not strategy. Hooks, format, and goal belong in `vid-framing`, not vid-intake.

## Fresh news, fast

### Worked

**Creator:** "Whoop just released their new strength training tracking. I want to do a video on it."

**Skill (set the pace):** "Fast capture on this one."

**Skill:** "What's the news? One sentence."

**Creator:** "Whoop now tracks strain from strength training, not just cardio. They added concentric/eccentric rep counting and recovery scoring."

**Skill:** "What does it mean for your audience? One sentence."

**Creator:** "It validates what I've been telling founders for 3 years: strength is the highest-payoff workout. Now they have data."

**Skill:** "What's your angle, take, or contrarian read?"

**Creator:** "I don't think Whoop's algorithm is right yet. They're undercounting strain on compound lifts. But the directional move matters."

**Skill (fit check):** "Inside your iceberg, the cardio-bias lane, validated by a wearable that's catching up. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill:** "Slug: `whoop-strength-tracking-finally`. Save?"

**Why this works:** three fast questions, no dumping needed. News is about the angle, not material depth. Speed wins.

### Near-miss

**Creator:** "Whoop just launched strength training tracking."

**Skill:** "Let's dump everything you know about Whoop. When did they start, what's their pricing, what's the company history, who's their CEO?"

**Why this fails:** a news take is about the angle on the news, not a deep dive into the company. Save material gathering for if/when the creator wants to make a long-form review.

## A client win

### Worked

**Creator:** "I want to do a video around James. He went from 135 to 225 squat in 12 weeks while losing 14 lbs of fat. Only 30 minutes a day."

**Skill (frame the capture):** "Let's capture the proof, then pivot to the lesson. A case study can't be a biography, it has to teach. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill (capture proof):** "Got the result: 135 to 225 squat, 14 lbs fat loss, 12 weeks, 30 min/day. Anonymize James or name him?"

**Creator:** "Use his first name. He gave permission."

**Skill (force the pivot):** "OK. The video isn't 'why James did this.' That's a biography. It's 'here's the principle James proves you can do too.' What's the principle? What does the viewer learn that they can DO?"

**Creator:** "The principle is that compound lifts on a tight schedule beat any program with cardio + accessories. James only had 30 minutes a day. We picked 4 lifts. He hit them 3x a week. Progressive overload by 2.5 lbs per session. That's it."

**Skill (drill the method):** "Specifics: which 4 lifts, in what order, sets and reps?"

**Creator:** "Squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press. 5x5 for the first 3, 5x3 for OHP. Once a week each, fully recovered."

**Skill (fit check):** "Inside your iceberg, the cardio-bias lane, with James as the proof case. Lesson: 4 compound lifts, 30 min, 3x/week, beats cardio + accessories. Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill:** "Slug: `james-30-min-strength-protocol`. Save?"

**Why this works:** the pivot from client biography to teaching arc happens explicitly, not implicitly. Skill captures the proof THEN forces "what's the principle?" That's what makes a case study a video instead of a testimonial reel.

### Near-miss

**Creator:** "I want to do a video around James."

**Skill:** "Great. Let's capture James's full backstory. When did he start? What was his life like before? What was his job? What was his weight at start? What did he eat? Walk me through the first 4 weeks."

**Why this fails:** the skill is making a James documentary. The video is a teaching piece with James as evidence. Capture the proof fast, then pivot HARD to the lesson.

## A story that happened

### Worked

**Creator:** "OK this thing happened yesterday and I want to make a video about it. I'm at a coffee shop, this founder I've coached for 6 months walks in, and he's 30 lbs lighter than when I last saw him in January. We talk for 20 minutes. He says the thing that finally worked was when I made him cut his program from 4 days to 2 days."

**Skill (frame the capture):** "Let's capture the moment first, the problem, what you did, what changed. Then we'll find the lesson and check it fits your channel."

**Skill (P-A-O capture):** "Problem first. What was his state in January? Specific."

**Creator:** "He'd been training 4 days a week for 8 months. Lost 8 lbs. Plateaued. Was getting up at 5am, hating it, considering quitting."

**Skill:** "Action: what was the move you made?"

**Creator:** "I cut him to 2 days a week. He pushed back. I told him to trust the lower frequency for 12 weeks."

**Skill:** "Outcome: what changed?"

**Creator:** "12 weeks later, down 30 lbs total, up in strength on every lift. Said he stopped resenting the gym."

**Skill (locate the lesson):** "What's the lesson the audience walks away with from this story?"

**Creator:** "Less is more for stressed founders. Recovery is the lever. Higher frequency without recovery just digs the hole."

**Skill (fit check):** "Inside your iceberg, the consistency-under-stress lane. The lesson lands on 'reduce frequency to maintain consistency.' Sound right?"

**Creator:** "Yes."

**Skill:** "Slug: `cut-frequency-to-stay-consistent`. Save?"

**Why this works:** story captured in P-A-O without forcing the lesson first. Skill captures the moment THEN asks "what's the lesson?" The story comes first, the teaching comes second. The fit check at the end naturally confirms the iceberg lane.

### Near-miss

**Creator:** "OK this thing happened yesterday and I want to make a video about it."

**Skill:** "What's the lesson this story teaches?"

**Why this fails:** asking for the lesson before the story is told means the creator either invents a fit or skips the actual emotion of the moment. Capture the story first. The lesson surfaces from the story, not the other way.

## What every run does the same

1. **Open to match what they brought, silently.** Clock whether they are talking or pasting, and what the material is, then open to fit. The creator never hears a category.
2. **Open the door, then shut up.** Give the creator space to land the dump without interrupting.
3. **Reflect back in the creator's own language.** Mirror what they said, do not paraphrase into your own words.
4. **Run the iceberg fit check fast.** Surface the fit, creator confirms, move on.
5. **Drill ONLY where the material is thin.** Never run an exhaustive question battery.
6. **Save with a clean confirmation.** "Saved to [path]. Run vid-framing next."

## When you can't tell what they brought

If the opening message is ambiguous, ask once in plain language: is this an idea to talk through, notes or a transcript they already have, a client win, fresh news, or a story? Then open to match. No numbered menu, no category named back to them.
