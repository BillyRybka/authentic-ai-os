# The Read

The Read captures three things:

* The situation the viewer is stuck in.
* What changes after the video.
* What it costs them if nothing changes.

Write each field in third person.

Keep each to one short paragraph.

Each field has one job.

## Ask the cost first

Before writing, ask:

> What does it actually cost them to keep doing it this way?

Ask every time.

The answer is what makes the read belong to this piece instead of any video on the channel.

Use only what appears in the dump, the foundation, or the creator’s answer.

Do not fill the gap with a plausible consequence.

## Target

Target describes the loop the viewer is stuck in.

Show:

* What they want.
* What keeps happening.
* What they do in response.
* What that response costs them.

Write it as one causal chain.

Weak:

> Freelancers who want projects to run more smoothly.

Stronger:

> They win the website project, but start work after a kickoff call where nobody writes down what was decided. Two weeks later, the client remembers the scope differently, so the freelancer spends the next meeting defending work they thought had already been approved.

The stronger version names the project, the missing decision, the moment the problem returns, and the cost.

Target is not:

* A demographic.
* A list of goals and pain points.
* A broad category of people.
* A consequence with no chain leading to it.

The condition matters more than the category.

“Freelancers” is a category.

“Freelancers whose clients reopen decisions after the work has started” is a viewer.

## Transformation

Transformation describes what they stop doing and what they do instead.

Then it shows what that change makes possible.

Weak:

> They run better kickoff calls.

Stronger:

> They stop treating the kickoff as an introduction and use it to lock the decisions that could derail the project later. The client leaves with the scope, priorities, and approval points in writing, so new feedback changes the work instead of reopening old decisions.

The tactic alone is not the transformation.

A template, tool, framework, or checklist may deliver the change. It is not the change itself.

Transformation should land in the same place as the locked payoff.

If the payoff promises one ending and Transformation describes another, rewrite Transformation.

## Stakes

Stakes show what happens if the Target loop continues.

Begin with the cost at the end of Target.

Then follow what that cost causes next.

Weak:

> They lose time and money.

Stronger:

> If they keep starting projects without locking the decisions, disagreements arrive after the work is already done. They redo pages they believed were approved, the deadline moves, and the next project starts while the first one is still dragging behind them.

Each consequence should follow from the one before it.

Do not inflate the stakes.

A small cost written clearly is stronger than a dramatic consequence the material cannot support.

Include a wrong diagnosis only when the creator or source material gives you one.

If the evidence supports one consequence, write one. Stop when the chain stops.

## Keep the three separate

Target shows the current loop.

Transformation shows the new behavior.

Stakes show what happens if the loop continues.

Do not write the same idea three times with different labels.

Use this check:

* **Target:** What keeps happening now?
* **Transformation:** What do they do differently?
* **Stakes:** What follows if nothing changes?

## Final check

Before showing the read, confirm:

* Target is a causal chain, not a profile.
* The situation is specific enough to picture.
* Transformation changes behavior, not only mood.
* Transformation reaches the same ending as the payoff.
* Stakes grow directly from Target.
* Stakes belong to this piece, not the audience in general.
* Every claim comes from the available material.
* Nothing was added because it sounded persuasive.
* Each field does a different job.

Then show only:

**Target.**

**Transformation.**

**Stakes.**
