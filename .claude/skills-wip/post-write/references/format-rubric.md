# Format Rubric

How to pick the two things every post needs: the **post-type** (the shape of the idea) and the **platform delivery** (where it goes and how it gets packaged). These are separate decisions. Decide the shape first, then the delivery.

The examples here are in one illustrative niche, a strength coach who helps busy founders train in 30 minutes a day (problems: time scarcity, cardio-bias, inconsistency). They teach the shape, not the topic. Read the structure, not the subject.

## Step 1: The idea's job picks the post-type

Do not ask "what format do I feel like writing." Ask "what does this idea need in order to land." Run down this list and take the first clean match.

| Ask | Post-type | Example idea (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Is it correcting a behavior the audience is doing wrong? | **mistake** | Founders treat training like a 90-minute gym session, so they bail the first busy week. |
| Is it built on something that actually happened? | **story** | The client who trained 25 minutes twice a week and out-progressed his old 5-day self. |
| Is it a repeatable model with named parts? | **framework** | The 3-lift week: one push, one pull, one hinge. That is the whole program. |
| Is it a repeatable action the reader can run right now? | **checklist** | The 5 checks before you let yourself skip a session. |
| Is it challenging a belief the audience holds? | **contrarian** | Cardio is the worst use of a busy founder's 30 minutes. |
| Is it protecting the reader from a costly move? | **warning** | Do not add a 4th training day to fix inconsistency. You will quit faster. |
| Is it clarifying two things people confuse? | **comparison** | Training for fitness and training for a photo are different programs. |
| Is it a fast tactical contrast, weak move vs strong move? | **do-this-not-that** | Do this: 3 hard sets near failure. Not that: 6 easy sets you can talk through. |

If two types fit, pick the one that matches the idea's strongest ingredient. A real lived moment wants a story even if it also teaches a lesson, because the proof is the moment. A clean named model wants a framework even if it corrects a behavior, because the model is the value.

## Step 2: The job lens (use when Step 1 ties)

Every post has one primary job. The job narrows the type.

- **Convince** (the reader believes the wrong thing): contrarian, mistake.
- **Teach** (the reader needs to know how): checklist, framework, do-this-not-that.
- **Prove** (the idea needs credibility): story.
- **Simplify** (the idea is easily confused): framework, comparison.
- **Warn** (the reader is about to make an expensive mistake): warning.

One job per post. A post that tries to convince and teach and prove at once does none of them. If an idea genuinely carries two jobs, that is two posts, not one.

## Step 3: Map the type to the platform

Same idea, different platform, means a different delivery, never the same text reflowed. The matrix below is fit, not permission. A Medium fit can still ship if the creator wants it; flag the weaker fit and name the stronger platform.

| Post-type | LinkedIn | Instagram carousel | Instagram caption |
|---|---|---|---|
| mistake | Strong | Good | Good |
| story | Strong | Medium | Very Strong |
| framework | Strong | Very Strong | Good |
| checklist | Strong | Very Strong | Good |
| contrarian | Very Strong | Good | Good |
| warning | Strong | Good | Good |
| comparison | Strong | Good | Medium |
| do-this-not-that | Good | Very Strong | Medium |

**Reading the matrix:**
- **Very Strong / Strong:** lead the idea here. The shape and the platform reinforce each other.
- **Good:** works, write it normally.
- **Medium:** weaker fit. Write it if asked, but say why it is weaker and name the stronger platform for that type.

**Why the ratings fall this way:**
- **Carousel** rewards structured, listy, one-point-per-slide shapes. Checklists, frameworks, and do-this-not-that break into slides cleanly. A story loses its flow when chopped into slides, so it sits at Medium.
- **Caption** rewards warmth and a single thread. Stories and beliefs breathe here. A do-this-not-that contrast feels cramped without the visual rows a carousel gives it, so it sits at Medium.
- **LinkedIn** rewards argument and point of view. Contrarian leads here. Almost every type works because the format tolerates length and structure.

## The line that matters

Post-type answers "what is the shape of the idea." Platform answers "how should this idea be packaged for where it gets read." Keep the idea, rebuild the delivery. Reflowing one platform's text into another is recycling, and it is one of the loudest slop tells. See `anti-slop.md`.
