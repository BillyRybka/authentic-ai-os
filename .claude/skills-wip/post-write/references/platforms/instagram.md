# Platform: Instagram

Instagram has two delivery shapes this skill writes: the **carousel** (swipeable slides plus a caption) and the standalone **caption**. They are different jobs. A carousel teaches or lists something visual. A caption tells or argues something warm. Pick the one the idea wants, do not default to carousels.

This skill writes the **copy** and a **text visual brief**. It does not render images. Rendering is a downstream handoff.

Examples are in the strength-coach niche (busy founders, 30-minute training). They teach the delivery, not the topic.

## Sub-format A: Carousel

### Best for

Structured, listy, one-point-at-a-time ideas. Checklists, frameworks, do-this-not-that, comparisons, mistakes. Anything that breaks into clean steps or rows.

### Structure

- **Title slide.** The hook, and it has to face the reader, a belief they hold, a pain they feel, or a promise that opens a gap. A contrarian line or the one big idea, big and short. It earns the swipe or nothing else gets seen. See `references/hooks.md`.
- **3 to 5 idea slides.** One point per slide. A few words to a short sentence. No paragraphs.
- **Recap slide.** The takeaway compressed, or the list in one view. The screenshot-this slide.
- **Caption.** Expands the idea, carries the nuance the slides dropped, and holds the CTA (save, comment, DM).

### Copy rules

- One idea per slide. If a slide has two ideas, split it.
- Short. The slide is a headline, not a paragraph. The caption carries the rest.
- Strong title slide. If it does not earn the swipe, nothing else matters.
- Clear hierarchy and rhythm across slides. They should feel like a sequence.

### The visual brief (text only)

For each slide, write a short note the designer or a rendering tool can act on. Layout, what to emphasize, any imagery or icon idea, design notes. Keep it text. Example:

- Slide 1: bold single line, largest type, high contrast. Nothing else on the slide.
- Slide 2 to 4: one line each, the key number or verb emphasized, consistent type scale.
- Recap slide: the three lifts stacked as a simple list, check-marks, brand color.

### What to avoid

- Cramming a full LinkedIn post into slides.
- Too many words per slide.
- Abstract claims with no visual structure.

### Good example (carousel)

Title slide: You don't need more training days. You need a shorter program.
Slide 2: The 4th day is where busy founders quit.
Slide 3: Three lifts. One push. One pull. One hinge.
Slide 4: 25 minutes, twice a week. Kept beats perfect.
Recap slide: Short and done beats long and skipped.

Caption: Most founders try to fix inconsistent training by adding days. It backfires. A longer program is a bigger thing to skip, so the first busy week takes it down. The client who finally stayed consistent did the opposite. He cut to three lifts, twice a week, 25 minutes. A year later he had not missed a block. If your training keeps collapsing, shorten it before you add to it. Save this for the next week your calendar explodes.

Visual brief: Slide 1 bold one-liner, high contrast, no imagery. Slides 2 to 4 single line each, the number emphasized, consistent type. Recap slide as a short checklist with the brand color. Caption plain, no hashtag stuffing.

Why it works: one idea per slide, a title slide that earns the swipe, a recap worth screenshotting, and a caption that carries the nuance and a specific CTA.

## Sub-format B: Caption (standalone)

### Best for

Story, belief, personal angle, behind-the-scenes, a warmer take. The single-thread post, no slides.

### How to adapt

- **Hook still matters.** The first line shows before the "more" cut. Face the reader and open a gap, then bring the story in. A caption that opens "So-and-so did X" buries the hook in a character. See `references/hooks.md`.
- **Warmer and more conversational** than LinkedIn. Closer to how the creator talks to one person.
- **One thread.** A caption follows a single line of thought to the end.
- **CTA** is usually comment, save, or DM, and only if earned.

### Best-fit post-types

Story (very strong), mistake, warning, contrarian as a warm take. Anything that breathes as one continuous thought rather than a list.

### What to avoid

- Dense business-essay formatting with hard bullets.
- A wall of disconnected one-liners.
- Reflex hashtags and "thoughts?" sign-offs.

### Good example (caption)

> My most consistent client trains 25 minutes, twice a week.
>
> He used to train five days a week. He also quit every single quarter, usually the week a big launch hit.
>
> The fix was not more discipline. It was a smaller program. Three lifts, two short sessions, something he could keep when his calendar fell apart.
>
> A year later he has not missed a block. He is stronger than he was on five days a week, because five days a week only existed on the calendar, never in real life.
>
> Short and kept beats long and skipped. If your training keeps falling apart, make it smaller before you make it harder.

Why it works: opens mid-story, stays on one thread, warm without being soft, a specific turn (five days that only existed on the calendar), and a takeaway that doubles as the point. No em-dashes, no filler.
