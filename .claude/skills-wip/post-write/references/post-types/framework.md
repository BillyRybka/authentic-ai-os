# Post-type: Framework

## When to pick it

The idea is a repeatable model or mental structure with named parts. The reader keeps hitting the same problem and solving it from scratch every time. The job is to hand them a reusable shape they can run on autopilot, so the decision is made before the hard moment arrives.

## Structure

1. Name the problem the framework solves. Concrete, not abstract.
2. Introduce the framework. Give it a simple name if a name makes it stick.
3. Define each part. Keep it to 3 to 5, each one a clear unit.
4. Show how the parts work together. The shape, not just the list.
5. Say when to use it. The exact situation it is built for.
6. Close with how to apply it this week.

## Good example

> Most founders pick their workout by mood. Tired equals cardio, guilty equals more cardio. So effort drifts to whatever feels productive, not what builds anything.
>
> Use the 3-Lever rule. Every session pulls one of three levers, and you pick the lever before you walk in.
>
> Push: a hard compound lift. Carry: loaded walking or a sled. Recover: easy zone-2, ten minutes, no ego.
>
> One lever per day. Over a week you hit each twice, and the recover day protects the push days instead of competing with them.
>
> Use it when your schedule is chaos and you only get 30 minutes. The lever is chosen, so the session never stalls on "what should I do today."
>
> Tomorrow, label your next three workouts Push, Carry, Recover. Run that order for two weeks before you change anything.

Why it works: it names the real problem (picking by mood), gives the framework a sticky name, defines three clean parts, shows how recover protects push instead of fighting it, names the exact situation, and closes with a move for this week. Specific, committed, no filler.

## Bad example (slop)

> Having a good system for your training is one of the most important things you can do.
>
> A solid framework helps you stay organized and make the most of your time, which is so valuable in today's world.
>
> The idea is to balance different types of exercise in a way that works for you and your unique goals.
>
> When you have a structure to follow, everything just becomes more seamless and you can really focus on results.
>
> Find a framework that fits your lifestyle and watch your progress take off.

Why it fails: the framework has no named parts and no shape, so there is nothing to actually run. It hedges ("in a way that works for you"), reaches for filler ("in today's world," "seamless"), and never says what the three levers are or when to use them. It could describe any system for anything. It uses no em-dashes and is still pure slop. Slop is a substance failure, not a punctuation one.
