# Post-type: Story

## When to pick it

The idea is built on something that actually happened, and the lived moment is the proof. You are not arguing a point, you are showing one. Pick this when the scene carries more weight than any explanation could, and the reader learns by watching it play out.

## Structure

1. Open in the scene or the moment, not the lesson. Drop the reader inside it.
2. The tension or the mistake. What was at stake or what went wrong.
3. What happened. The turn, the choice, the result.
4. Pull the lesson out. Name what the moment actually taught.
5. Connect it to the reader's situation. Make it theirs, not just yours.
6. The practical takeaway. One thing they can do with it.

## Good example

> A founder texted me at 9:40pm from the airport. Flight delayed, hotel gym closed, and he was about to break a 60-day streak.
>
> He asked what to skip. I told him to skip nothing and do the workout in his room.
>
> 20 air squats, 20 pushups, a 60-second plank. Four rounds. Done in 11 minutes next to his suitcase.
>
> The lesson was not discipline. It was that his whole streak almost died because he thought training required a building.
>
> Your routine breaks the night it depends on perfect conditions to exist.
>
> Build one workout you can do in a hotel room with no gear. That is the version that survives your travel weeks.

Why it works: it opens in a real moment with a time and a place, raises a concrete stake (the streak), shows exactly what happened, pulls a sharp lesson, then hands the reader one specific move. Committed and specific, no filler.

## Bad example (slop)

> I once had a client who was traveling and almost missed a workout.
>
> It happens to all of us, and that is okay, because life gets busy and things come up.
>
> But he found a way to stay consistent, which just goes to show what is possible.
>
> The takeaway here is that where there is a will, there is a way.
>
> So next time you feel like skipping, just remember that you can always find a way to make it work.

Why it fails: the scene is vague, the stake is fuzzy, and the lesson collapses into a greeting-card platitude. No time, no place, no real moment, no concrete takeaway. It uses no em-dashes and is still slop, because the story never actually happens on the page.
