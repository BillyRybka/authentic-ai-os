# Post-type: Do this, not that

## When to pick it

The idea is a fast tactical contrast: a weak move the audience defaults to versus the strong move that beats it. The reader is already doing the work. They are just spending it in the wrong place. The job is to put the two side by side so the better choice is obvious, then name the principle that makes it true.

## Structure

1. Introduce the contrast. One line that frames what you are about to compare.
2. Five to seven paired lines. Each pair is "Do this: X" then "Not that: Y."
3. Name the bigger principle behind the pairs. Why the strong column wins every time.
4. Close short. A line that lands the takeaway or hands them the next move.

## Good example

> Thirty minutes before work. Here is where to spend it.
>
> Do this: pick three lifts and add weight each week.
> Not that: run on the treadmill until you are tired.
>
> Do this: track one number, the load on the bar.
> Not that: track calories burned on the watch.
>
> Do this: rest 90 seconds and lift heavy.
> Not that: rush through to keep your heart rate up.
>
> Do this: train the same three lifts for six weeks.
> Not that: swap the workout every time you get bored.
>
> The principle: a 200-pound founder who can squat his bodyweight looks and moves nothing like one who jogged for an hour. Strength compounds. Sweat evaporates.
>
> Pick the squat, the press, the row. Add five pounds Monday.

Why it works: the contrast is concrete (three lifts versus the treadmill), every pair names a real default the busy founder reaches for, the principle explains why the strong column wins (strength compounds, the cardio loop does not), and the close hands one move for Monday. Specific, committed, no filler.

## Bad example (slop)

> When it comes to fitness, there are a lot of dos and don'ts to keep in mind.
>
> Do focus on your goals, and try not to lose sight of what matters to you.
>
> Do listen to your body, and avoid pushing yourself too hard or too fast.
>
> Do find what works for you, instead of comparing yourself to others.
>
> At the end of the day, the most important thing is to stay positive and trust the process.

Why it fails: the pairs are not real contrasts, just one vague platitude restated as its opposite ("focus on your goals" versus "lose sight of what matters"). There is no specific move, no number, no default the reader actually makes. It could be about dieting, dating, or running a startup. It hedges and stays positive and says nothing. Note that it uses no em-dashes and is still pure slop. Slop is a substance failure, not a punctuation one.
