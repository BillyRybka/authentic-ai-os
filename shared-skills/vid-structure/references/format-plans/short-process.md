# Short Process

Plan a bounded route to one result.

Every section still carries the shared core: **Job**, **Sources**, and **Takeaway**. The fields below are conditional additions, not blank slots to fill.

## The shape

One parable up front, then sequential steps.

This is the distinction that defines the format. It is **not** parable-principle-parable-principle like a Listicle. There is one emotional beat at the start, and everything after it is the process.

A second parable is allowed in exactly one situation: a step that is complex enough to lose the viewer, or a claim they will not believe. Insert it before that step only. Most videos need zero.

## Decide

- the finish state the viewer can reach in one sitting
- the ordered actions and any true dependencies between them
- one shared story, contrast, or demonstration only when the process needs belief or orientation
- an extra example or evidence item only on a step that is hard to understand or trust

## The one parable

Pick a single type: Visual Demo, Contrast (old way against new way), Story, or Metaphor.

The choice follows the material. A process whose problem is invisible wants the demo. A process replacing a known habit wants the contrast.

## The steps

Cap at eight. Fewer is usually right.

If ten steps exist and five deliver most of the result, plan the five and log the rest as cuts. Step count follows what the material actually supports, never a target.

Name a dependency only when one step genuinely cannot run before another. Invented sequence makes a process feel heavier than it is.

Useful optional fields: **Action**, **Input**, **Output**, **Demonstration**, **Evidence**, **Dependency**.

## Do not

- give every step a fresh story
- keep setup that delays the first useful action
- alternate parable and principle like a listicle
- pad the step count
- plan a step the avatar already knows
- stack detail that makes a workable process feel hard

Positive: three clear actions, one opening contrast, and a screenshot attached to the one result claim.

Negative: eight steps padded from five, each carrying a separate metaphor and client story.
