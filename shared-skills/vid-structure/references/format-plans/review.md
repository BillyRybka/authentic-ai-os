# Review

One to three subjects, each shown, diagnosed, and fixed. The problem is the parable, the fix is the principle, and the fix is where the video converts.

## The body

```
## Review 1: {subject}
## Review 2: {subject}
## Review 3: {subject}
```

- **Three subjects at most, three problems each at most.** The planner's caps. Fewer is fine.
- Pick the universal problems from the submissions, not the obscure ones. A viewer who did not submit has to see themselves in the seat.
- Decide the strategy once: themed, where every subject has the same problem and the fixes compare, or variety, where each subject teaches a different one. Say which in the spine.
- Small audience with no submissions: review public work from famous brands or creators. Same shape.

## What each section locks

Every field is a label line with its beats under it. Asset is usually one line. Problems, Fix, and Result carry one beat per problem.

Every review:

- **Asset:** the exact thing on screen and where it came from. Submission with permission, or public. No asset is a hole that breaks the format.
- **Problems:** up to three, one beat each, specific and visible. This is the parable: the viewer sees what is wrong before they hear it.
- **Fix:** one beat per problem, as an instruction the viewer can apply to their own work. This is the principle.
- **Result:** what the fix produces: a mockup, a rewritten version, a comparable winner. Label a predicted effect as predicted. A fix that has not run is not a result that happened.

## Where the payoff lands

The pattern that emerges across subjects, at the end of the last review, or the most egregious subject's reveal when the title promises entertainment. Order subjects so the pattern builds: the shared mistake sharpens with each one.

## Do not

- show a problem without its fix; that is just being mean
- plan a 10 out of 10 takedown; 7 out of 10, frank and kind
- exceed three subjects or three problems each
- assert a result the fix has not produced

## Example

Title: "I fixed 3 subscriber thumbnails. Same mistake on all of them"

```markdown
## Review 1: the morning routine channel
**Asset:** submission 2, thumbnail PNG, permission checked on the form
**Problems:**
- text names the topic, not the angle: "Daily Routine Hacks"
- a posed stock smile, no reaction
- washed-out colours that vanish in the feed
**Fix:**
- text names the angle: "5am without caffeine"
- a reacting face, hand on chin, slight grimace
- saturation up by about a third
**Result:**
- mockup to build from the creator's own redesign, dump 22:10
- predicted: readable at feed size where the original is not

## Review 2: the agency owner's landing page
**Asset:** submission 5, page URL, public
**Problems:**
- headline says what they do, not what changes for the client
- three CTAs above the fold, three colours
**Fix:**
- headline becomes the client's result, in the client's words from their own testimonial
- one CTA, one colour, repeated once at the bottom
**Result:**
- rewritten headline in the dump, 27:40
- predicted, no data yet
```
