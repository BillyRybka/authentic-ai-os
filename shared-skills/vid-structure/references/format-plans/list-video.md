# List Video

Parable then principle at every point, N times. Each point stands on its own. The transitions between them are what keep the viewer, and those are the writer's.

## The body

```
## 1: {point}
## 2: {point}
...
## N: {point}
```

- **N is what the material supports.** The title's count is a promise the dump has to keep. If the locked title says seven and the dump honestly carries five, that is a title or framing problem; return it rather than padding.
- One lesson per point. A twenty-step process inside one point is a step-by-step hiding in a list; cut it to the one move or move it to its own video.
- Apply the planner's refine test to every point: would the average viewer in this avatar action it? No means cut.

## What each section locks

Every field is a label line with its beats under it. As many beats as the point needs for a writer to draft without asking.

Every point:

- **Parable:** the type and the material on the label. The beats: the story in order, the two sides of the contrast, the parts of the breakdown, what the demo shows. Vary the type across points. Five stories in a row flattens the video; the planner's matrix picks per point, and the matrix has five answers.
- **Principle:** the lesson as the viewer repeats it, why it is true, the move. `Proof:` rides the beat that makes the claim, where the claim needs it.

## Where the payoff lands

The final item, or the item that delivers the title's core promise, and it comes late. Order the rest by what the avatar already believes: recognition first and fast, the point that inverts their current practice last. The positional thread ("which one is the one?") stays open until then.

## Do not

- reuse one parable type at every point
- pad the count
- bury a process inside a point
- keep a point the avatar would not act on
- pay off the title at point one

## Example

Title: "5 onboarding mistakes that keep you as the bottleneck"

```markdown
## 1: No written steps, it all lives in your head
**Parable:** contrast, dump "the Tuesday scramble"
- new client signs and everyone waits for you
- you are the checklist, from memory, at 11pm
- versus the week the doc ran it and you found out at lunch
**Principle:**
- if it lives in your head it is not a process, it is a memory
- write it like you are teaching a smart twelve year old
- the moment it is on paper, the paper can start doing the work

## 2: Client onboarding and team onboarding treated as the same thing
**Parable:** breakdown, dump "one checklist for everyone"
- the client gets the internal task list
- the team gets the client welcome email
- both sides confused, both sides back to you
**Principle:**
- the client bought a result and needs to know what happens next and what you need from them
- the team runs the steps and needs the checklist and the owner
- two documents: a client-facing welcome, an internal runbook

## 5: No owner assigned to the process
**Parable:** story, [[story-bank/agency-owner-fired-himself]]
- Marcus had the process written, every step
- nobody owned it, so every step still routed through him
- the fix was one name next to each step
**Principle:**
- a process with no owner defaults back to you
- assign the owner before you assign the work. Proof: [[proof-bank/onboarding-5h-to-1h]]
```

Three types across three points, the story saved for the point that pays off the title.
