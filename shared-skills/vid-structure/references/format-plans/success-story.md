# Success Story

One transformation, told as a story, then one lesson. The parable is the story. The principle is what the viewer does with it.

## The body

```
## The story: {the transformation, in one line}
## The lesson: {the one thing}
```

- **One transformation.** Not first client and scaled and hired. The single biggest thing. Everything that did not cause the outcome is a cut.
- The story section may split into `## The problem`, `## What we did`, `## The outcome` when the arc runs long and the creator wants to write it in pieces. Default is one section, because the viewer follows one story, not three.
- The story must answer the planner's five questions or the video has a hole. Missing any one of them is a hole that breaks the format; name it before saving.

## What each section locks

Every field is a label line with its beats under it. As many beats as the story needs for a writer to draft without asking. Outcome and Proof are usually one line each.

The story:

- **Problem:** where they started, specific. The number, the date, the thing that was not working. Not "stuck."
- **Stakes:** why it had to change. The pressure, in their words from the dump.
- **Actions:** only the ones that caused the outcome, in order, including the one that failed first. Bank link or dump anchor on the beat it comes from.
- **Outcome:** the number and the timeframe.
- **Proof:** the screenshot, metric, clip, or before and after. `to build` is allowed only when the creator has said what the asset is and where it comes from.

The lesson:

- **Principle:** the one big lesson stated plainly, and why the story proves it. "What this proves is..."
- **Steps:** one to three things the viewer can do, each a verb. Proof on a step where the planner's own rule wants it shown.

## Where the payoff lands

The outcome moment, after the obstacles. The outcome can be in the title; then the question that carries the viewer is how, and whether they could do it too. The thread is the transformation itself, opened in the intro and closed at the outcome. The lesson and steps are application, not the payoff.

## Do not

- turn the story into five generic lessons
- include side missions
- skip the failure mid-arc; it is what makes the story believable
- plan a stage mismatch: a $1M story for a $10k viewer
- plan long client footage; one confirming line at the moment of impact

## Example

Title: "How Marcus cut client onboarding from 5 hours to 1"

```markdown
## The story: Marcus fired himself from onboarding
**Problem:** dump 02:00
- small content agency, every client onboarded by him personally
- 5 hours per client, "nothing shipped unless he touched it"
- two people had been given it before and it came back both times
**Stakes:** dump 05:40
- doing onboarding at 11pm, losing the Saturday
- "if this doesn't change I'm raising the kid alone"
**Actions:** [[story-bank/agency-owner-fired-himself]]
- first attempt: handed it to the coordinator with nothing written. Back on his desk in a week
- six weeks writing every step down, in order, as if teaching someone who had never seen it
- an owner named next to every step
- a checklist the coordinator could run without asking him anything
- the handoff, on a Monday, with him out of the thread
**Outcome:** 5 hours to 1, first client after the handoff
**Proof:** [[proof-bank/onboarding-5h-to-1h]]

## The lesson: documentation, not trust
**Principle:**
- it was never a trust problem
- there was nothing to trust anyone with until it was written down
- the first two handoffs failed for the same reason, not because of the people
**Steps:**
- pick the one task only you can do this week
- write every step, including the ones that feel obvious
- name the owner before you hand it over
```
