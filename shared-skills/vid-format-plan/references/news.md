# News format card

Thirteen blanks, in the order the file lays them out and the order they get asked. Nothing repeats in this format.

`knowledge/format-planners/news.md` is the content source of truth. This card holds the wording of each ask and the bar a passing answer clears, and points back at the planner for everything else. Nothing from the planner gets restated here. Two copies of one rule drift the first time either gets edited.

**Load-bearing: blanks 8 and 13.** The take, because the planner gives it a section headed "POV is required" and says a news video without one is a press release, and press releases don't get watched. And the end screen, because the planner heads that section "the single most important post-publish move" and says always route to evergreen. Those two get up to two follow-ups when the answer comes back thin, then a gap callout. Every other blank gets one ask and takes whatever comes back.

**Speed is the format.** The planner's window for breaking news is 24 hours, after which the story has aged out. Keep this session short. Every blank that can take a fast answer should get one, and a creator racing the clock who wants to stop halfway has a file full of gap callouts they can film from.

**Section names in the plan file are the blank names below**, numbered as they are numbered here.

**One thing per ask.** Some blanks hold two things and carry an `Ask 2`. That is two short turns, not one long question. The count in the progress line does not move between them.

## 1. Goal

- **Section:** `## 1. Goal`
- **Planner:** step 1 of the 8-step planner
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Views or emails?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** one of the two named.
- **Notes:** usually answered at step 0. Sales is wrong for this format and the planner says so twice: viewers came for the story, not the offer. If they say sales, say that once and ask which of the two instead. If they hold, write sales down as theirs and carry it.

## 2. Title and thumbnail

- **Section:** `## 2. Title and thumbnail`
- **Planner:** step 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Working title?
- **Ask 2:** What's on the thumbnail?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** a specific title naming the actual event, plus a thumbnail showing the news element itself: the logo, the person, the product, the screenshot.
- **Notes:** the planner rejects vague packages outright. "Big Changes In Tech This Week" is its example of what not to run. It also says abstract thumbnails fail here because viewers can't tell what the news IS at a glance. Say that once if the title is a category rather than an event.

## 3. Viewer's top 3 questions

- **Section:** `## 3. Viewer's top 3 questions`
- **Planner:** step 3
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What three questions does that package put in their head?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** three questions, phrased as questions.
- **Notes:** the planner says these usually come out as what happened, why does it matter to me, what do I do about it, which is the video's structure. If they land there, the next three blanks are already half answered and the session moves fast.

## 4. Is this actually news

- **Section:** `## 4. Is this actually news`
- **Planner:** the "What makes a news story worth covering" section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Why is this a big deal right now?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** something that changed, recently, that the avatar has not already seen everywhere.
- **Notes:** the planner's gut check is "holy crap, this is a big deal", and its counter-test is that if the reaction is "is there a news story here", there isn't one. It also warns the first headline you read is rarely the right one: the story may already be three days old in the niche. And the planner counts new-to-the-viewer as news: a feature nobody noticed, research nobody read. The energy is you need to know this now, not this happened in the world today. If the answer is weak, say so once, plainly, and let them decide whether to make it. Do not refuse to plan the video and do not keep raising it.

## 5. Intro: how fast in

- **Section:** `## 5. Intro: how fast in`
- **Planner:** the Intro adaptation table
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Straight into the story, or does anything need setting up first?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** straight in, which is the planner's default, or the one line they want first.
- **Notes:** news runs the most compressed intro of any format, 5 to 10 seconds, with setup and credibility usually skipped outright. The planner is clear these are defaults and not gates. Propose, do not enforce.

## 6. What happened

- **Section:** `## 6. What happened`
- **Planner:** step 4, and the structure section beat 1
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What happened?
- **Ask 2:** Who, and when exactly?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the facts, the players, the date. Enough that the viewer knows the story without looking it up.

## 7. Why it matters

- **Section:** `## 7. Why it matters`
- **Planner:** step 4, and the structure section beat 2
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What does it change for your viewer?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the stakes stated in the avatar's terms, not in the industry's.
- **Notes:** the planner maps this beat to the parable slot. It is where the viewer decides whether to keep watching.

## 8. Your take

- **Section:** `## 8. Your take`
- **Planner:** the "POV is required" section
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** What's your read on it?
- **Push 1:** What's everyone else saying, and where are they wrong?
- **Push 2:** What do you know about this that a viewer wouldn't get from the headline?
- **Passing:** an angle that is theirs, plus the line back to the viewer's world. The planner wants both: not just what happened, but what nobody else is saying, and why it matters to you specifically.
- **Notes:** the planner: a news video without POV is a press release, and press releases don't get watched. This is the blank the format lives on, and it is the one a creator racing the clock skips. Hold both pushes.

## 9. What viewers should do

- **Section:** `## 9. What viewers should do`
- **Planner:** step 4, and the structure section beat 3
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** What should they do about it?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** an action, or a watching position ("nothing yet, here's what to watch for"). The planner accepts both.

## 10. The cut

- **Section:** `## 10. The cut`
- **Planner:** step 5
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Anything in there that isn't answering the three questions?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** whatever they strike, or a clear nothing.
- **Notes:** the planner's rule for this blank is short: speed beats completeness. Cut everything that isn't paying off the three viewer questions.

## 11. Conversion

- **Section:** `## 11. Conversion`
- **Planner:** step 6, and the matching subsection of Conversion strategy by goal
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** set by the goal in blank 1.
  - **Views:** No links on this one. Anything at the end instead?
  - **Emails:** What's the lead magnet?
  - **Emails 2:** Opening and closing for the two mentions?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** the form of the ask plus the description decision. For views, "no links" is a complete answer.
- **Notes:** the planner gives news two mentions rather than the usual three, and wants the magnet tied to the story itself: the checklist for navigating the thing that just happened.

## 12. Delivery

- **Section:** `## 12. Delivery`
- **Planner:** step 7, and the Speed rules section
- **Load-bearing:** no
- **Ask:** Scripted, or presenting off bullets to get it out today?
- **Push:** none.
- **Passing:** either one. Both are correct here.
- **Notes:** this is the one format where the planner explicitly permits bullets, because writing can eat the whole window. It also says no b-roll fishing trips: use the screenshots and the source footage you already have rather than delaying the post.

## 13. Upload window and the end screen

- **Section:** `## 13. Upload window and the end screen`
- **Planner:** step 8, and the "End screen strategy" section
- **Load-bearing:** yes
- **Ask:** When does this go up?
- **Ask 2:** Which evergreen video does the end screen point at?
- **Push 1:** Not another news video. Which one shows what you actually do?
- **Push 2:** Which of your videos would make a new subscriber stay?
- **Passing:** a publish time inside the window, plus a real published non-news video.
- **Notes:** the planner calls the end screen the single most important post-publish move for this format, because the whole point is converting an algorithm spike into channel audience. Routing news to more news makes a news channel, where the audience comes for the news and the evergreen library never grows. The pushes exist to move them off another news video, not to make them think harder about a good answer.
