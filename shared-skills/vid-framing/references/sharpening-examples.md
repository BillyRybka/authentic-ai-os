# Sharpening examples

## No selected video

Ask only:

> What video do you want to frame?

Do not read or add explanation before the creator answers.

## Confirm intent without turning it into a form

The selected material suggests a video for independent dog groomers whose late cancellations leave gaps they cannot refill. Begin with the audience and current situation, not a list of fields:

> From what you've shared, I think this video is for independent dog groomers who keep losing income to same-day cancellations. Is that right?

After the creator confirms or corrects that read, ask one highest-value unresolved question:

> What should they be able to do differently after watching?

If the creator says they should leave able to set a cancellation policy clients accept, that settles the desired outcome. Ask goal separately because it must be explicit:

> Is this video mainly meant to drive sales, collect emails, or earn views?

Do not infer goal from a mentioned offer, link, or call to action. Before presenting the package, make sure the creator has settled the audience, current situation and stakes, desired viewer outcome, and goal. Do not announce those categories or present them as a four-part form.

## Paraphrase clearly without inventing a quote

The selected material says new managers delay difficult feedback, then spend Friday fixing work they should have corrected on Tuesday. A clear, unattributed paraphrase is:

> New managers who put off hard feedback and end up redoing the work themselves.

Do not put quotation marks around that sentence or claim managers said it. It is a faithful synthesis of the supplied material, not an exact quote.

## Use a concrete name when it earns its place

The creator calls their meal-planning method the Dinner Map. No audience quote is supplied. The name can still work because it is concrete and helps a parent picture the vehicle:

> **Frame:** I want to show parents who reach dinnertime with no idea what to make how to use the Dinner Map to turn what they already have in the kitchen into five decided meals

Lead with the parent's situation and result. Keep the named mechanism only because it makes the promise easier to recognize, not because the audience supposedly used the name.

## Propose a handle instead of hiding behind an abstraction

The material describes a short check photographers complete before sending a gallery, but the check has no name. Do not invent vague language such as "quality guard," "delivery layer," or "review framework."

Offer a concrete handle as a proposal:

> Would "pre-delivery checklist" feel right for this, or would you call it something else?

The proposal is not audience language until the creator confirms it. If the natural wording is still unclear, ask rather than forcing a name.

## Keep quotes intact

Two identified interview notes say:

- Interview A: "I lose the first hour deciding what matters."
- Interview B: "By lunch, I'm reacting to everyone else."

Either sentence may be quoted exactly with its source. A sentence such as "I lose the first hour reacting to everyone else" is a stitched line. It may inform an unattributed paraphrase, but it must never appear as a quote or as exact audience wording.

## Respond to feedback locally

Creator: "The Frame makes this sound like a checklist video. The real change is that they stop reopening decisions."

Respond with the Frame only:

> **Frame:** I want to show project leads who keep reopening settled decisions how to make those decisions clear enough that the team can actually move on.

Do not restate Core payoff, Must deliver, format, or goal. Do not reopen a two-video discussion or ask to save.

When the creator instead corrects Core payoff, revise only Core payoff:

> **Core payoff:** You can point back to what was agreed when an approved decision comes back into question, instead of reopening it from scratch.

## Keep one cohesive transformation together

Giving a shop owner an inventory-count sheet, showing the setup, and using it during the first count can serve one outcome: completing an accurate weekly count. Keep that sequence in one video.

Do not diagnose setup, tool, and use as separate videos. Ask one outcome question only when the material contains genuinely independent endings.

## Present one package after intent is settled

The creator has confirmed independent dog groomers, same-day cancellations that leave unfillable gaps, the ability to set a cancellation policy clients accept, and an email-growth goal. The material supports a bounded process, so choose `short-process` deliberately and present one package:

> **Frame:** I want to show dog groomers how to stop same-day cancellations from leaving unpaid gaps in their calendar by setting a cancellation policy clients will actually accept.
>
> **Core payoff:** You can set and introduce a cancellation policy that protects income when a last-minute opening can’t be filled.
>
> **Must deliver:** Draft the policy from a realistic booking scenario, show the client message that introduces it, and walk through one cancellation handled under the new policy.
>
> **Format:** short-process
>
> **Goal:** emails

Do not add `Must not become` when the creator has not stated a boundary. Do not present the package before the required intent is confirmed.

## Narrow an unsupported claim after intent

If the confirmed direction promises to cut cancellations in half but the material contains no supporting records, say naturally that the number is not supportable yet. Offer to remove the number while preserving the confirmed relief the policy can honestly earn. Ask one focused question only when the choice changes the promise.

## Construction checks

- Confirm or correct the audience and current situation before asking one highest-value unresolved question at a time.
- Settle audience, situation and stakes, desired outcome, and explicit goal before presenting the package.
- Use clear, familiar language without falsely attributing a paraphrase, proposed handle, or stitched sentence.
- Lead with the transformation and keep a named mechanism only when it makes the promise easier to recognize.
- Keep Core payoff direct second person and limited to one end capability.
- Make Must deliver concrete and filmable after intent is clear.
- Answer follow-up fields locally instead of regenerating the package.
- Preserve one creator-faithful direction without automatic forks, batch generation, or packaging copy.
