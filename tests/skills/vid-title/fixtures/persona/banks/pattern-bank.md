## Synthesis (cross-channel insights)

The "what we learned" layer. `vid-framing` reads this first when picking angles; the outlier rows below are the evidence.

> [!important] The split that matters most for you
> The strongest, most convergent patterns in this set fall into two off-lane buckets: **tool-news jacking** ("[Tool] Just Dropped, First Cut") and **big-shop spectacle** (4,000 sq ft tours, challenge builds). They print views, but they are not your lane and they feed the exact tool-collecting itch your iceberg argues against. Your lane (small-kit hand-tool woodworking where beginners actually finish) shows up in a smaller, higher-fit cluster: **ruined-boards-to-result arcs**, **no-shop builds**, and **contrarian beginner takes**. The curation pass is about keeping the on-lane gold and consciously deciding how much off-lane reach you are willing to borrow.

> [!important] Convergent patterns (proven across the most channels)
> Appear across multiple channels and across circles. Test these first.
>
> 1. **Mistakes-with-a-cost** `[N] [Skill] Mistakes That [Cost]` / `[Practice] WRONG (Do This Instead)`. Wren, Tom, Mabel. ON-LANE (e.g. "9 Hand-Tool Mistakes That Ruin Your First Build").
> 2. **Result arc with receipts** `From [failure] to [specific result] in [time]` / `[cheap] to [sold]`. Tom, Salvage, Northfield. ON-LANE.
> 3. **Speed-to-outcome compression** `The [N]-Minute [Finish]` / `[Outcome] in [N] Minutes`. Mabel, Fine Grain, Dovetail Daily. ON-LANE.
> 4. **New-tool news jacking** `[Tool] Just Dropped (...)` / `[Tool] Just Changed [task]`. Copper Creek, Sawdust Empire. OFF-LANE by default (tool-chasing, fast decay). The one survivable variant reads what a release means for a small shop, used sparingly.
> 5. **"Steal this" generosity** `[N] [Tools] I Can't Live Without (steal my kit)`. Tom, Northfield. ON-LANE.

> [!note] Niche-specific patterns (strong for YOUR audience)
> Appear in the direct set. Lower raw frequency, highest strategic fit.
>
> 1. **No-shop build** `I Built [specific outcome] (No Shop)` / `With No Table Saw`. Wren, Bea, Tom, Northfield. The avatar's exact dream stated as proof: real furniture without the dream shop.
> 2. **Ruined-boards honesty** `I Ruined [specific thing] So You Don't Have To` / counting the failures out loud. Tom, Mabel, Wren. Failure counted as tuition is the trust engine of this niche.
> 3. **Contrarian beginner takes** `Why Beginners Build the BEST [X] (Nobody Talks About This)`. Wren (x2), Mabel. Against the "buy the dream shop first" default. Differentiating, on-lane.
> 4. **Finishing as the multiplier** `The [N]-Minute Finish That [outcome]`. Mabel. Speaks to builders whose pieces look homemade at the last step.

> [!tip] Adjacent translations (differentiation gold, structure only)
> Shapes from adjacent channels not yet used in your exact lane. Take the shape, never the topic.
>
> 1. **Contrarian command** `DON'T [common practice], [do Y] instead`. Mabel, Salvage, Workshop Wars. Transfers cleanly as a shop take.
> 2. **Challenge framing** `[constraint] build in [time limit]`. Workshop Wars, Fine Grain. Aggressive; flag for tone fit.
> 3. **Definitive resource** `The Only [X] You'll Ever Need`. Copper Creek, Bea. The "end the search" packaging could anchor a cornerstone kit video.
> 4. **Free-material result arc** `$0 materials to [specific outcome]`. Salvage, Northfield. Topic (reclaimed stock) is adjacent; the zero-to-result structure transfers.

### Topic clusters (own + direct only, never adjacent)

What the direct audience clicks on, by subject. Adjacent topics deliberately excluded.

> [!abstract] Cluster 1: The small-kit, no-shop toolbox
> Wren's no-table-saw bedroom set, Tom's 11-tool kit and $65 workbench, Mabel's "DON'T Buy Another Tool."
> Why it pulls: the avatar's permission slip to start with what they have.

> [!abstract] Cluster 2: Ruined boards as tuition
> Tom's 9-boards-to-sale arc, Mabel's ruined walnut top, Wren's 9 mistakes.
> Why it pulls: beginners think ruin means stop; these videos reframe ruin as the price of skill.

> [!abstract] Cluster 3: Finishing and first sales
> Mabel's finishing and prices set, Tom's bookshelf that pays for lumber.
> Why it pulls: the last ten percent is where homemade turns into sellable, and the avatar knows it.

> [!abstract] Cluster 4: Sharp edges and hand-tool skill
> Wren's dovetails, chisel-a-day, and $40 hand plane; Mabel's sharpening correction.
> Why it pulls: sharp is the one upgrade money can't shortcut, and everyone suspects it.

> [!tip] Thumbnail convergence (12 top performers classified)
> The niche speaks one visual language. **Minimal dominates** (about 7 of 12): an oversized white sans-serif claim of 3 to 4 words, a dark workshop background, and one warm wood-toned accent, with the creator's face mid-work or a single hand tool in frame. The hooks that layer on top of Minimal are **Counted Proof** (the literal pile of ruined boards, a tally on the wall), **Curiosity** ("NOBODY TEACHES THIS", a covered workpiece), and **Result** (the finished piece staged like a product shot, a sold tag). The challenge channels (Workshop Wars) use a **versus** variant: two builders, one board, a timer graphic, which is the proven shape for a head-to-head piece.
>
> The takeaways for your packaging: lead with **Minimal** (huge legible claim, dark shop background, warm accent, 4 words or fewer), keep **Counted Proof** in your pocket for the ruined-boards stories (nobody else shows the failures), and save the versus look for a rare challenge collab.
