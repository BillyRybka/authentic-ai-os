# vid-ideas Tier B Rubric (AI judge)

DO NOT MODIFY during an autoresearch loop. This is the locked rubric. You are a
fresh judge with no memory of prior iterations. Score only what is in front of
you, against this rubric. Be consistent and a little stingy: a 5 is rare.

## What you receive per case

- `ideas-batch.md`, the surfaced batch (4 anchored ideas + 1-2 flagged swings)
- `ideas-backlog.md`, the backlog state after the run, when one exists
- `seed-packet.md`, the handoff to vid-intake for the picked idea
- `transcript.md`, the full ideas conversation
- the case config (focus, flags, pick, adversarial or not)
- the shared foundation: `creator-foundation.md` (iceberg, pillars, avatar,
  Top 3) and `reference-pieces/youtube-script.md` (the creator's real voice)
- the suite pattern-bank fixture (the receipts ground truth)

Only run Tier B on cases that already passed Tier A. If a case failed Tier A, do
not score it: the mechanical floor was not met. (case_02 in this suite is the
designed dull-bend failure and never reaches Tier B.)

## Read-aloud anchor (calibrate voice here first)

Before scoring, read two passages from `reference-pieces/youtube-script.md` out
loud in your head. That is the creator at a 5 for voice. A consultant-flavored
idea line like "Maximizing Operational Efficiency Through Documentation" is a 1.

## Dimensions (score each 1 to 5)

### 1. signal_honesty

**What it measures:** Is every anchored idea backed by a REAL outlier from the
bank, named with its real title, channel, views, and multiple? Is the engine
the idea claims to borrow actually the load-bearing element of that source?
Are swings honestly flagged as unproven, never dressed up as proven? On the
adversarial case, did the skill refuse to chase the off-lane giant's topics
even when the creator asked about them?

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Invented an outlier, a view count, or a multiple, or cited a row whose engine the line does not carry. | Citing a "340k views" row that exists nowhere in the bank. |
| 2 | No outright invention, but receipts are vague (no views or multiple), or the named engine is a hand-wave ("it is catchy"). | "inspired by a big Olivia Chen video" with no row named. |
| 3 | Receipts are real and complete, but the named engine is the abstract label, not the load-bearing phrasing of the actual title. | Engine named as "speed content" when the title's engine is "in 15 Minutes (So Anyone Can Run It)". |
| 4 | Receipts real and complete, engine correctly named from the actual title, swings cleanly flagged. On the adversarial case, the off-lane temptation is declined with a reason. | Engine: the "(No Employees)"-style kicker, named exactly as the source phrases it. |
| 5 | All of 4, and the batch shows judgment in WHICH rows it anchored to: own-channel and convergent shapes lead, a one-channel row leads only when its multiple clears that channel's outlier floor, low-multiple rows do not, and the "why it could land" line names a real pull for THIS avatar. | The rationale ties the engine to the avatar's named want, not to the view count. |

### 2. sharpness

**What it measures:** Did each bend keep the engine's sharp FORM, or did it
dull into a synonym? The number, the hot proper noun, the parenthetical
kicker, the named system: these are the load-bearing forms. Run the
side-by-side test yourself: put the idea line next to the source title. If the
source gets the click, the bend failed.

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | The sharp form is gone across the batch: numbers become "most", kickers become clauses, named systems become "a system". | "How to Run Your Agency Without Hiring Anyone" for the "Zero Employees (Full System)" source. |
| 2 | Most lines keep a form but the strongest engine in the batch was dulled. | The 14.2x anchor's kicker dropped while weaker anchors kept theirs. |
| 3 | Forms mostly survive, but one line is visibly duller than its source, or a borrowed number is swapped for a vague quantity. | "in a few minutes" standing in for "in 15 Minutes". |
| 4 | Every anchored line keeps its engine's sharp form or a bracketed placeholder, and side-by-side the bend competes with the source for the click. | "Document Any Client Process in 15 Minutes (So Someone Else Can Run It)". |
| 5 | All of 4, and at least one bend is genuinely sharper for this avatar than the source was for its own: the engine lands on the avatar's exact want. | The superiority stat bent onto the avatar's specific feared mistake, number intact. |

### 3. click_pull

**What it measures:** Would the avatar actually click these? Each idea is a
concrete video, not a category; the pull is a dream outcome or a sharp
tension, not a defensive reassurance; the "why it could land" line says why a
human clicks THIS one.

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Category topics, not videos. No pull named. | "A video about pricing." |
| 2 | Concrete topics, but the pull is reassurance or on-brand flavor, not a want. | "...and it still sounds like you" bolted onto every line. |
| 3 | Real pull on most ideas, but one or two "why it could land" lines are interchangeable across ideas. | "People want to save time" under three different ideas. |
| 4 | Every idea names a distinct, concrete pull tied to the avatar's stated pains. | "The owner who cannot take a week off clicks this because the doc runs without them." |
| 5 | All of 4, and the batch as a whole reads like a menu the creator genuinely cannot choose from because several would work. | The creator in the transcript says some version of "I want to make three of these". |

### 4. fit_and_range

**What it measures:** Iceberg discipline and batch spread. Every idea inside
the iceberg (checked honestly, not stamped), 3-4 pillars covered, Top 3
problem tags only where one genuinely fits, no forced coverage.

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | An off-iceberg idea surfaced, or every idea sits on one pillar. | Six systems ideas; the pricing and delegation pillars untouched. |
| 2 | Fit verdicts stamped without reading ("inside" on everything with no phrase of why), or problem tags forced onto ideas that do not serve one. | A pricing-receipt idea tagged to problem 1 with a stretch rationale. |
| 3 | Spread is real and verdicts are honest, but the batch leans on one pillar or one problem noticeably. | Four of six ideas on systems + delegation, pricing only in a swing. |
| 4 | 3-4 pillars, verdicts named with a phrase of why, problem tags only where they fit, swings still iceberg-gated. | The swing is wild but plainly inside the iceberg. |
| 5 | All of 4, and the range shows strategy: the pillars chosen balance what the avatar needs with what the channel has not said lately, and the batch does not read as a rut. | The skill notes which pillar the backlog already covers and ranges wider. |

### 5. voice_read_aloud

**What it measures:** Do the idea lines read like something this creator would
say out loud? Plain, spoken, second-person where the creator uses it. No
consultant polish, no keyword stuffing, no AI flavor.

| Score | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Corporate or keyword-stuffed lines. | "Maximizing Agency Operational Efficiency (Complete Framework)". |
| 2 | Mostly plain but at least one line the creator would reword on camera. | "The Systematization of Client Onboarding in Quarter-Hour Increments". |
| 3 | Plain and clean, but cadence is generic; could be any business channel's lines. | "How to Delegate Better in Your Business". |
| 4 | Close to the creator's voice; blunt, spoken, minor word choices slightly off. | "Stop Being the Hero of Your Client's Project". |
| 5 | You would hear these exact lines in the creator's mouth. The why-lines sound like the creator talking to a peer, not a deck. | "You Do Not Have a Time Problem (You Have a Delegation Problem)". |

## Output format

Return JSON only:

```json
{
  "per_case": [
    {
      "case": 0,
      "slug": "blank-slate-keeps",
      "scores": {
        "signal_honesty": 4,
        "sharpness": 4,
        "click_pull": 4,
        "fit_and_range": 4,
        "voice_read_aloud": 4
      },
      "average": 4.0,
      "reasoning": "one or two sentences, concrete, cite the specific phrase or move that drove the score"
    }
  ],
  "dimension_averages": {
    "signal_honesty": 0.0,
    "sharpness": 0.0,
    "click_pull": 0.0,
    "fit_and_range": 0.0,
    "voice_read_aloud": 0.0
  },
  "quality_score": 0.0
}
```

`quality_score` is the mean of all dimension scores across all scored cases,
normalized to 0 to 1 (divide the 1 to 5 average by 5). This is the single
number the optimizer reads. Per-dimension averages tell the optimizer where to
spend the next iteration (a 2.5 on sharpness means the bends are dulling; a
2.5 on signal_honesty means fabrication is leaking).
