# He Fired Himself From His Own Onboarding

> Tier 1 outline. vid-intro fills ## Intro. vid-segment writes each body section.
> vid-ending fills ## Ending.

## Intro
Marcus ran a small content agency and he was the bottleneck on everything. Nothing shipped unless he touched it. Six weeks later his onboarding ran without him, and his own time on it dropped from five hours a client to one. This is the whole story, and the teaching point is the part most people get backwards: delegation is not about trust, it is about documentation.

## Setup
**Parable:** story. [[story-bank/agency-owner-fired-himself]]: the agency, the bottleneck, the five hours.
**Principle:** stakes first; the viewer has to feel the bottleneck before they want the fix.
When Marcus came to me, he ran a small content agency and he was the bottleneck on everything. Nothing shipped unless he touched it. Onboarding a new client ate five hours of his week, every time, because the whole process lived in his head. He was not the only one who could do the work. He was just the only one with the steps in his head. And the fix was not what he expected, because he walked in convinced he had a completely different problem.

## Problem
**Parable:** story (continued). The wrong diagnosis: he thought it was trust.
**Principle:** the handoff kept bouncing back because the knowledge never moved, only the task did.
Marcus thought he had a trust problem. He told me he could not hand onboarding off because nobody else cared enough to do it right. Sound familiar? Here is what was actually going on. Every time he tried to hand it off, he handed over the task but kept the knowledge. His coordinator got the job, Marcus kept the steps. Of course it bounced back to him. It had to. There was nowhere else for it to go. So we did not work on trust at all. We spent six weeks doing something much more boring, and that boring thing is the whole move.

## Action
**Parable:** story (continued). Six weeks pulling the process out of his head, every step written down, an owner assigned.
**Principle:** documentation before delegation. The checklist his coordinator could run without him.

## Outcome
**Parable:** story (continued). The handoff to the coordinator, the bottleneck gone.
**Principle:** proof drop after the result lands. Proof: [[proof-bank/onboarding-5h-to-1h]]
Six weeks after we finished the document, Marcus handed the whole thing to his coordinator. Not part of it. All of it. The checklist, the owner, the steps, gone from his desk. He stopped being the bottleneck, because the bottleneck was never him. It was the missing document.

Here is the receipt. His own onboarding time, the hours he personally spent per client, went from five hours down to one. Same clients, same service, same Marcus. The only thing that changed was the steps lived on paper with a name next to them.

Five hours to one. That is what documentation buys. But a receipt is not a plan. The next part is the three steps that produce it, starting with the one task only you can do.

## Lesson + Steps
**Parable:** none. The story already carried it.
**Principle:** delegation is not about trust, it is about documentation. Steps: pick the one task only you can do, write every step, hand it to a named owner.
