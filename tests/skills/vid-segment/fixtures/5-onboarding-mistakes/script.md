# 5 Onboarding Mistakes That Keep You as the Bottleneck

> Tier 1 outline. vid-intro fills ## Intro. vid-segment writes each body section.
> vid-ending fills ## Ending.

## Intro
Quick question. When a new client signs, does everyone involved know exactly what happens next, or does it all route back through your head? I have made every onboarding mistake on this list. Some of them for years. By the end you will have all five, in order, and you will know which one is quietly keeping you as the bottleneck. Starting with the one everything else sits on: mistake number one.

## Mistake 1: No written steps, it all lives in your head
**Parable:** contrast. The Tuesday scramble versus the written checklist.
**Principle:** if it lives in your head, it is not a process, it is a memory.
Your onboarding is not a process. It is a memory. Every step lives in your head, so every step needs you there to run it. New client signs, and you are the checklist. You tell yourself you will write it down when things calm down, and things never calm down, because nothing calms down while the process is a person. Write the steps down. All of them, like you are teaching a smart twelve year old. The moment the steps live on paper, the paper can start doing the work. Which sets up the second mistake, because even people who write it down usually write the wrong document.

## Mistake 2: Client onboarding and team onboarding treated as the same thing
**Parable:** breakdown. Two different jobs sharing one checklist.
**Principle:** the client buys the result, the team runs the steps; one document cannot serve both.
Here is what the wrong document looks like. You write one onboarding checklist and hand it to everyone. The client gets the internal task list. The team gets the client welcome email. Both sides end up confused, because onboarding the client and onboarding your team are two different jobs. The client bought a result and needs to know what happens next and what you need from them. Your team runs the steps and needs the checklist and the owner. One document cannot do both jobs. Split them: a client-facing welcome that sets expectations, and an internal runbook that runs the work. Now, mistake 3 is the one that quietly puts it all back on your plate, even when the right documents exist.

## Mistake 3: No owner assigned to the process
**Parable:** story. [[story-bank/agency-owner-fired-himself]]: Marcus had a process but no owner, so it defaulted back to him.
**Principle:** a process with no owner defaults back to you. Assign the owner.

## Mistake 4: You document it once and never update it
**Parable:** to build. Needs a quick real example of a stale document causing a miss.
**Principle:** the document is a living thing; reality changes, the doc follows.

## Mistake 5: You measure nothing so you cannot tell if it is working
**Parable:** to build. Needs a quick real example of an unmeasured process drifting.
**Principle:** track one number so you know it is working.
