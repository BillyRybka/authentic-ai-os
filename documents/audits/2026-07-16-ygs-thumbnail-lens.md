# The YGS thumbnail process, distilled (internal lens doc)

Source: Ed Lawrence, YGS phase 3 (packaging), lessons 2 and 4-7. Internal doc only. Nothing here ships; skill and knowledge files stay attribution-scrubbed. This is the lens for auditing `vid-thumbnail` and `knowledge/thumbnail-text-patterns.md`.

## The packaging concept (lesson 2)

- A video is a gift. Wrapping paper = thumbnail + title. Box = format. Gift = information. Viewers open or pass on the wrapping alone.
- Packaging is not pretty vs ugly. It is "what does MY ideal buyer want to click," which scares off the wrong people and pulls the right ones.
- The engine is a test loop: MVP options, post, measure, adjust. When a wrapping style works, repeat it almost identically until it stops working. Winning packaging gets reused (same layout, same title structure, same format).
- The video must then match or exceed the expectation the packaging set. Good channels match. Great channels exceed.

## What makes a powerful thumbnail (lesson 4): the triangle

Three parts, each with a one-line test:

1. **Context.** The topic is clear in a split second, before the title is read. Delivered via icons, familiar scenes, text, or items. Test: **"Would my buyer instantly understand the topic of this video just by looking at the image?"**
2. **Curiosity.** The thumbnail makes the viewer think how, what, or why. Test: **"Does this make my buyers think what, why, or how?"**
3. **Clarity.** One HERO element sticks out first (the text, an item, or a result), so context and curiosity can actually land. Test: **"Does the most important element to my viewers stick out?"**

## The six strategies (lesson 5)

Channel-level styles to test, find the winner, then repeat:

1. **Cognitive dissonance** (go-to; challenge what the viewer believes is true; try it on every video)
2. **Comparison / before-after** (needs a visually dramatic result)
3. **The result** (show or tease the outcome the viewer wants; needs a convincing result)
4. **Social hacking** (borrow a famous face or brand; rocket fuel but attracts the wrong audience if overused)
5. **Curiosity** (pure what/why/how without the belief clash; clickbait risk, must deliver fast)
6. **The minimal** (aesthetic, authentic; needs real photography skill; for vulnerable moments)

Which one? None permanently. Test through the MVP loop; repeat the winner.

## Text-relevant design rules (lesson 6)

- Thumbnail text is NOT another title. 4 words max, maybe sneak 5.
- The hero (usually the text) must be the highest-contrast, first-read element.
- Hierarchy: what sticks out first is what the viewer reads first.
- Emotion must match the packaging tone (positive title = positive emotion, or stay pan).
- Kill list: visual metaphors (monkey brain must never have to decode), inside jokes (only make sense after watching), overly creative, obvious stock, exact copies of another channel, beyond-your-talent art, obviously-AI if the audience hates it.
- Match after the click: thumbnail quality and video quality must agree.

## Creation process (lesson 7)

Decision matrix for making the IMAGE: learn Photoshop, AI generation, batch-shoot photos of yourself and reuse them, or outsource. Starts from a crappy doodle sent to a designer. This is a separate future skill (rendered thumbnail), not vid-thumbnail's job. vid-thumbnail ends at locked text.

## Verdict against our system (2026-07-16)

**Aligned.** The 5 text patterns in `thumbnail-text-patterns.md` are the text-expressible slice of the 6 strategies (comparison / social hacking / minimal are mostly visual). Word cap, anti-patterns (visual metaphors, inside jokes), title pairing, no-title-repeat, test-then-repeat via packaging-system: all present and consistent with the source.

**Gap 1: the triangle is not the skill's judging spine.** Context, curiosity, clarity exist implicitly, but the three one-line tests are not run on candidates. The 21-lessons run produced texts that fail the context test ("LIP-SYNC", "THE PERSON IS GONE": buyer cannot tell what the video is about from title + text). Fix: Step 3 filters every survivor through the three tests, adapted for text (context = topic clear from title + text together, or explicitly flag that the image must carry the context; curiosity = what/why/how; clarity = one idea, instantly readable).

**Gap 2: the buyer is missing from the judgment.** Every one of Ed's tests says "my buyer," not "a viewer." The skill judges against generic click psychology. Fix: one line, judge through the ideal buyer's eyes.

**Not gaps:** strategy selection lives at channel level (packaging-system + measurement loop), correct; rendered-image process is a future separate skill, correct; 1-2 locked picks = MVP options for the test loop, correct.
